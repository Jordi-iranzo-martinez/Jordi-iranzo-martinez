
Squamata (From Latin *squama*: "scale") es el grupo más grande y diverso de reptiles vivos en el planeta, abarcando más del 95% de todas las especies de reptiles actuales. En inglés, se les conoce colectivamente bajo el nombre común de scaled reptiles o de forma directa como lizards, snakes, and amphisbaenians.*

### Squamata taxonomy

- **Sauria** (Lizards): The most anatomically diverse squamate group, typically featuring four well-developed legs, visible external ear openings, and movable eyelids (e.g., geckos, iguanas, chameleons, and monitor lizards).

- **Serpentes** (snakes): highly specialized, elongated carnivores completely lacking limbs, external ears, and movable eyelids, renowned for highly kinetic skulls that allow them to swallow prey much larger than their heads.

- **Amphisbaenia** (Worm lizards): a unique group of mostly limbless, burrowing squamates with cylindrical bodies, fused eyelids, and ring-like body scales that closely resemble earthworms.
