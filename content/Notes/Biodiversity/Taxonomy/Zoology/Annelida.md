
Annelida (from latin *anellus* "little ring") comprise over 22,000 living species of earthworms, leeches, and marine bristle worms.

They are found worldwide in marine, freshwater, and damp terrestrial ecosystems. 

### Annelida Characteritic


a body divided into repeating ring-like segments.

- **Metameric Segmentation:** Their bodies are divided into a series of repeating external and internal segments called **metameres**. This modular design allows for highly specialized movement, as muscles in individual segments can contract independently.

- **True Coelom:** They possess a fluid-filled body cavity (**coelom**) completely lined with mesoderm tissue. This fluid acts as a hydrostatic skeleton against which muscles can push, giving them structural support.

- **Closed Circulatory System:** Annelids were among the first animals to evolve a closed circulatory network, meaning their blood flows entirely within enclosed blood vessels rather than sloshing freely inside body cavities. They use multiple pairs of muscular blood vessels functioning as "lateral hearts" to pump blood.

- **Setae (or Chaetae):** Most annelids have microscopic, hair-like bristles made of chitin embedded in their skin. These bristles act like tiny cleats, anchoring the worm into soil or mud as it crawls.


### Anellida taxonomy

- **Polychaeta** (bristle Worms): Primarily marine annelids featuring fleshy, paddle-like appendages called parapodia on each segment, which bear numerous sharp chaetae. _Examples: Christmas tree worms, bobbit worms, and lugworms._

- **Clitellata** (earthworms & leeches): Annelids defined by the possession of a clitellum (a specialized reproductive collar). It contains two massive subclasses: _Oligochaeta_ (terrestrial and freshwater earthworms) and _Hirudinea_ (ectoparasitic or predatory leeches).

- **Echiura & Sipuncula** (unsegmented): Highly modified marine lineages that completely lost their external segmentation over evolutionary time, formerly treated as independent phyla but now firmly nested within the annelid family tree. _Examples: Spoon worms and peanut worms._
