### Insects taxonomy

- **Apterygota**: without wings
	- **Archaeognatha**: Jumping bristletails
	- **Zygentoma**: Silverfish and firebrats

- **Pterygota**: with wings
	- Paleoptera (Rigid-winged)
		- **Ephemeroptera**: Mayflies.  
		- **Odonata**: Dragonflies and damselflies.
	- Neoptera (Folding-winged)
		- **Polyneoptera** (Incomplete Metamorphosis - Hemimetabolous): _Nymphs look like miniature versions of adults but lack functional wings._  
			- **Plecoptera**: Stoneflies.  
			- **Dermaptera**: Earwigs.  
			- **Zoraptera**: Angel insects.  
			- **Orthoptera**: Grasshoppers, crickets, and locusts.  
			- **Embioptera**: Webspinners (spin silk using glands on their front legs).  
			- **Phasmatodea**: Stick insects and leaf insects (masters of camouflage).  
			- **Grylloblattaria**: Ice crawlers (extremophiles living in ice and caves). 
			- **Mantophasmatodea**: Gladiators or heelwalkers (discovered in the early 2000s).  
			- **Mantodea**: Mantises / Praying mantises.  
			- **Blattodea**: Cockroaches and termites (_Isoptera_ is now nested here).
		- Paraneoptera (Sucking Insects)
			- **Psocodea**: Lice, barklice, and booklice (includes parasitic biting and sucking lice).
			- **Thysanoptera**: Thrips.
			- **Hemiptera**: True bugs, cicadas, aphids, and planthoppers (characterized by piercing-sucking mouthparts).
		- Endopterygota / Holometabola (Complete Metamorphosis)
			- **Hymenoptera**: Ants, bees, wasps, and sawflies.  
			- **Coleoptera**: Beetles and weevils (the largest order in the entire animal kingdom).  
			- **Strepsiptera**: Twisted-wing parasites.  
			- **Neuroptera**: Net-winged insects (lacewings, antlions).  
			- **Megaloptera**: Alderflies, dobsonflies, and fishflies.  
			- **Raphidioptera**: Snakeflies.  
			- **Trichoptera**: Caddisflies.  
			- **Lepidoptera**: Butterflies and moths.  
			- **Mecoptera**: Scorpionflies.  
			- **Siphonaptera**: Fleas (wingless jumping parasites).  
			- **Diptera**: True flies, mosquitoes, and gnats (possess only two functional wings; the hindwings are modified into balancing organs called halteres).
