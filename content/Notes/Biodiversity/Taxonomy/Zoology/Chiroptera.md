
[[Biodiversity/Taxonomy/Zoology/Mammals|Mammals]]

They are the only mammals capable of true, sustained flight, and they represent roughly 20% of all classified mammal species on Earth.

### Bats taxonomy

- **Yinpterochiroptera** (Pteropodiformes) (flying foxes)
	- **Pteropodidae** (Megabats / Flying Foxes): The only family of large fruit-eating bats; they rely on keen vision and smell rather than echolocation.
	- **Rhinolophidae** (Horseshoe Bats): Characterized by horseshoe-shaped horseshoe flesh on their noses used to focus echolocation.
	- **Hipposideridae** (Old World Leaf-nosed Bats): Closely related to horseshoe bats, possessing elaborate nose leaves.
	- **Rhinopomatidae** (Mouse-tailed Bats): Small bats with exceptionally long, mouse-like tails found in arid climates.
	- **Craseonycteridae** (Kitti's Hog-nosed Bat): A family consisting of a single species, the bumblebee bat, which is the world's smallest mammal.
	- **Megadermatidae** (False Vampire Bats): Relatively large, predatory bats that hunt insects, frogs, and even other bats.

- **Yangochiroptera** (Vespertilioniformes) (microbats): rely entirely on advanced laryngeal echolocation to navigate and hunt in the dark.
	- **Vespertilionidae** (Vesper / Evening Bats): The largest and most widespread bat family globally; they make up the bulk of the species protected under the EUROBATS Agreement.
	- **Molossidae** (Free-tailed Bats): High-speed, narrow-winged flyers with tails that extend far past their flight membranes.
	- **Phyllostomidae** (New World Leaf-nosed Bats): Highly ecologically diverse bats of the Americas, containing fruit-feeders, nectar-feeders, and true vampire bats.
	- **Miniopteridae** (Long-winged or Bent-winged Bats): Fast-flying cave dwellers with highly elongated third fingers.
	- **Mormoopidae** (Ghost-faced / Mustached Bats): Specialized New World bats with intricate skin folds and leaflets around their mouths.
	- **Emballonuridae** (Sac-winged / Sheath-tailed Bats): Named for small gland sacs on their wings that release pheromones.
	- **Noctilionidae** (Bulldog / Fisherman Bats): Highly specialized South American bats with long claws used to gaff fish from the water's surface.
	- **Furipteridae** (Smoky Bats): Small South American bats with tiny, almost entirely reduced thumbs enclosed in their wing membranes.
	- **Thyropteridae** (Disk-winged Bats): Possess specialized suction-cup disks on their wrists and ankles to cling to smooth tropical leaves.
	- **Myzopodidae** (Sucker-footed Bats): Endemic to Madagascar, they independently evolved adhesive suction pads similar to disk-winged bats.
	- **Mystacinidae** (New Zealand Short-tailed Bats): Unique burrowing bats that spend a large amount of time foraging and running on the forest floor.
	- **Natalidae** (Funnel-eared Bats): Fragile, slender-bodied Caribbean and American bats with exceptionally large, funnel-like ears.
	- **Nycteridae** (Slit-faced Bats): African and Asian bats with a deep, distinct longitudinal slit running down the middle of their faces.
	- **Cistugidae** (Wing-gland Bats): A small African family closely related to vesper bats but distinguished by unique glands on their wings.
	- **Cyclorhipidionidae**: A newly split taxonomic classification recognizing specialized Old World lineages.
