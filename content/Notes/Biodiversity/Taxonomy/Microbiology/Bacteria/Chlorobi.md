
Chlorobi (or Chlorobiota) is a phylum of obligately anaerobic, anoxygenic photoautotrophic bacteria commonly known as green sulfur bacteria.

They are evolutionary specialists renowned for their ability to thrive in extremely low-light, sulfide-rich environments where no other photosynthetic organisms can survive.

Chlorobi use reduced sulfur compounds like hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), elemental sulfur (S), or molecular hydrogen (H₂). They do not use water as an electron donor and do not produce oxygen.

When they oxidize hydrogen sulfide, they deposit granules of elemental sulfur outside of their cells (differently from purple sulfur bacteria, which typically store sulfur internally).

They fix carbon dioxide (CO₂) utilizing the reverse tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle (also known as the reductive TCA cycle) (rather than the Calvin cycle used by plants)
