Life


### Life traits

- **Cellular tissue organization:** All living organisms are composed of **one or more cells**, which serve as the fundamental structural and functional units of life

- **Active metabolic processes:** Life requires metabolism, the capacity to consume external energy sources, convert them into chemical energy, and excrete waste products

- **Homeostatic internal regulation:** Living things actively maintain homeostasis, a stable and balanced internal environment despite shifting, hostile external conditions

- **Adaptive evolutionary growth:** Organisms grow, develop according to genetic blueprints, and evolve over generations through natural selection to fit changing environments

- **Response to environmental stimuli:** Life exhibits irritability, meaning it can detect and dynamically react to physical or chemical changes in its surroundings

- **Genetic self-reproduction:** Every living entity has the capacity to reproduce and pass genetic information (DNA or RNA) to offspring, ensuring the continuity of the species

### Life elements

About 98% of any living organism's mass is composed of just six elements:

- **Carbon** (C)
- **Hydrogen** (H)
- **Nitrogen** (N)
- **Oxygen** (O)
- **Phosphorus** (P)
- **Sulfur** (S)

### Life macromolecules

- **Proteins** (The molecular machinery): Made of chains of amino acids, proteins act as structural tissues, cellular receptors, and specialized enzymes that drive metabolic reactions.

- **Nucleic Acids** (The genetic blueprints): DNA and RNA store, transmit, and express the hereditary instructions needed to build and operate a living being.

- **Carbohydrates** (The immediate fuel): Sugars and starches provide quick metabolic energy and form structural barriers, such as cellulose in plant walls or chitin in insect skeletons.

- **Lipids** (The structural boundaries): Fats and oils store long-term energy and form the crucial phospholipid membranes that seal cells, keeping their internal chemistry separated from the outside world.
