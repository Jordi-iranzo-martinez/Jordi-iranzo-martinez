title: Convention for the Protection of the Marine Environment and the Coastal Region of the Mediterranean
---
Originally adopted in 1976 to counter rapid industrial pollution, it was substantially updated in 1995 to expand its scope from merely stopping pollution to fostering comprehensive sustainable development and coastal management. It currently serves as the legal backbone of the United Nations Environment Programme's Mediterranean Action Plan (UNEP/MAP).


The convention unites 22 Contracting Parties: 21 sovereign nations bordering the Mediterranean Sea (including European, North African, and Middle Eastern countries) alongside the European Union.

Geographically, it regulates the entire Mediterranean marine basin, including its internal gulfs. Through specialized sub-protocols, its jurisdiction explicitly stretches inland to the hydrologic basins and drainage systems feeding freshwater into the sea, allowing it to regulate pollution at its land-based source.

The convention operates through seven distinct, legally binding protocols that force signatory nations to coordinate public policies across critical environmental domains:

- **The Dumping Protocol:** Bans or heavily restricts the deliberate disposal of waste from ships and aircraft.

- **The Prevention and Emergency Protocol:** Coordinates regional responses to maritime accidents, massive oil spills, and toxic cargo discharges.

- **The Land-Based Sources (LBS) Protocol:** Targets industrial runoff, agricultural chemical discharges, and municipal wastewater traveling via rivers into the sea.

- **The Specially Protected Areas and Biological Diversity (SPA/BD) Protocol:** Establishes Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) and safeguards threatened marine life.

- **The Offshore Protocol:** Regulates safety and pollution resulting from seabed oil and gas exploration.

- **The Hazardous Wastes Protocol:** Prevents the illegal transboundary movement and disposal of radioactive or toxic industrial waste.

- **The Integrated Coastal Zone Management (ICZM) Protocol:** Establishes sustainable urban planning standards on land to prevent the over-development of fragile coastlines.

Following recent high-level diplomatic agreements, including the Cairo Ministerial Declaration, the treaty framework has established its core targets for the 2026–2035 decade: 

- **The 30x30 Target Integration:** Signatories have officially aligned with the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, committing to place 30% of the Mediterranean Sea under protected status by 2030.

- **Strict Habitat Updates:** To counter ecological decline, the convention's protected annexes have implemented stricter legal bans protecting highly vulnerable deep-sea species, such as the _bramble shark_ and the _kitefin shark_.

- **Climate Resilience Expansion:** An updated **Regional Climate Change Adaptation Framework (RCCAF) for 2026–2035** has entered full force. This protocol establishes regional coordinated centers to handle acute marine heatwaves, sea-level rise along coastal heritage sites, and structural economic adjustments for sustainable tourism.
