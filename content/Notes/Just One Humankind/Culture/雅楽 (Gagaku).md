
雅楽 (Gagaku), literally meaning "elegant music," is the oldest classical music and dance tradition in [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Humankind/Just One World/Japan]]. Developed at the imperial court during the Nara and Heian periods (8th to 12th centuries), it is preserved as one of the world's oldest continuous performing arts and 

The _gagaku_ repertoire blends indigenous Japanese traditions with continental musical influences introduced from China and the Korean Peninsula via the Silk Road:

- **Kuniburi no utamai**. Native Japanese ritual songs and dances, including imperial Shinto music (_kagura_) and _azuma-asobi_.

- **Kangen**. Purely instrumental music performed by an ensemble of wind, string, and percussion instruments.

- **Bugaku**. Dance repertoire accompanied by the orchestra. It is divided into Dances of the Left (_samai_, of Chinese or Indian origin, featuring predominantly red costumes) and Dances of the Right (_umai_, of Korean or Manchurian origin, featuring predominantly green or blue costumes).

- **Utamono**. Court vocal music, comprising _saibara_ (folk songs arranged in court style) and _rōei_ (chanted recitations of Chinese and Japanese poetry).

The _gagaku_ ensemble is known for its distinctive timbre, in which each instrument group symbolically represents elements of the natural world:

- **Winds** (_kangakki_). The _shō_ (a 17-pipe bamboo mouth organ that produces ethereal, sustained clusters representing light from heaven), the _hichiriki_ (a small double-reed oboe with an intense, piercing tone symbolizing the human voice), and transverse flutes such as the _ryūteki_ (representing dragons ascending between heaven and earth).

- **Strings** (_gengakki_). The _gaku-sō_ (a 13-string zither and ancestor of the modern _koto_) and the _gaku-biwa_ (a four-string, short-necked lute played with a large plectrum).

- **Percussion** (_uchimono_). The _taiko_ or _dadaiko_ (large suspended drums), the _kakko_ (a small two-headed drum that leads the tempo in Chinese-derived pieces), and the _shōko_ (a small bronze gong).

Pieces follow a progressive rhythmic structure that begins at a very slow, elastic pace (_jo_), develops the melody (_ha_), and gradually accelerates toward a resolved conclusion (_kyū_).

Today, _gagaku_ is maintained primarily by the Music Department of the Imperial Household Agency in Tokyo, as well as at major Shinto shrines (such as Ise, Kasuga, and Itsukushima) and Buddhist temples for state ceremonies and seasonal festivals.

It is inscribed on the [[UNESCO]] Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
