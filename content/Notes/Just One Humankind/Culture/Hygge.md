
Hygge is a Danish and Norwegian cultural concept describing a cozy, warm atmosphere and a feeling of contentment, safety, and simple well-being enjoyed alone or with close friends and family. 

*The word originates from a 16th-century Norwegian term hugga, meaning "to comfort" or "to console," which is related to the English word "hug.". It encompasses a deeper emotional sense of intimacy, equality, gratitude, and freedom from stress or social status.

Rather than a single physical object or activity, hygge is an everyday philosophy centered on slowing down, savoring the present moment, and creating comfort—especially during Denmark's long, dark winters.

Key Elements:

- **Atmosphere and lighting**. Soft, warm lighting is essential—most notably candles (Danes burn more candles per capita than almost anywhere else in Europe), fireplaces, and warm-toned lamps rather than harsh overhead lights.

- **Togetherness and conversation**. _Hygge_ thrives in small, intimate gatherings with close friends or family where everyone feels relaxed, without bragging, arguments, or digital distractions.

- **Simple pleasures and comfort**. It involves tactile comforts like thick woolen socks (_hyggesokker_), soft blankets, wooden interiors, books, board games, and warm drinks or homemade treats such as coffee, mulled wine (_gløgg_), pastries, and slow-cooked stews.

- **Presence and mindfulness**. Being fully present in the moment—whether watching rain against a window, walking in nature, or sharing a quiet dinner—is central to experiencing _hygge_.

It entered Danish in the 18th century and evolved into a defining pillar of Danish national identity.

Cultural researchers often link it to why Denmark consistently ranks among the happiest countries in the world, as it acts as a psychological buffer against winter darkness and daily stress.

In the mid-2010s, _hygge_ became an international lifestyle trend, inspiring bestselling books (such as Meik Wiking's _The Little Book of Hygge_) and influencing interior design, hospitality, and slow-living movements worldwide.
