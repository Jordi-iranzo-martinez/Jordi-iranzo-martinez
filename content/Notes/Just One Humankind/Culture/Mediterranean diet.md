The Mediterranean diet is an eating pattern inspired by the traditional cuisines of countries bordering the [[Mediterranean Sea]], such as [[Spain]], Italy, and Greece.

Widely recognized by nutritionists and medical organizations like the American Heart Association as one of the healthiest ways to eat, it emphasizes whole, minimally processed plant foods and healthy fats rather than strict calorie counting.

Rather than banning specific food groups, the Mediterranean diet organizes meals around the [Mediterranean Diet Pyramid](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAEShAEBxCJF11_no4bVabgFm7-cT0X4Yd3ObnXudmZizrn-8F7Ot8V-2ETU1VdFZaeFoCHp2gJyWHxLWNazGG1dMXCrDh4FrrAiJdU7nyaPjQHht6pkuqN155AJLiYtjK2_7Y0yrkWnlggDxaK6gSIKkX6JChRBx112R-J_cNL7TAk52jtd19A) and the **plate method** (filling half your plate with vegetables, a quarter with whole grains, and a quarter with lean protein):

- **Eat Daily** (Base of the Diet).** Fill meals with colorful vegetables, fresh fruits, whole grains (such as oats, brown rice, whole-wheat bread, and farro), legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans), nuts, seeds, and fresh herbs and spices instead of excess salt.

- **Primary Fat Source**. Use extra virgin olive oil as your main cooking and dressing fat, replacing butter, margarine, and refined vegetable oils.

- **Eat at Least Twice a Week**. Prioritize fatty fish and seafood rich in omega-3 fatty acids, such as salmon, sardines, mackerel, trout, and tuna.

- **Eat in Moderate Portions** (Daily to Weekly). Include poultry (chicken, turkey), eggs, and fermented dairy like plain Greek yogurt and small amounts of natural cheese.

- **Limit or Eat Rarely**. Keep red meat, processed meats (bacon, sausages), refined sugars, pastries, and ultra-processed packaged foods to occasional small treats.

- **Hydration and Lifestyle**. Drink plenty of water throughout the day. If you drink alcohol, red wine is traditionally consumed in strict moderation with meals (up to one glass per day for women and two for men), alongside regular physical activity and shared social meals.


Key Health Benefits

- **Cardiovascular Protection**. High intake of monounsaturated fats, fiber, and antioxidants helps lower LDL ("bad") cholesterol, reduce blood pressure, and decrease the risk of heart disease and stroke.

- **Blood Sugar and Weight Management**. Fiber-rich legumes, whole grains, and healthy fats slow digestion, stabilizing blood sugar levels and promoting lasting fullness without restrictive dieting.

- **Anti-Inflammatory and Brain Health**. Polyphenols from olive oil, berries, leafy greens, and omega-3s from fish help combat chronic inflammation and support long-term cognitive function.


Practical Everyday Swaps

- **Cooking Fats**. Swap butter or creamy sauces for a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil, lemon juice, and garlic.

- **Snacks**. Replace chips or cookies with a small handful of almonds or walnuts, hummus with raw vegetables, or fresh fruit.

- **Proteins**. Swap red meat dinners a couple of nights a week for grilled sardines, baked salmon, or a hearty lentil and vegetable stew.
