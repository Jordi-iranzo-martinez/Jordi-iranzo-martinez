
Zaouli (also spelled Zawli) is a traditional mask dance and musical performance of the Guro people of central Ivory Coast (Côte d'Ivoire). Famous worldwide for its extraordinarily rapid, intricate footwork and illusion of floating across the ground. 

Unlike ancient ritual dances, Zaouli is a relatively modern tradition that originated in the mid-20th century in the Bouaflé and Zuénoula regions of Ivory Coast.

According to Guro oral tradition, the dance was inspired by a strikingly beautiful young woman named Djela Lou Zaouli (meaning "Zaouli, daughter of Djela"). Every Zaouli mask represents a feminine ideal of beauty, grace, and serenity.

Performed exclusively by male dancers, Zaouli demands immense athletic stamina. The dancer moves their feet at blurring speed while keeping their upper body and masked head completely still, creating the optical illusion that they are gliding effortlessly over the earth.

The wooden mask features delicate, symmetrical female facial features painted in vibrant colors, topped with carved animals, horns, birds, or serpents representing spiritual protection and fertility. The dancer wears a brightly colored woven raffia skirt, bells around their ankles to accentuate the rhythm, and holds horsetail whisks in each hand.

A live orchestra of flutes and percussion instruments (such as drums and gourds) engages in a rhythmic dialogue with the dancer, accelerating the tempo as the performance builds to a climax.

Originally performed at funerals, harvests, and village celebrations, Zaouli serves both as a form of entertainment and as a symbol of social cohesion and cultural identity for the Guro communities.

It was inscribed on the [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Humankind/People/UNESCO]] Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017.
