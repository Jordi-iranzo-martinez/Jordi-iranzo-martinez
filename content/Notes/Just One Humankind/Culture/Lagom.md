Lagom is a Swedish cultural concept and lifestyle philosophy meaning "just the right amount"—neither too much nor too little, but balanced, sufficient, and in moderation. 

Unlike strict austerity or self-denial, lagom emphasizes sufficiency, fairness, and contentment—finding satisfaction in what is adequate rather than constantly striving for extremes.

Deeply embedded in Swedish everyday life, etiquette, design, and sustainability, lagom serves as a guiding principle for living harmoniously without excess or deprivation.

*The word derives from the Old Swedish laghum, the dative plural form of lag ("law" or "team/group"), originally meaning "according to common sense" or "suitable for the group." A popular folk etymology links it to the Viking phrase laget om ("around the team"), referring to passing a horn of mead around a circle so that everyone received a fair, equal share.*

Unlike strict austerity or self-denial, lagom emphasizes sufficiency, fairness, and contentment—finding satisfaction in what is adequate rather than constantly striving for extremes.

Sweden's workplace culture reflects _lagom_ through reasonable working hours, generous parental leave, mandatory vacation time, and daily fika (social coffee-and-pastry breaks) to recharge without burnout.

_Lagom_ encourages mindful resource use—buying durable goods rather than fast fashion, recycling, conserving energy, and minimizing waste so there is enough left for others and the environment.

Alongside Danish _hygge_, _lagom_ shapes Scandinavian design: clean, functional, uncluttered spaces combining natural wood, soft neutral tones, practical furniture, and just enough warmth to feel inviting without excess ornamentation.

Socially, *lagom* discourages boastfulness, conspicuous wealth, or dominating conversations, favoring modesty, consensus, and mutual respect.
