he image was captured during the British Antarctic Expedition (1910–1913)—widely known as the Terra Nova Expedition—led by Captain Robert Falcon Scott.

*[[Antarctica]], [[Cryosphere]]*

Ponting ventured inside a hollow carved by wind and waves into a large iceberg grounded about a mile off Ross Island, Antarctica. The fluted, striated ice walls and hanging fringe of icicles act as a dramatic natural frame drawing the viewer's eye outward.

Directly through the opening of the ice cavern sits the silhouette of the expedition ship *Terra Nova* anchored along the edge of the sea ice.

Ponting exposed multiple glass plates during the same session: one version without people (focusing purely on the sculptural ice architecture and distant ship) and another equally famous version featuring two expedition scientists—geologist Thomas Griffith Taylor and meteorologist Charles S. Wright—standing inside the grotto's entrance to provide scale.

Although the original glass-plate negatives were monochrome, Ponting was so struck by the shifting blue, green, and violet light inside the ice cave that he later produced exhibition carbon prints tinted in green or blue to recreate the cavern's luminous atmosphere.

Hired specifically as the expedition's "camera artist," Ponting developed his glass plates in a makeshift darkroom aboard the _Terra Nova_ and later in the expedition hut at Cape Evans.

Considered by Ponting himself to be his finest photograph, vintage prints are preserved today in major collections including the Royal Collection Trust (presented to King George V in 1914), the Victoria and Albert Museum, and the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA).
