The Soweto Uprising was a series of student-led demonstrations and protests in [[South Africa]] that began on June 16, 1976, in the segregated Black township of [Soweto](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Soweto,+South+Africa/@-26.2438955,27.8382714,22599m/data=!3m2!1e3!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x1e95a6b1e2746071:0x3a0838c21e9e6ddf!8m2!3d-26.2485377!4d27.8540323!16zL20vMDcyOXQ?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MTAyNy4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D) (South Western Townships) near Johannesburg.

Sparked by resistance to the apartheid government's imposition of Afrikaans as a mandatory language of instruction in Black schools, the peaceful march was met with lethal police gunfire, marking a major turning point in the struggle against apartheid.

Under the 1953 Bantu Education Act, South Africa's white minority regime deliberately underfunded schools for Black children to prepare them strictly for manual labor and subservient roles.

The government mandated that Black secondary schools teach core subjects (such as mathematics and social studies) equally in English and Afrikaans (The Afrikaans Medium Decree of 1974). Most Black students and teachers neither spoke Afrikaans fluently nor wished to learn in what they viewed as the "language of the oppressor".

Inspired by activist Steve Biko's Black Consciousness Movement, high school students organized through the South African Students' Movement (SASM) and the Soweto Students' Representative Council (SSRC).

On the morning of June 16, between 10,000 and 20,000 Black schoolchildren walked out of their classrooms and marched peacefully toward Orlando Stadium carrying handmade signs reading "Down with Afrikaans" and "Away with Bantu Education".

Heavily armed police barricaded the route, released police dogs, fired tear gas, and ultimately opened fire with live ammunition directly into the crowd of unarmed youths.

Thirteen-year-old Hastings Ndlovu and twelve-year-old Hector Pieterson were among the very first students killed. Photojournalist Sam Nzima captured an iconic photograph of high school student Mbuyisa Makhubo carrying the mortally wounded Hector Pieterson with Hector's sister Antoinette Sithole running beside them—an image published worldwide that exposed the brutality of the apartheid state.

Outraged by the shootings, protests, strikes, and clashes spread rapidly from Soweto to townships across South Africa over the following months. While the government initially claimed only 23 people died, official inquiries later recorded 575 deaths, and independent estimates place the toll at over 600 to 700 killed (mostly young people) and thousands injured or arrested.

The uprising revitalized anti-apartheid resistance inside South Africa, drove thousands of young people into exile to join liberation movements like the African National Congress (ANC), and triggered international condemnation—including UN Security Council Resolution 392 and growing economic sanctions against Pretoria.

In democratic South Africa, June 16 is commemorated annually as Youth Day (a national public holiday) as well as the continental Day of the African Child.
