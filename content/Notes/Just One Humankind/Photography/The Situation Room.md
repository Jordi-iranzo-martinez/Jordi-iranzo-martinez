The Situation Room is a famous documentary photograph taken on May 1, 2011, at 4:05 PM EDT by Chief Official White House Photographer Pete Souza.

The image captures U.S. President Barack Obama and his national security team watching live updates of Operation Neptune Spear, the U.S. Navy SEAL raid on Osama bin Laden's compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan.

Although the underground White House intelligence complex is commonly referred to in the singular, it consists of multiple rooms. The photo was actually taken in a smaller adjoining conference room where Brigadier General Marshall B. "Brad" Webb had a live communications feed open on his laptop with Admiral William McRaven (commander of the raid). Obama chose to remain there rather than risk losing the connection by moving to the larger room.

Rather than sitting at the head of a grand table, Obama sits on a black folding chair in the corner wearing casual golf attire (having played earlier that day to avoid raising suspicion), leaning forward with a tense, focused expression.

Secretary of State Hillary Clinton is seen holding her right hand over her mouth in apparent shock—snapped just as one of the U.S. helicopters suffered a hard landing inside the compound. Clinton later noted with humor that she was likely stifling a spring allergy cough.

A classified briefing folder resting on Clinton's lap was digitally blurred by the White House before the photograph was released to the public.

Key People Pictured:

- **Seated** (left to right): Vice President Joe Biden, President Barack Obama, Brigadier General Marshall B. "Brad" Webb, Deputy National Security Advisor Denis McDonough, Secretary of State Hillary Clinton, and Secretary of Defense Robert Gates.

- **Standing** (left to right): Admiral Mike Mullen (Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff), National Security Advisor Tom Donilon, White House Chief of Staff Bill Daley, Antony Blinken (then National Security Advisor to the Vice President), Audrey Tomason (Director for Counterterrorism), John Brennan (Homeland Security Advisor), and Director of National Intelligence James Clapper (with a CIA analyst partially visible at the far right edge).

Uploaded to the White House Flickr account on May 2, 2011, it rapidly became one of the most-viewed images on the internet and was widely compared by art critics to classic historical paintings for its dramatic composition.

Shortly after its release, the Brooklyn-based Hasidic newspaper _Der Zeitung_ sparked widespread criticism when it published a doctored version of the photo that digitally erased both women in the room (Hillary Clinton and Audrey Tomason) to comply with its modesty policy.
