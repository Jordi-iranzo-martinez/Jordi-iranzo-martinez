Frame 313 (Z-313) is the single most famous and controversial frame of the Zapruder film, the silent 8mm home movie shot by Dallas dressmaker Abraham Zapruder during the assassination of President John F. Kennedy on November 22, 1963. It captures the exact moment the fatal bullet struck Kennedy in the head.

Out of the 486 frames in the 26.6-second film (shot at roughly 18.3 frames per second), Frame 313 records the instantaneous explosion of blood and brain matter as the bullet strikes the right side of the President's skull.

In the frames immediately following Z-313 (specifically frames 314 through 321), Kennedy's head and upper body snap sharply back and to the left against the rear seat cushion, prompting First Lady Jacqueline Kennedy to climb onto the back trunk of the limousine.

Trajectory and Conspiracy Debates. For decades, researchers and critics of the official investigation pointed to Kennedy's violent backward movement right after Frame 313 as evidence of a second shooter firing from the "grassy knoll" in front and to the right of the motorcade, rather than Lee Harvey Oswald firing from behind in the Texas School Book Depository.

Ballistic and Medical Explanations. Both the Warren Commission (1964) and the House Select Committee on Assassinations (1979) concluded that the fatal shot came from behind. Ballistics experts and physicists attributed the backward jerk to a combination of a neuromuscular spasm triggered by severe brainstem trauma and the jet effect (momentum transfer from matter exiting the front-right side of the skull).


When Life magazine purchased the film rights immediately after the assassination, editors published black-and-white stills of several frames in November 1963 but deliberately withheld Frame 313 out of respect for the Kennedy family and due to its graphic nature.

Although bootleg copies circulated among researchers in the late 1960s, Frame 313 and the full motion sequence were not broadcast on national television until March 1975, when Geraldo Rivera aired a copy on the ABC late-night show Good Night America, sparking widespread public outrage that led to new congressional investigations.
