[Cathedral Rocks](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Cathedral+Rocks/@-34.6457266,150.8360837,14z/data=!4m10!1m2!2m1!1sCathedral+Rocks!3m6!1s0x6b136c83867fb677:0x9c5967cbe6966029!8m2!3d-34.6448026!4d150.8565595!15sCg9DYXRoZWRyYWwgUm9ja3NaESIPY2F0aGVkcmFsIHJvY2tzkgESdG91cmlzdF9hdHRyYWN0aW9umgFEQ2k5RFFVbFJRVU52WkVOb2RIbGpSamx2VDI1c2NHRXdkRVZWUlRWWFYwVnNSbE5ITkhoTlJra3pWVVp2TVZreFJSQULgAQD6AQQIABA5!16s%2Fm%2F0k52jz0?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MTExMC4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D) are a massive, three-summit granite formation rising over 2,000 feet above the south side of Yosemite Valley, situated directly across from El Capitan and just east of Bridalveil Fall.

Known to the Indigenous Ahwahneechee people as Poosenachuka, the formation is flanked by two detached, needle-like granite towers—Higher and Lower Cathedral Spires—which together form one of the most iconic silhouettes in Yosemite National Park.

It stands as one of the most influential images in American history, serving as a primary visual catalyst that inspired President Abraham Lincoln to sign the historic Yosemite Valley Grant Act of 1864. This landmark legislation paved the way for the creation of the United States National Park System.
