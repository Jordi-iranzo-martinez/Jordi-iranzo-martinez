The iconic photographs of U.S. President Theodore Roosevelt jumping over a split-rail fence on horseback were taken in May 1902 near Washington, D.C., during his first year in office.

Captured mid-leap on his favorite hunter horse, Bleistein, the images became some of the most famous action portraits of an American president.

Roosevelt was riding his prized bay hunter, Bleistein—named after a German newspaper publisher—known for being a strong, reliable jumper.

Taken in early May 1902 (commonly dated May 8, 1902), the photos were shot by press photographers invited to document the president's afternoon rides through Rock Creek Park and the surrounding Maryland countryside.

At a time when presidential photography usually consisted of stiff, formal studio portraits, seeing the 43-year-old president airborne in mid-stride reinforced his reputation as a rugged outdoorsman, former Dakota Territory rancher, and Rough Rider cavalry commander.

Photographers captured several variations of the jump—including a dramatic front-quarter angle and a clean side profile—which are preserved in the Library of Congress and the Theodore Roosevelt Center.
