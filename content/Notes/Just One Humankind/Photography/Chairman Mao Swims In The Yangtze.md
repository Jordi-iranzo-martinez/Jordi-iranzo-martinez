Following the catastrophic failure of the Great Leap Forward, Mao had been politically sidelined by moderate leaders like Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping. Rumors circulated both inside and outside China that Mao was gravely ill or near death.

By joining 5,000 swimmers in Wuhan's annual Cross-Yangtze competition, Mao proved his physical vitality. Two days later, he returned to Beijing to purge his political rivals and mobilize the Red Guards.

State media framed the Yangtze's strong winds and currents as metaphors for political struggle. The message was clear: just as Mao conquered the mighty river, he was ready to lead China through a turbulent new revolution.

The event inspired countless colorful Cultural Revolution posters, typically depicting Mao standing heroically in a white bathrobe aboard a boat, waving to thousands of ecstatic swimmers beneath the Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge.
