
Buddhism is a major non-theistic religion, philosophy, and spiritual tradition that originated in ancient northern India between the 6th and 5th centuries BCE. 

Based on the teachings of Siddhartha Gautama—known as the Buddha ("the Awakened One")—it has over 500 million followers worldwide and focuses on ending human suffering through wisdom, ethical conduct, and meditation.

Siddhartha Gautama, bornt a royal prince in Lumbini (modern-day Nepal), renounced his sheltered life of luxury after encountering old age, sickness, and death, setting out to understand the root of human suffering.

After realizing that neither extreme indulgence nor severe self-mortification led to truth, he achieved enlightenment (_bodhi_) while meditating beneath a sacred fig tree (the Bodhi Tree) in Bodh Gaya, India.

Core Teachings

- **The Four Noble Truths.** These form the foundation of Buddhist thought:
    1. **Dukkha** (Suffering). Life inherently involves dissatisfaction, pain, and impermanence.
    2. **Samudaya** (Origin of Suffering). Suffering arises from craving, attachment, and ignorance.
    3. **Nirodha** (Cessation of Suffering). Suffering can end by letting go of craving and attachment.
    4. **Magga** (The Path). The way to end suffering is by following the Noble Eightfold Path.

- **The Noble Eightfold Path.** A practical guide divided into three areas:
	- **Wisdom** (right view, right intention)
	- **Ethical conduct** (right speech, right action, right livelihood)
	- **Mental discipline** (right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration).

Buddhism teaches that intentional actions (karma) shape the continuous cycle of birth, death, and rebirth (samsara).

The ultimate goal is nirvana—a state of profound peace and liberation from desire and rebirth.

Major Branches of Buddhism

- **Theravada** ("School of the Elders"). The oldest surviving tradition, rooted in early Pali scriptures and focused on personal liberation through meditation and monastic discipline. It predominates in Thailand, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Laos, and Cambodia.

- **Mahayana** ("Great Vehicle"). Emphasizes universal compassion and the ideal of the _bodhisattva_—an enlightened being who delays final nirvana to help all other beings achieve liberation. It includes Zen and Pure Land traditions and predominates in [[China]], [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Humankind/Just One World/Japan]], [[Korea]], and Vietnam.

- **Vajrayana** ("Diamond Vehicle" or Tibetan Buddhism). Developed in [[Tibet]] and the Himalayas, it uses specialized rituals, mantras, mandalas, and visualization practices to accelerate enlightenment. It is practiced primarily in Tibet, Bhutan, and Mongolia.

Buddhism spread across [[Asia]] over several centuries through a combination of royal patronage, traveling monks, and major overland and maritime trade routes—most notably the [[Silk Road]]. Rather than spreading through military conquest, it adapted organically to local cultures, languages, and spiritual traditions.
