
Hinduism is one of the world's oldest major religions, with roots and customs dating back more than 4,000 years in the Indian subcontinent. Unlike other major global faiths, it has no single founder, no absolute scripture, and no centralized institutional authority.

The Hindu worldview is sustained by several interconnected spiritual pillars:

- **Brahman**: The ultimate reality, the supreme, absolute, and universal principle that underlies everything that exists. God is not just an external entity, but an infinite divine energy residing inside every living being—this individual soul or divine spark is known as Atman.

- **Karma**: The cosmic law of cause and effect. It establishes that every action (physical, verbal, or mental) has a direct consequence in this life or in future incarnations.

- **Samsara**: The continuous cycle of birth, death, and rebirth. The soul transmigrates from one body to another based on the merit accumulated by its karma.

- **Moksha**: The ultimate liberation from the cycle of _Samsara_. It is the highest spiritual goal in Hinduism, where the individual soul (_Atman_) unites and completely merges back with the Absolute (_Brahman_).

- **Dharma**: The moral and religious duty, righteousness, and cosmic order that each individual must follow according to their stage of life and social role.

While Hinduism recognizes countless deities and local manifestations, the primary theology is organized around three supreme gods who control the forces of the universe:

- **Brahma**: The Creator of the universe (not to be confused with the abstract concept of _Brahman_).

- **Vishnu**: The Preserver and protector of cosmic order. He frequently descends to Earth in the form of avatars (such as Rama or Krishna) to restore good whenever chaos threatens the world.

- **Shiva**: The Destroyer and transformer. He destroys the physical universe at the end of each cosmic era to allow it to be purely recreated.

Hinduism possesses a vast library of spiritual literature written in ancient Sanskrit, divided primarily into two categories:

- **Shruti** ("What is heard"): Direct divine revelations. Which include:
	- Four [[Vedas]]: the oldest texts filled with hymns and rituals)
	- [[Upanishads]]: deep philosophical reflections on the nature of the soul and God.

- **Smriti** ("What is remembered"): Traditions passed down by humans. This includes the great mythological epics like the _Ramayana_ and the _Mahabharata_. Tucked within the latter is the Bhagavad Gita, one of the most widely read and revered spiritual books in the world.


Major Festivals:

- **Diwali** (The Festival of Lights): the most important celebration of the Hindu year. It broadly symbolizes the spiritual triumph of light over darkness, knowledge over ignorance, and good over evil.

- **Holi** (The Festival of Colors): a vibrant spring festival where people throw brightly colored powders and water at each other, celebrating the arrival of the new season and the divine love of Radha and Krishna.
