
Fascism is a far-right, authoritarian, and ultranationalist political ideology characterized by dictatorial power, the forcible suppression of opposition, and the strict subordination of individual interests to the perceived good of the nation or race.

Emerging in early 20th-century Europe—most notably in [[Benito Mussolini]]'s Italy and [[Adolf Hitler]]'s Germany—fascism rejected both liberal democracy and Marxist socialism, promising national rebirth through mass mobilization, militarism, and charismatic leadership.

Historians and political scientists generally identify several defining features of fascist movements and regimes:

- **Ultranationalism & Mythic Past**. An intense, exclusionary nationalism centered on purifying and rejuvenating the nation, often invoking a mythical golden age of former glory and portraying the nation as under siege by internal and external enemies.

- **Authoritarian Dictatorship**. Concentration of absolute power in a single supreme leader (*Il Duce*, *Der Führer*, *El Caudillo*), accompanied by the dismantling of democratic institutions, independent courts, and a free press.

- **Glorification of Violence & Militarism**. Viewing struggle, war, and imperial expansion not merely as political tools, but as noble, purifying forces that prove a nation's vitality.

- **Suppression of Opposition**. Systematic use of state terror, secret police, paramilitary squads (such as Italy's Blackshirts or Germany's Brownshirts), censorship, and political imprisonment to eliminate rivals.

- **State-Directed Economy** (Corporatism). Unlike communism, fascism typically preserves private property and profit, but subordinates businesses, labor unions, and the economy to state goals—suppressing independent labor movements while coordinating production for national power and rearmament.

- **Cult of Youth, Masculinity & Mass Spectacle**. Reliance on mass rallies, uniforms, propaganda, and youth organizations to instill obedience, conformity, and devotion to the state.

*The term comes from the Italian fascio ("bundle") and the ancient Roman fasces—a bundle of wooden rods bound around an axe that symbolized authority and strength through unity.*

- **Fascist [[Italy]]** (1922–1943). Founded by Benito Mussolini after World War I, the National Fascist Party seized power following the 1922 March on Rome, establishing the first self-described fascist totalitarian state.

- **Nazi [[Germany]]** (1933–1945). Adolf Hitler's National Socialism adapted Italian fascism with an extreme biological racism and virulent antisemitism at its core, culminating in World War II in Europe and the Holocaust.

*During the 1930s and 1940s, fascist and fascist-aligned movements—such as [[Francisco Franco]]'s Falangist regime in Spain, António de Oliveira Salazar's Estado Novo in Portugal, the Iron Guard in Romania, and the Ustaše in Croatia—shared many anti-democratic, ultranationalist, and militaristic traits, though historians debate the exact boundaries between classical fascism and conservative authoritarianism.*
