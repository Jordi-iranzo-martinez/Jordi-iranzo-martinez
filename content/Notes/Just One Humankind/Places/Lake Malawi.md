[Lake Malawi National Park](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Lake+Malawi+National+Park/@-14.064759,34.8846502,764m/data=!3m2!1e3!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x18dfbe247d37fbc5:0x2a1299ca1bf623a6!8m2!3d-14.064759!4d34.8846502!16zL20vMGIzZ3Jk?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MTAyOC4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D) is the world's first freshwater national park and a designated UNESCO World Heritage Site located at the southern end of Lake Malawi in southern Malawi. Established in 1980, the park protects a unique underwater ecosystem famous for its extraordinary concentration of colorful endemic fish.

Covering roughly 94 to 95 square kilometers (about 37 square miles), the park encompasses the hilly Nankumbu Peninsula around Cape Maclear, nearby coastal headlands, and 12 to 13 small offshore islands, including Mumbo Island, Domwe Island, Thumbi West Island, and Boadzulu (Bird) Island.

To safeguard shallow-water habitats, the park's boundaries extend 100 meters (330 feet) out into the lake waters from all protected shorelines and islands.
