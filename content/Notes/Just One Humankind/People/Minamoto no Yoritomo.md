Minamoto no Yoritomo (1147–1199) was the founder and first shōgun of the Kamakura shogunate, establishing nearly 700 years of samurai-led feudal rule in Japan. By defeating the rival Taira clan in the Genpei War (1180–1185), he shifted real political power away from the imperial court in Kyoto to his military government (_bakufu_) in Kamakura.]

Born into the prestigious Seiwa Genji branch of the Minamoto clan, Yoritomo was spared execution as a child after his father's failed rebellion against the Taira clan in 1160. He spent 20 years in exile in Izu Province, where he married Hōjō Masako and built vital alliances with eastern warrior families.

In 1180, Yoritomo rallied the Minamoto clan to overthrow Taira dominance. Relying heavily on the battlefield brilliance of his younger half-brother, Minamoto no Yoshitsune, Yoritomo's forces crushed the Taira at the naval Battle of Dan-no-ura in 1185.

Rather than overthrowing the emperor, Yoritomo set up a parallel military administration in Kamakura. In 1192, the imperial court formally granted him the title of _Seii Taishōgun_ ("Commander-in-Chief of the Expeditionary Force Against the Barbarians"), recognizing him as Japan's supreme military ruler.

Determined to eliminate any threats to his authority, Yoritomo turned against his own family members—most famously hunting down and forcing the suicide of his brother Yoshitsune, whose popularity and independent ambitions he viewed as dangerous.

Yoritomo left the emperor and aristocracy intact as ceremonial figures in Kyoto while centralizing military, legal, and land-management authority in Kamakura.

He rewarded loyal samurai retainers (_gokenin_) by confirming their land rights and appointing them as provincial military governors (_shugo_) and estate stewards (_jitō_), laying the institutional groundwork for Japanese feudalism.

After Yoritomo died following a fall from a horse in 1199, his widow Hōjō Masako and her family seized real control behind his sons, ruling as regents (_shikken_) until the Kamakura shogunate fell in 1333.
