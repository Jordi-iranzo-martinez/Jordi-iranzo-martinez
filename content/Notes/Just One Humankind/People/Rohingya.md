
The Rohingya are a stateless Indo-Aryan ethnic group who predominantly follow Islam and historically resided in Rakhine State (formerly Arakan) on the western coast of Myanmar (Burma).

Widely described by the United Nations as one of the most persecuted minorities in the world, the vast majority of the Rohingya population now lives as refugees in neighboring Bangladesh and across South and Southeast [[Asia]].

The Rohingya speak Rohingya (_Ruáingga_), an Eastern Indo-Aryan language closely related to the Chittagonian dialect spoken in southeastern Bangladesh, and distinct from the Sino-Tibetan Burmese language spoken by Myanmar's majority Bamar population.

The overwhelming majority of Rohingya are Sunni Muslims, alongside a small Hindu minority, setting them apart religiously in predominantly Buddhist Myanmar.

While Rohingya historians trace their presence in the independent Kingdom of Arakan back centuries to Arab, Persian, and Bengali traders and settlers, Myanmar's government and nationalist groups have long claimed they are illegal immigrants brought from Bengal during British colonial rule (1824–1948).

Under Myanmar's military junta, the 1982 Citizenship Law excluded the Rohingya from the country's 135 officially recognized ethnic groups (_taingyintha_), effectively stripping them of nationality and rendering them stateless.

For decades, authorities imposed severe restrictions on Rohingya freedom of movement, marriage, family size, healthcare, higher education, and employment, alongside periodic military crackdowns in 1978, 1991–1992, 2012, and 2016.

In August 2017, following attacks on security posts by Rohingya insurgents (ARSA), Myanmar's military launched a brutal "clearance operation" involving mass killings, sexual violence, and the burning of hundreds of villages. More than 740,000 Rohingya fled across the border into Bangladesh within months, in an operation the UN described as a "textbook example of ethnic cleansing" and which is currently the subject of a genocide case brought by The Gambia against Myanmar at the [[International Court of Justice]] (ICJ).

Over 1 million Rohingya refugees currently live in densely populated, makeshift camps in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh (including Kutupalong, the world's largest refugee settlement), where they remain largely dependent on international humanitarian aid and face restrictions on formal employment and movement.

Escalating civil war in Myanmar between the military junta and the ethnic armed Arakan Army in Rakhine State has displaced tens of thousands more civilians, leaving the roughly 600,000 Rohingya still inside Myanmar caught between warring factions with bleak prospects for safe repatriation.
