Mordred (also spelled Modred, from the Old Welsh Medraut) is the infamous traitor of Arthurian legend who usurped the throne of Camelot and fatally wounded King Arthur at the Battle of Camlann.

While early Welsh traditions depict him ambiguously—and sometimes even heroically—later medieval romances cast him as Arthur's illegitimate son and the catalyst for the Round Table's downfall.

His earliest known appearance is in the 10th-century Annales Cambriae (entry for c. 537), which briefly notes the "strife of Camlann, in which Arthur and Medraut fell," without specifying whether they fought against each other or side by side. Early Welsh poetry actually praises Medraut for his valor and courtesy.

In Historia Regum Britanniae (c. 1136), Geoffrey popularized Mordred as King Arthur's treacherous nephew (son of Arthur's sister Anna/Morgause and King Lot of Lothian), who seizes the crown and marries Queen Guinevere while Arthur is away campaigning in Europe.

Later prose cycles (the Vulgate Cycle) and Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur (1485) deepened the tragedy by making Mordred Arthur's incestuous son, conceived unknowingly with his half-sister Morgause before Arthur realized they were related.

Warned by Merlin that a child born on May Day would destroy him and his kingdom, Arthur famously ordered all noble newborn boys set adrift in a ship—a dark act that failed when baby Mordred washed ashore alive and was raised in secret.

As a Knight of the Round Table (and half-brother to Gawain, Agravain, Gaheris, and Gareth), Mordred conspired with Agravain to catch Sir Lancelot and Queen Guinevere together, sparking the civil war that fractured the Round Table.

Left as regent while Arthur pursued Lancelot to France, Mordred forged letters claiming Arthur had died, crowned himself king, and tried to force Guinevere to marry him.

When Arthur returned to Britain, their armies met at Camlann. During a tense truce negotiation, an adder bit a knight's foot; the knight drew his sword to kill the snake, accidentally triggering a bloodbath. In the final duel, Arthur ran Mordred through with a spear, and Mordred pulled himself up the shaft to deal Arthur a mortal blow to the head before dying.
