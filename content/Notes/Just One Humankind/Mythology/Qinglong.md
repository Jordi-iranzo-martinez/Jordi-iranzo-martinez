Qinglong (Chinese: 青龙, the "Azure Dragon" or "Blue-Green Dragon"; known as Seiryū in Japan and Cheongryong in Korea) is one of the Four Symbols (Sì Xiàng) of Chinese constellations and mythology, serving as the celestial guardian of the East, the season of Spring, and the element of Wood

*[[Ancient China]], [[Ancient Japan]], [[Ancient Korea]]*

Representing sunrise, new growth, rain, and life-giving _Yang_ energy, Qinglong governs the seven eastern lunar mansions (constellations) of the night sky.

Unlike Western fire-breathing dragons, Qinglong is a majestic, wingless serpentine dragon with gleaming blue-green (_qīng_) scales, stag-like antlers, whiskers, and five-toed claws—often depicted coiling through clouds and controlling rain, storms, and rivers.

Together with the Vermilion Bird (*Zhuque*) of the South (Summer/Fire), the White Tiger (*Baihu*) of the West (Autumn/Metal), and the Black Tortoise (*Xuanwu*) of the North (Winter/Water), Qinglong maintains cosmic balance across the heavens and Earth.

In [[Taoism]], Qinglong is revered as a protective deity (often paired with the White Tiger on temple gates), and historically symbolized the Chinese Emperor and imperial authority.
