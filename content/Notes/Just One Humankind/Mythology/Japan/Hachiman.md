Hachiman (八幡神, Hachiman-shin, meaning "God of Eight Banners") is the Japanese syncretic god of war, archery, and divine protection, revered both in Shinto and Japanese Buddhism as the guardian deity of warriors and the Japanese nation.

*[[Ancient Japan]], [[Buddhism]], [[Shintoism]]*

Unlike gods born in creation myths, Hachiman is identified as the deified spirit of the semi-legendary Emperor Ōjin (who ruled in the late 3rd/early 4th century), often worshipped alongside his mother, Empress Jingū.

During the feudal era, Hachiman became the tutelary deity of the Minamoto samurai clan (and later the Ashikaga and Tokugawa shogunates). Samurai across Japan invoked his name (_"Namu Hachiman Daibosatsu"_) before battle for courage and victory.

In 1274 and 1281, when massive Mongol fleets attempted to invade Japan, devastating typhoons (_kamikaze_, or "divine winds") destroyed the invading armadas—miracles widely attributed to prayers offered to Hachiman.

*[[Mongols]]*

His sacred animal and messenger is the dove (whose cooing was said to guide warriors), and his emblem is the swirling _mitsudomoe_ symbol. Because he was among the first Shinto _kami_ harmonized with Buddhism, he is famously depicted wearing the robes of a Buddhist monk (_Sōgyō Hachiman_).

Before becoming a war deity, Hachiman originated as an agricultural and fishing god—and despite his martial title, he is also prayed to for peace, safe childbirth, and protection of children.
