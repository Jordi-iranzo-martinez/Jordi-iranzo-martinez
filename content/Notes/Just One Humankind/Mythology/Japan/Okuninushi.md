Ōkuninushi (大国主, literally "Great Master of the Land") is one of the central deities of Japanese Shinto mythology, revered as the god of nation-building, agriculture, medicine, magic, and matchmaking (en-musubi).

*[[Ancient Japan]]*

A descendant of the storm god [[Susanoo-no-Mikoto]], Ōkuninushi was originally a humble, kind-hearted younger brother (named Ōnamuji) who carried the heavy luggage for his eighty jealous elder brothers.

While traveling with his brothers to woo a princess, Ōkuninushi stopped to heal a skinned white rabbit that his cruel brothers had tricked. In gratitude, the rabbit prophesied that Ōkuninushi—not his brothers—would win the princess's hand.

Enraged, his brothers murdered him twice, only for his mother to resurrect him. Fleeing to the underworld (_Ne-no-Kuni_), he fell in love with Susanoo's daughter, Suseri-hime, and survived Susanoo's deadly trials (including sleeping in rooms full of snakes, centipedes, and bees, and retrieving an arrow from a burning field with the help of a friendly mouse) before escaping with Susanoo's magical sword, bow, and harp to pacify and rule the earthly realm of Izumo.

Together with the tiny deity [[Sukunabikona]], he shaped the lands of Japan and taught humanity medicine and farming before peacefully ceding political rule to Amaterasu's grandson, becoming the unseen ruler of spirits and matchmaking enshrined at Izumo Taisha (one of Japan's oldest and most sacred shrines). Over time, he also syncretized with Daikokuten, one of the Seven Lucky Gods.
