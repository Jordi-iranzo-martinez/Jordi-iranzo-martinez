Kagutsuchi (カグツチ, also known as Hi-no-Kagutsuchi or Homusubi) is the Japanese Shinto god of fire, volcanoes, and blacksmithing, famous for the tragic myth in which his fiery birth caused the death of his mother, the creator goddess Izanami.

Kagutsuchi was the final child born to the primordial creator couple [[Izanagi]] and [[Izanami]]. Because he was the literal incarnation of fire, he severely burned Izanami as he was born, fatally injuring her and sending her down to **Yomi** (the Japanese underworld).

Overcome with grief and rage at losing his wife, Izanagi drew his ten-span sword (*Ame-no-Ohabari*) and beheaded his newborn son Kagutsuchi, cutting his body into eight pieces.

Even in death, Kagutsuchi's essence sparked new creation:
    
- From the blood dripping off Izanagi's sword sprang eight new deities—including Takemikazuchi (the god of thunder and swords).
- From the eight pieces of Kagutsuchi's body emerged eight mountain and volcano deities (_Yamatsumi_).

Historically, because traditional Japanese cities were built largely of wood and paper, people worshipped Kagutsuchi (especially at Atago Shrine in Kyoto) to appease him and protect their homes from devastating fires, while blacksmiths and ceramic workers honored him as a patron of the forge.
