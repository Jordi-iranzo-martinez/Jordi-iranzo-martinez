A Kitsune (狐, literally "fox") is one of the most famous supernatural fox spirits (yōkai) in Japanese folklore, celebrated for its intelligence, shapeshifting magic, and ability to grow up to nine tails as it ages and gains wisdom.

[[Ancient Japan]]

A kitsune begins with one tail and grows an additional tail every 100 years (or as its spiritual power increases), reaching a maximum of nine tails after a millennium—at which point its fur turns white or gold and it gains near-divine omniscience.

Once a kitsune reaches a certain age (traditionally 50 or 100 years), it can transform into a human—most often a beautiful young woman, an elderly man, or a scholar—though it sometimes struggles to hide its tail or shadow when startled or intoxicated.

Kitsune can generate ghostly blue or orange flames from their tails or breath, weave elaborate illusions, possess people (_kitsunetsuki_), and carry a glowing white pearl (_hoshi no tama_) that holds part of their soul and magic.

Two Main Types:
    
- **Zenko** ("*Good Foxes*"): Benevolent, celestial white foxes that serve as loyal messengers and guardians for Inari Ōkami, the Shinto deity of rice, prosperity, and commerce.

- **Yako** ("*Field Foxes*"): Mischievous, wild, and sometimes malicious tricksters that play pranks on arrogant samurai, greedy merchants, or travelers.
