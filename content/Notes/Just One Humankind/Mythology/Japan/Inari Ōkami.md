Inari Ōkami (稲荷大神) is one of the most popular and widely worshipped Japanese Shinto deities (kami), revered as the god of rice, agriculture, foxes, fertility, industry, and worldly prosperity.

Unlike most gods with a fixed appearance, Inari is depicted in many forms—most commonly as a benevolent young goddess of food, a wise bearded old man carrying sacks of rice, an androgynous bodhisattva, or even a white fox.

Although Inari is not strictly a fox deity, white foxes (*zenko*) are Inari's loyal messengers, guardians, and earthly avatars. Pairs of stone fox statues wearing red bibs (_yodarekake_) guard the entrances of Inari shrines, often holding symbolic items in their mouths: a key (to the rice granary), a jewel (representing Inari's spirit), a **scroll** (wisdom), or a sheaf of rice.

Around one-third of all Shinto shrines in Japan are dedicated to Inari—most famously Fushimi Inari Taisha in Kyoto, renowned for its thousands of vibrant vermilion _torii_ gates donated by merchants and businesses praying for success.

Because rice was historically the measure of wealth in Japan, Inari evolved naturally from an agricultural deity into the patron saint of blacksmiths, merchants, and modern corporations.

*(Inarizushi—sweet fried tofu pouches stuffed with sushi rice—is named after Inari because fried tofu is said to be a favorite food of foxes!)*
