Ahura Mazda (also known as Ohrmazd or Ormuzd) is the supreme creator deity and Lord of Wisdom in [[Zoroastrianism]], one of the world's oldest continuously practiced monotheistic faiths. 

Proclaimed by the ancient Iranian prophet Zoroaster (Zarathustra) as the uncreated, all-knowing, and wholly benevolent God, Ahura Mazda is the source of all light, truth (Asha), and cosmic order.

*In the ancient Avestan language, Ahura means "Lord" (cognate with the Sanskrit Asura) and Mazda means "Wisdom."*

Unlike deities in many ancient pantheons who possessed both constructive and destructive traits, Ahura Mazda is purely benevolent—creating the spiritual and physical worlds through his Holy Spirit (_Spenta Mainyu_) and six divine emanations known as the Amesha Spentas ("Bounteous Immortals").

Zoroastrians pray facing light—most famously sacred fire in Fire Temples—not because they worship fire itself, but because fire and sunlight represent the pure, illuminating wisdom and truth of Ahura Mazda.

 In Zoroastrian theology, Ahura Mazda is opposed by Angra Mainyu (the Destructive Spirit), the embodiment of falsehood (_Druj_), darkness, and chaos.
 
Ahura Mazda grants humans complete free will to choose between truth and deceit, summed up in the core Zoroastrian maxim: "Good Thoughts, Good Words, Good Deeds" (*Humata, Hukhta, Huvarshta*). At the end of time (*Frashokereti*), Ahura Mazda and the forces of good will triumph permanently over evil, renovating the world.

Worship of Ahura Mazda flourished under the Achaemenid Empire (kings like Cyrus the Great and Darius the Great proclaimed his favor in royal inscriptions at Persepolis and Behistun) and later became the official state religion of the Sasanian Empire.

[[Persians]]

The famous winged-disc figure carved at [[Persepolis]]—known today as the Faravahar—was historically associated with Ahura Mazda or royal glory (_khvarenah_) and remains the most recognizable emblem of Zoroastrianism and Iranian heritage.

*The Japanese automaker Mazda took its name directly from Ahura Mazda, chosen by founder Jujiro Matsuda to symbolize wisdom, intelligence, and harmony (while also echoing his own surname).*
