Ægir (anglicized as Aegir, and also known as Hlér or Gymir) is a sea jötunn (giant) in [[Norse mythology]] who personifies the ocean and serves as the legendary host and brewer of ale for the gods.

Unlike most giants, who were bitter enemies of [[Asgard]], Ægir maintained friendly relations with the Æsir gods, throwing lavish banquets in his glowing underwater hall.

He is the son of the primeval giant Fornjót ("Ancient Giant") and brother to Logi (personification of fire) and Kári (personification of wind).

 He is married to the sea goddess Rán, who uses a magical net to drag drowned sailors down to the ocean floor.

Together, Ægir and Rán have nine daughters—Himinglæva, Dúfa, Blóðughadda, Hefring, Uðr, Hrönn, Bylgja, Dröfn, and Kólga—each personifying a different type of ocean wave.

Located near the island of Hléysey (modern Læsø, Denmark), his hall was illuminated not by fire, but by shining gold spread across the floor, and featured self-serving beer steins that refilled themselves automatically.

When Ægir complained he lacked a kettle large enough to brew ale for all the gods at once, [[Thor]] journeyed to the edge of the world with [[Týr]] to steal a mile-deep cauldron from the giant [[Hymir]] so Ægir could host their feast.

• Loki's Flyting (Lokasenna). During one of Ægir's grand autumn banquets, Loki grew jealous of the praise given to Ægir's faithful servants, Fimafeng and Eldir. Loki murdered Fimafeng in the hall and proceeded to hurl vicious, revealing insults at every god and goddess present until Thor arrived with his hammer Mjölnir and drove him out.

Because of his legendary brewing skills, Old Norse skalds (poets) frequently used kennings (poetic metaphors) calling beer and ale "Ægir's drink" and describing gold as "Ægir's fire."
