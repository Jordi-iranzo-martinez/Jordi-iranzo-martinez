Charybdis is a terrifying sea monster in Greek mythology that took the form of a colossal, ship-swallowing whirlpool.

[[Ancient Greeks]]

Born as a giant naiad (sea nymph) and daughter of [[Poseidon]] and [[Gaia]], she flooded lands to expand her father's kingdom until [[Zeus]] punished her by transforming her into a bloated, ravenous sea beast chained to the seabed.

Three times a day, Charybdis swallowed massive amounts of ocean water—creating a giant vortex that dragged entire ships down to the depths—before belching the water back out.

She lived beneath a rock on one side of a narrow channel (traditionally identified as the Strait of Messina between Sicily and mainland Italy), directly opposite Scylla, a six-headed sea monster.

Sailors like [[Odysseus]] in Homer's [[Odyssey]] had to risk losing a few men to [[Scylla]] rather than losing their entire ship to Charybdis.
