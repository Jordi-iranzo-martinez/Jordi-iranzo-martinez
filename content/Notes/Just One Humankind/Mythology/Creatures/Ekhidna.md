Echidna (Ancient Greek: Ἔχιδνα, Ekhidna, meaning "she-viper") is a primordial half-woman, half-serpent monster in Greek mythology known as the "Mother of All Monsters."

[[Ancient Greeks]]

Described by the poet Hesiod as having the upper body of a fair-cheeked nymph and the lower body of a massive, spotted, flesh-eating serpent, she dwelt alone in a deep cave in Cilicia (modern-day southern Turkey) called Arima.

She paired with [[Typhon]], the deadliest storm-giant in Greek myth, and together they gave birth to most of the famous monsters battled by Greek heroes.

Her legendary children include [[Cerberus]] (the three-headed hound of Hades), the Lernaean [[Hydra]], the [[Chimera]], [[Orthrus]] (the two-headed dog), the Nemean Lion, the [[Sphinx]], and [[Ladon]] (the dragon guarding the golden apples).

Unlike most monsters, Echidna was immortal and ageless—Zeus spared her and her children to serve as trials for future heroes—though one late myth claims she was eventually slain in her sleep by the hundred-eyed giant Argus Panoptes.
