A Dullahan (Irish: Dúlachán, meaning "dark man") is a terrifying headless rider from Irish folklore and a harbinger of death—widely considered the original inspiration behind The Legend of Sleepy Hollow's Headless Horseman.

He rides a black, flame-snorting steed (or drives a black carriage called the Coiste Bodhar) while carrying his own severed head under one arm. His head has a pale, rotting complexion, a hideous ear-to-ear grin, and darting eyes that can see across the countryside even in pitch darkness.

Instead of a regular whip, he wields a whip made from a human spine—and according to legend, he uses it to lash out the eyes of anyone foolish enough to spy on him as he passes.

No locked gate or barred door can stop a Dullahan; locks spring open at his approach. Wherever his horse stops riding, a mortal is doomed to die. He can only speak once per ride—to call out the name of the person whose soul he has come to claim.

Despite being virtually unstoppable, the Dullahan has an irrational fear of gold. Dropping even a small gold coin or wearing a gold pin in his path is said to drive him away immediately.
