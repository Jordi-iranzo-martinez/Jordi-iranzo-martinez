The Qilin (Chinese: 麒麟, known as Kirin in Japan and Korea) is a revered, hooved mythological creature in East Asian folklore—often called the "Chinese Unicorn"—that symbolizes good luck, prosperity, wisdom, and justice.

*[[Ancient China]]*

Despite its fiery, dragon-like look, it is a gentle, peaceful creature. It is typically depicted with the head of a dragon, antlers or one or two fleshy horns, the scaled body of a deer or horse, the tail of an ox, and cloven hooves—often wreathed in sacred flames.

According to legend, a Qilin only appears in the world to herald the imminent birth or passing of a wise sage or benevolent ruler (one famously appeared before the birth of Confucius).

Although it can breathe fire and fiercely defend the innocent against evil, a Qilin is so pure and compassionate that it walks upon clouds or water—and when it steps on grass, it avoids crushing even a single blade or insect.

During the Ming Dynasty, when Admiral Zheng He brought live giraffes back from East Africa to China, the imperial court believed they had found real-life Qilin—which is why the Japanese and Korean word for giraffe today is still *kirin* (麒麟).
