Ymir (also called Aurgelmir) is the primordial Frost Giant in Norse mythology—the very first living being in the cosmos, from whose colossal body the gods fashioned the world.

At the dawn of time, the freezing mist of Niflheim met the scorching heat of Muspelheim inside the vast void known as Ginnungagap. As the rime thawed, Ymir took shape from the melting droplets, nourished by the milk of the primeval cow Auðumbla.

While Ymir slept, he sweated—and from his left armpit sprang a male and female giant, while his legs rubbed together to produce a six-headed son, making Ymir the forefather of all Frost Giants (*Jötnar*).

Ymir's descendants eventually produced [[Odin]] and his brothers Vili and Vé. Viewing Ymir as brutal and chaotic, the three brothers slew him. His cascading blood drowned nearly all the other Frost Giants, and the gods used Ymir's corpse to build Midgard (Earth):

- **Flesh**: the land and soil.
- **Blood**: the oceans, lakes, and rivers.
- **Bones**: the mountains
- **Teeth**: rocks and boulders.
- **Skull**: the dome of the sky (held up by four dwarves)
- **Brains**: the clouds
