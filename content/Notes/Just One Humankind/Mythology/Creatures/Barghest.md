The Barghest (also spelled Barguest) is a legendary monstrous black dog from the folklore of Northern [[England]]—especially [[Yorkshire]]—feared as a harbinger of death and misfortune.

Depicted with shaggy black fur, huge fangs, razor-sharp claws, and glowing saucer-like red or fiery eyes, it was said to prowl lonely lanes, churchyards, and narrow city alleys at night.

 Seeing a Barghest clearly was believed to mean imminent death—often within days—while catching only a fleeting glimpse meant a person might live for a few more months. In Leeds, it was said to lie across the threshold of a dying person's home or lead a silent funeral procession of local neighborhood dogs before a prominent resident passed away.

In many accounts, the Barghest could turn invisible, revealing its presence only through heavy footsteps and the eerie clanking of dragging chains.

In counties further north like Durham and Northumberland, the term _Barghest_ also referred to shapeshifting bogeys or mischievous household ghosts (similar to the Cauld Lad of Hylton) capable of appearing as a white cat, a rabbit, or a headless man or woman that vanished in a flash of flame.

Like several creatures in British folklore, it was traditionally believed unable to cross running streams or rivers.

*Scholars debate its exact linguistic origin—proposing roots in Old English burh-ghest ("town-ghost") or German cognates like Bahr-Geist ("spirit of the funeral bier") and Berg-geist ("mountain spirit").*

Famous sightings were tied to Troller's Gill (a limestone gorge in the Yorkshire Dales), the narrow snickelways of York, Whitby (where local black-dog legends helped inspire Bram Stoker's _Dracula_), Leeds, and Darlington.

The Barghest belongs to a wider family of spectral British hounds, alongside Black Shuck (East Anglia), Padfoot (West Yorkshire), Skriker/Trash (Lancashire), Moddey Dhoo (Isle of Man), and Gwyllgi (Wales).
