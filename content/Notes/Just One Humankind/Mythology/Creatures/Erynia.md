An Erinys (plural: Erinyes, also spelled Erynia and known to the Romans as the Furies) is a winged, vengeful underworld deity in Greek mythology who relentlessly pursues and punishes mortals guilty of heinous crimes—especially oath-breaking and murdering family members.

[[Ancient Greeks]]

According to Hesiod, they sprang from the drops of blood spilled onto [[Gaia]] (Earth) when Cronus castrated his father, [[Uranus]] (Sky).

While their exact number varied in early tales, tradition names three primary Erinyes:
    
- **Alecto** ("*Unceasing*"): Punishes moral crimes and anger.
- **Megaera** ("*Jealous*"): Punishes infidelity, envy, and oath-breaking.
- **Tisiphone** ("*Avenger of Murder*"): Punishes blood crimes like patricide and matricide.

They are depicted as fierce, black-winged women with snakes braided into their hair, blood dripping from their eyes, and brass-studded scourges (whips) or burning torches in hand.

They famously tormented [[Orestes]] to the brink of madness after he killed his mother Clytemnestra, until Athena held a trial in Athens and pacified them, granting them the new title of Eumenides ("*The Kindly Ones*").
