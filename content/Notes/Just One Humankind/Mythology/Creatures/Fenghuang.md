The Fenghuang (Chinese: 凤凰, often called the "Chinese Phoenix") is an immortal, benevolent mythological bird in East Asian folklore that reigns over all other birds and symbolizes virtue, grace, peace, and prosperity

Unlike the Greco-Egyptian phoenix that dies in flames and rises from its own ashes, the Fenghuang is not a bird of fire and rebirth—instead, it is a celestial omen that only appears in the mortal world during times of great peace, harmony, and righteous leadership.

 Classical texts describe it as a majestic composite of many birds and creatures: the beak of a rooster, the face of a swallow, the forehead of a fowl, the neck of a snake, the breast of a goose, the back of a tortoise, the hindquarters of a stag, and the tail of a fish or peacock.

Its dazzling plumage displays the five fundamental colors (black, white, red, yellow, and green), representing the five Confucian virtues: benevolence, righteousness, propriety, wisdom, and sincerity.

Originally, *Feng* referred to the male bird and _Huang_ to the female, but over time the Fenghuang became viewed as a single feminine entity paired with the Chinese Dragon (the masculine symbol of the Emperor), making the Fenghuang the traditional emblem of the Empress and marital harmony.
