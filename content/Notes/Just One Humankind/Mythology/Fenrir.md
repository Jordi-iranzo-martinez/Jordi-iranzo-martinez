Fenrir (or _Fenrisúlfr_, "Fenris-wolf") is a monstrous, giant wolf in Norse mythology, fated to break free from his magical chains during [[Ragnarök]] and devour [[Odin]], the king of the gods.

He is the eldest son of the trickster god [[Loki]] and the giantess Angrboða. His siblings are equally infamous monsters: [[Jörmungandr]] (the World Serpent) and [[Hel]] (ruler of the underworld).

Fenrir is also the father of the wolves [[Sköll]] and [[Hati]], who chase the sun and moon across the sky until they swallow them at Ragnarök.

Forewarned that Loki's children would bring destruction to [[Asgard]], the [[Æsir]] gods brought Fenrir to live among them so they could keep watch over him. However, he grew at an alarming rate, and only [[Týr]], the god of war, was brave enough to feed him.

Fearing his strength, the gods challenged Fenrir to test his power against two massive iron fetters—Lœðingr and Drómi—both of which he shattered effortlessly.

The gods commissioned the dwarves to forge Gleipnir, a deceptively thin, silky ribbon crafted from six impossible things (the sound of a cat's footfall, the beard of a woman, the roots of a mountain, the sinews of a bear, the breath of a fish, and the spittle of a bird). Sensing a trick, Fenrir agreed to be bound only if one of the gods placed their hand in his jaws as a pledge of good faith. Týr volunteered, and when Fenrir realized he could not break Gleipnir, he bit off Týr's right hand at the wrist.

The gods anchored Gleipnir to a deep boulder and wedged a sword vertically into Fenrir's open jaws to keep him from biting; his foaming saliva formed the river Ván ("Hope").

When Ragnarök begins, earthquakes shatter the earth, snapping Fenrir's bonds.

He strides into battle at the Plain of Vígríðr with his lower jaw scraping the earth and his upper jaw touching the sky, devouring Odin whole.

Immediately afterward, Odin's son Víðarr steps on Fenrir's lower jaw with a magical shoe made from discarded leather scraps, wrenches his upper jaw upward, and tears the giant wolf apart.
