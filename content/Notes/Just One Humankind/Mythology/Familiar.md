In European folklore and mythology, a familiar (or familiar spirit) is a supernatural entity—most often taking the form of a small animal—believed to assist witches and cunning folk in their magic. 

Rooted in early modern witch trials and older pagan beliefs about household and nature spirits, familiars were thought to serve as companions, spies, protectors, and magical helpers.

While black cats are the most famous image today, historical trial records mention a wide variety of ordinary creatures—including toads, dogs, hares, owls, ravens, ferrets, rats, mice, spiders, and even flies or bees.

In 16th- and 17th-century confessions, familiars were occasionally described with uncanny details, such as a cat with a human face, a horned toad, or a creature that could vanish or change shape at will.

Some traditions viewed familiars as disembodied spirits or lesser demons temporarily inhabiting an animal's body, while others saw them as physical creatures gifted to a witch by the Devil or inherited from an older relative.

The concept evolved from ancient beliefs in personal guardian spirits—such as the Roman *genius*, the Greek *daimon*, and Norse *fylgjur* (animal-shaped guardian spirits tied to a person's fate).

Before the major witch hunts, English and European "cunning folk" (traditional healers and folk magicians) often claimed to work with helpful fairy or ancestral spirits to find lost items, heal illnesses, or break curses.

During the English and Scottish witch trials—especially under Witchfinder General Matthew Hopkins—church authorities demonized familiars as imps or minor devils. Prosecutors searched suspects for a "witch's mark" (an extra mole, wart, or skin tag), claiming it was a teat used to nurse the familiar with the witch's blood.

Similar Concepts Around the World

- **Shikigami** (Japan). Conjured spirits summoned by _onmyōji_ (traditional Japanese occult practitioners) that often take the form of paper cutouts, birds, or small animals to carry out tasks.

- **Nagual / Tonal** (Mesoamerica). Indigenous traditions in Mexico and Central America describing animal spirit counterparts or shapeshifting animal forms linked to an individual's soul.

- **Fylgja** (Norse Mythology). A supernatural spirit—frequently appearing as a wolf, bear, fox, or hawk—that accompanies a person throughout life and reflects their character or destiny.
