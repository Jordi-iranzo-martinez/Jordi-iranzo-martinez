The Terracotta Warriors and Horses (commonly known as the Terracotta Army) is an immense collection of life-sized clay sculptures depicting the armies of Qin Shi Huang, the first Emperor of [[China]].

Buried around 210–209 BCE near Xi'an (Shaanxi Province) to protect the emperor in the afterlife, it is considered one of the greatest archaeological discoveries of the 20th century.

Qin Shi Huang unified China in 221 BCE and commissioned a massive underground mausoleum complex early in his reign. The terracotta army was positioned east of his tomb to guard him against enemies in the spirit world.

Accidental Discovery. The site remained hidden underground for over 2,000 years until March 1974, when local farmers digging a well uncovered fragments of a clay soldier.

Archaeologists estimate that the three main underground pits contain more than 8,000 soldiers, 130 chariots with 520 horses, and 150 cavalry horses, alongside non-military figures like officials, acrobats, and musicians.

- **马** (*mǎ*): caballo.
- **俑** (*yǒng*): figura funeraria.

Although body parts (torsos, arms, legs, and heads) were mass-produced using molds and assembled like an early production line, artisans hand-sculpted each warrior's facial features, hair, and expressions, meaning no two soldiers look exactly alike.

The figures are arranged in precise battle formation and vary in height and uniform according to rank—from towering generals and armored officers to kneeling archers and charioteers. Many originally carried real bronze weapons (swords, spears, and crossbows) treated with chromium oxide to prevent rust.

Originally, every statue was painted in bright, vibrant pigments (red, green, blue, purple, and gold). However, exposure to dry air upon excavation causes the lacquer underneath the paint to curl and flake off within minutes.

The Three Main Pits

1. The largest pit, housing the main infantry force of over 6,000 warriors and horse-drawn chariots arranged in battle columns.
2. A smaller, specialized unit containing cavalry, archers, chariots, and infantry arranged in a complex military formation.
3. The smallest pit, believed to represent the army's command headquarters, containing high-ranking officers and a war chariot.

The Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor and its Terracotta Army were designated a [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Humankind/People/UNESCO]] World Heritage Site in 1987.
