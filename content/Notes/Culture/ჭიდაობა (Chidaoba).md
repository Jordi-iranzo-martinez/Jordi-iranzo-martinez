
ჭიდაობა (Chidaoba) (meaning in Georgian "wrestling") is an ancient, traditional folk wrestling style from Georgia. 

From at least to the 9th century and served as crucial combat training for aristocratic warriors and knights during the Middle Ages.

Unlike modern sports, a Chidaoba match is a festive, theatrical performance deeply bound by a chivalric code of honor.

Competitors wear a specialized uniform called a chokha—a heavy, sleeveless canvas jacket paired with a belt and shorts. Wrestlers typically compete barefoot.

Tournaments traditionally take place outdoors in an open-air sand ring called a _krug_.

It is a fast-paced, highly explosive discipline centered entirely around upper-body throws. Matches typically have a strict 5-minute time limit

Wrestlers use the jacket, lapels, and belt to establish dominant grips

A clean victory (_lasha_) is achieved instantly if a wrestler throws their opponent flat onto their back or shoulders.

Ground fighting, submission holds chokes, joint locks...), and striking are forbidden. Wrestlers are also not allowed to intentionally touch their knees to the ground or turn their back completely to the opponent. Furthermore, traditionally gripping below the waist or directly grabbing the opponent's legs with the hands is restricted.

Chidaoba features over 200 distinct throwing and tripping techniques that have profoundly shaped modern international combat sports

*When the Soviet Union created the hybrid martial art of Sambo in the 1920s, they heavily integrated Chidaoba's highly effective leg trips, sweeps, and unique jacket grips into its core syllabus*

Matches are accompanied by live folk music featuring the **doli** (a Georgian drum) and the zurna (a traditional wind instrument). The musicians alter the tempo and intensity of the music to match the rhythm and drama of the fight.

The sport emphasizes mutual respect. It is customary for the winner to pull the defeated opponent to their feet, and matches frequently conclude with the wrestlers executing a traditional Georgian folk dance together for the crowd. ]

In 2018, [[UNESCO]] recognized Chidaoba by inscribing it onto the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
