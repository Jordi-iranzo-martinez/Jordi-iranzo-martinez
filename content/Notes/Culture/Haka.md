The haka is a traditional Māori ceremonial dance from New Zealand, characterized by strong, rhythmic movements, chanting, stamping feet, and vigorous body slapping, along with facial expressions like the _pūkana_ (bulging eyes) and _whētero_ (tongue protrusion).

A few things worth knowing about it:

- **Origins**: It comes from Māori culture and has deep spiritual and cultural significance, traditionally performed to prepare warriors for battle, welcome distinguished guests, or mark important occasions like funerals and celebrations.

- **Types**: There are different kinds of haka — some are war dances (_peruperu_), others are performed without weapons (_haka taparahi_), and each has its own purpose and meaning.

- **"Ka Mate"**: The most internationally famous haka is "Ka Mate," composed by Māori chief Te Rauparaha in the early 1800s. It's the haka most people recognize from the New Zealand All Blacks rugby team, who perform it before international matches.

- **Modern use**: Today it's performed at weddings, graduations, sporting events, and other ceremonies as an expression of Māori identity, pride, and unity — not just by Māori people, but often collectively at schools and community events across New Zealand.
