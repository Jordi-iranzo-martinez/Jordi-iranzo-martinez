
The Kankurang is a highly revered masked character and an ancient initiation rite practiced by the Mandinka community in The Gambia, Senegal (particularly in the Casamance region and M'bour), and Guinea-Bissau. 

It represents a spiritual protector responsible for maintaining order, dispensing justice, and warding off evil spirits. His appearance is intentionally mysterious and imposing.

The initiated performer covers his entire body with strips of reddish-brown tree bark and fibers extracted from the _faara_ tree (camel's foot tree), topped with long leaves and wild grasses.

During his appearances, he moves through the streets in a rhythmic, erratic dance. He emits piercing cries and brandishes two machetes, clashing them together. This metallic sound alerts the village—especially women and uninitiated children—to stay indoors or keep a respectful distance for their own spiritual safety.

The ritual is deeply tied to male circumcision ceremonies (the transition from boyhood to manhood) and typically takes place between **August and September**.

While isolated in the sacred forest, young boys receive vital lessons from community elders. They learn moral codes, traditional laws, the secrets of medicinal plants, social responsibility, and hunting techniques

He marches alongside elder initiates and villagers who sing traditional call-and-response songs (_djambadon_) accompanied by the fast-paced rhythms of _sabaro_ and other traditional drums.

It was proclaimed a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity by [[UNESCO]] in 2005.
