Box turtles (most famously the North American genus Terrapene, within the pond turtle family Emydidae) are primarily terrestrial turtles known for their high-domed shells and a hinged bottom shell (plastron) that allows them to seal themselves completely inside like a closed box when threatened. 

Native to [[North America]] (with distinct species in the [[United States of America]] and [[Mexico]]), they are closely related to aquatic pond turtles rather than true tortoises.

[[Reptiles]]

Unlike most turtles, a box turtle has a flexible transverse hinge across its lower shell. When startled by a predator, it pulls in its head, tail, and legs and snaps the front and rear lobes of the plastron tightly against the upper shell (_carapace_), leaving virtually no exposed soft tissue.

Their upper shell is rounded and dome-shaped—usually dark brown or black adorned with intricate yellow, orange, or olive-green lines, spots, or radiating starburst patterns that provide effective camouflage among fallen leaves and grasses.

Adults are relatively small, measuring 11 to 20 centimeters (4.5–8 in) in shell length. In many species (especially the eastern box turtle), males can be distinguished by bright red or orange eyes, thicker tails, and a slightly concave lower shell, whereas females typically have brown or yellowish-brown eyes and a flat plastron.

Main North American Species

- **Common box turtle** (_Terrapene carolina_). Divided into several well-known subspecies across eastern North America and Mexico, including:
	- Eastern box turtle (_T. c. carolina_)
	- Three-toed box turtle (_T. c. triunguis_)
	- Florida box turtle (_T. c. bauri_)
	- Gulf Coast box turtle (_T. c. major_).

- **Ornate / Western box turtle** (_Terrapene ornata_). Adapted to open prairies, grasslands, and arid scrublands of the central and southwestern United States and northern Mexico, featuring a flatter dome with crisp yellow radiating lines.

- **Coahuilan box turtle** (_Terrapene coahuila_). An endangered, semi-aquatic species restricted to desert spring wetlands in the Cuatro Ciénegas basin of Coahuila, Mexico.

- **Spotted box turtle** (_Terrapene nelsoni_) & **Yucatan box turtle** (_Terrapene yucatana_). Native to western and southeastern Mexico, respectively.

[[Asia]] is also home to unrelated "Asian box turtles" of the genus **Cuora**, which evolved a similar hinged shell independently.

Wild box turtles spend their entire lives within a remarkably small home range—often just a few acres—and possess a powerful homing instinct; if relocated far from their territory, they will often wander endlessly trying to return, putting them at high risk of road mortality.

Their diet shifts with age and season, including earthworms, slugs, snails, insects, berries, wild mushrooms (even some toxic to humans), leafy greens, and carrion.

Box turtles grow slowly, reach maturity around 7 to 10 years old, and regularly live 40 to 50+ years in the wild (occasionally exceeding 80–100 years). Most species are declining and legally protected due to habitat fragmentation, road deaths, and illegal collection for the pet trade.
