The black widow spider (genus *Latrodectus*) refers to a group of notoriously venomous spiders found across every continent except Antarctica. Best known for the glossy black body and bright red hourglass marking of the adult female, they are among the most recognizable—and misunderstood—spiders in the world.

*[[Arachnids]]*

- **The Iconic Female**. Adult females measure roughly 8 to 13 mm (0.3 to 0.5 inches) in body length (up to 3.8 cm / 1.5 inches including their long legs). They have a shiny, jet-black abdomen with a distinct red or orange hourglass shape on the underside (and occasionally red spots along the back).

- **The Tiny Male**. Males are drastically smaller—often less than half the female's size—and are typically brownish or grey with pale stripes and yellow or red markings rather than solid black. They are completely harmless to humans.

- **The "Widow" Myth**. The spider gets its name from the belief that females always eat their mates after copulation. While sexual cannibalism does occur in some species (most notably the Australian redback and southern black widow) when males cannot escape, in the wild, males often survive and go on to mate with other females.

Black widows belong to the cobweb spider family (_Theridiidae_). They weave messy, irregular, three-dimensional webs made of remarkably strong silk near the ground in dark, sheltered spots—such as woodpiles, rock crevices, sheds, garages, and outdoor meter boxes.

They spend the day hidden in a silk retreat at the edge of their web and emerge at night to wait upside down for prey. When an insect (like a fly, beetle, grasshopper, or caterpillar) gets tangled, the spider wraps it in silk, injects digestive enzymes and venom, and drinks the liquefied insides.

Black widow venom contains latrotoxin, a potent neurotoxin that causes nerve endings to flood the body with neurotransmitters, triggering painful muscle spasms.

Black widows are timid and non-aggressive; they almost never bite unless accidentally pressed against skin (for example, when someone puts on a gardening glove or reaches into a dark woodpile).

A bite may initially feel like a faint pinprick, followed within an hour by localized swelling, severe muscle cramps, abdominal pain, sweating, and nausea (latrodectism). While bites require prompt medical evaluation—especially for young children, older adults, or people with heart conditions—fatalities are extraordinarily rare thanks to modern medical care and antivenom.

Regardless of the country, black widows choose very similar local environments. They heavily favor warm, dark, dry, and undisturbed locations.
