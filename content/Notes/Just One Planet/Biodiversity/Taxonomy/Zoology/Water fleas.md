
Water fleas (order Cladocera, most famously the genus *Daphnia*) are tiny, planktonic freshwater crustaceans—not insects—named for their jerky, hopping swimming motion. 

*[[Crustacea]]*

Measuring roughly 0.2 to 6 mm long, they are a cornerstone of freshwater food webs and one of the most widely studied organisms in biology and environmental science

Their bodies are enclosed in a clear, folded shell (_carapace_) made of chitin. Because it is completely transparent, you can watch their heart beating, their digestive tract working, and developing embryos inside their brood pouch under a simple microscope.

They have a prominent, dark, single cyclopean compound eye that constantly rotates to detect light and movement.

Instead of swimming with legs, water fleas use a large pair of branched second antennae like oars to propel themselves upward in short hops before slowly sinking.

When *Daphnia* detect chemical cues (*kairomones*) released by predators like fish or phantom midge larvae, they can grow protective helmets (pointed head crests) and longer tail spines over a single generation to make themselves harder to swallow.

During favorable spring and summer conditions they reproduce by parthenogenesis (cloned female without fertilization) and populations consist almost entirely of females that reproduce asexually, producing clutches of genetically identical daughters inside a dorsal brood chamber every few days.

When ponds begin to dry up, freeze, or run low on food, females switch to producing males and mating sexually. This creates *ephippia*: tough, dark, saddle-shaped resting egg cases called ephippia that can survive drought, freezing, and even passing through a bird's digestive tract for decades until conditions improve.

Using rhythmic beats of their thoracic legs, water fleas filter massive amounts of algae, bacteria, and yeast from the water, helping keep lakes and ponds clear.

*[[Algae]], [[Bacteria]], [[Yeast]]*, *[[Ponds]]*

They serve as the primary link between microscopic algae and larger animals like fish fry, salamanders, tadpoles, and aquatic insects (and are widely cultured as live or frozen food for aquarium fish).

Because they are highly sensitive to toxins and pollutants—and their heart rate is easy to observe—_Daphnia_ are standard bioindictor: a test organisms used globally to monitor water quality and chemical toxicity.
