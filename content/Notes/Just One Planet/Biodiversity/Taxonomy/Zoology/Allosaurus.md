Allosaurus (*Allosaurus fragilis*) is an extinct genus of large carnivorous theropod dinosaur that lived during the Late [[Jurassic 1]] period, roughly 155 to 145 million years ago. As the most common apex predator of [[North America]]'s famous Morrison Formation, it was the "lion of the Jurassic"—a fast, agile hunter built to take down massive herbivorous dinosaurs.

[[Reptiles]]

[[Dinosaurs]]

The best-known species, Allosaurus fragilis, typically measured 8.5 to 9.7 meters (28 to 32 feet) in length and weighed roughly 1.5 to 2.3 metric tons, with rare giant specimens (such as Allosaurus anax / formerly Saurophaganax debates or large A. fragilis) reaching over 10 meters (33 feet).

Its lightweight, heavily fenestrated skull featured two distinctive, rough **horn-like crests** just above and in front of its eyes, likely covered in keratin and used for visual display or species recognition.

Unlike _Tyrannosaurus rex_, which had tiny two-fingered arms, _Allosaurus_ possessed strong, muscular three-fingered forelimbs tipped with curved, eagle-like claws up to 15 cm (6 inches) long—ideal for grappling and holding onto struggling prey.

Its jaws held dozens of backward-curving, finely serrated teeth that continually shed and regrew throughout its life.

Biomechanical studies show _Allosaurus_ had a relatively weak bite force compared to _T. rex_, but an extraordinarily wide jaw gape (able to open its mouth nearly 80 to 90 degrees) and powerful neck muscles. Paleontologists hypothesize it used its upper jaw like a hatchet or slasher—slamming its open mouth downward into large prey to tear out chunks of flesh and cause massive blood loss.

*Allosaurus* hunted large herbivorous dinosaurs of its ecosystem, including armored [[Stegosaurus stenops]], ornithopods like [[Camptosaurus]], and juvenile or weakened giant sauropods such as [[Diplodocus]], [[Apatosaurus]], and [[Camarasaurus]].

Famous fossils preserve direct evidence of violent Jurassic battles, including an _Allosaurus_ tail vertebra punctured by a _Stegosaurus_ tail spike (_thagomizer_) and a _Stegosaurus_ neck plate with a U-shaped bite mark matching _Allosaurus_ jaws.

The Cleveland-Lloyd Dinosaur Quarry, located in central Utah, this site contains the densest concentration of Jurassic dinosaur bones ever found—over 12,000 bones, roughly 75% of which belong to Allosaurus (representing at least 46 individuals). Paleontologists think this ancient mudhole acted as a "predator trap" that lured in numerous Allosaurus scavengers to feed on trapped herbivores before they too became mired in the mud.
