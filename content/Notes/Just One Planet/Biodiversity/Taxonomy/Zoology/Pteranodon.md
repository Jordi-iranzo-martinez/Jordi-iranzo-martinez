Pteranodon (from Greek *pteron* meaning "wing" and *anodon* meaning "toothless") is a genus of giant pterosaur—not a dinosaur, though it lived alongside them—that inhabited [[North America]] during the Late [[Cretaceous]] period (roughly 86 to 84.5 million years ago).

Famous for its massive wingspan, long toothless beak, and prominent cranial crest, it is the best-known pterosaur in the fossil record, with over 1,200 specimens discovered.

*[[Reptiles]]

*Although often called a "flying dinosaur" in popular media, pterosaurs belong to a separate branch of flying reptiles (Pterosauria) that evolved powered flight independently.*

Adult males of Pteranodon longiceps reached wingspans of 5.6 to over 6 meters (18 to 20+ feet), whereas females were significantly smaller, averaging around 3.5 to 3.8 meters (11.5 to 12.5 feet). Despite their size, hollow, paper-thin bones kept their total body weight remarkably light—likely between 20 and 50 kilograms (45 to 110 pounds).

Males sported a long, backward-pointing bony crest on the back of their skulls, while females had much smaller, rounded crests. Paleontologists believe the crest functioned primarily as a visual display to attract mates and signal dominance, rather than as an aerodynamic rudder.

Unlike earlier pterosaurs that had sharp teeth, Pteranodon had a long, pointed, toothless jaw resembling that of a modern pelican or heron.

Nearly all Pteranodon fossils have been found in the marine chalk deposits of the Western Interior Seaway—a vast, shallow inland sea that once split North America in two (covering modern-day Kansas, Nebraska, Wyoming, South Dakota, and Alabama).

Built with long, narrow wings adapted for dynamic soaring, Pteranodon glided effortlessly over open ocean waters using sea breezes and thermal updrafts, launching into the air from the ground or water using all four limbs (quadrupedal launch).

Fossilized stomach contents—including fish bones and scales found inside preserved skeletons—confirm that Pteranodon fed primarily on fish, swooping down to snatch prey near the ocean's surface.
