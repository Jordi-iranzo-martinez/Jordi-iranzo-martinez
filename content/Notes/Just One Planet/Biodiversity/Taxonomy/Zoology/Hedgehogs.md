Hedgehogs (subfamily Erinaceinae) are small, nocturnal, spiny mammal belonging to the subfamily Erinaceinae. 

*[[Mammalia]]*

Native to parts of [[Europe]], [[Asia]], and [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Wonderful places/Africa]]—and introduced to New Zealand—there are 17 recognized species of hedgehogs, best known for their protective coat of sharp spines and their ability to curl into a tight, defensive ball.

An adult hedgehog is covered in 5,000 to 7,000 hollow, stiff hairs made of keratin. Unlike porcupine quills, hedgehog spines are not barbed, do not detach easily, and are not venomous.

A specialized circular muscle (_orbicularis panniculi_) runs along the edges of the hedgehog's back. When threatened, it contracts this muscle like a drawstring bag, tucking its head, legs, and soft belly completely inside while muscles pull every spine erect in all directions.

Most species measure 14 to 30 cm (5.5 to 12 inches) in length and weigh 400 to 1,200 grams (0.9 to 2.6 lbs). Because they have relatively poor eyesight, they rely heavily on an acute sense of smell and sharp hearing to navigate in the dark.

Hedgehogs sleep during the day in nests made of leaves and grass under hedges, logs, or brush piles, emerging at night to forage. Despite historically being classified as insectivores, they are opportunistic omnivores that eat beetles, caterpillars, earthworms, slugs, snails, spiders, frogs, bird eggs, and fallen fruit.

When encountering an unfamiliar or strong-smelling substance, a hedgehog will often lick or chew it until it produces a frothy saliva, then contort its body to smear the foam across its spines. Scientists believe this behavior may help mask its scent from predators or deter parasites.

In colder climates (such as northern and central Europe), hedgehogs enter true hibernation from autumn until spring, dropping their body temperature and slowing their heart rate from around 190 beats per minute down to roughly 20 beats per minute to survive winter food shortages.

While several species remain widespread, wild European hedgehog populations have declined significantly in recent decades due to habitat fragmentation, road traffic, slug pellets and garden pesticides, and robotic lawnmowers operating at night.
