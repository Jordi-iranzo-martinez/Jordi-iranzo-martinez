
Flagellates are single-celled eukaryotic microorganisms (protists) characterized by having one or more whip-like appendages called flagella used for swimming, sensing their environment, and capturing food.

Rather than representing a single evolutionary family, "flagellates" (historically grouped under the phylum Mastigophora) is a descriptive term spanning diverse lineages of free-living aquatic organisms, photosynthetic algae, symbiotic gut microbes, and major human parasites.

They have long, flexible, microtubule-based structures (arranged in a classic "9+2" internal pattern in eukaryotes). They beat in undulating, wave-like motions to pull or push the cell through water, or act as a trailing rudder.

Many lack a rigid cell wall and are instead enclosed by a flexible, protein-rich membrane called a pellicle, allowing them to change shape slightly as they swim.

Most reproduce primarily by longitudinal binary fission—splitting lengthwise down the middle into two identical daughter cells.

Major Types & Notable Examples

Flagellates are commonly grouped by how they obtain energy and where they live:

- **Photosynthetic & mixotrophic flagellates (Phytoflagellates).** These plant-like protists contain chloroplasts and carry out photosynthesis, forming a vital part of marine and freshwater plankton.
    
    - _**Euglena**_ — A freshwater pond organism with a light-sensitive red eyespot (_stigma_) that swims toward sunlight to photosynthesize, yet can also absorb nutrients in the dark.

    - **Dinoflagellates** — Mostly marine plankton with two perpendicular flagella; famous for causing bioluminescent "sea sparkle" as well as toxic "red tides."

    - **Choanoflagellates** — Collar-bearing marine flagellates recognized as the closest living single-celled relatives of multicellular animals (sponges in particular).

- **Heterotrophic & parasitic flagellates (Zooflagellates).** These organisms feed on bacteria, organic matter, or host tissues.
    
    - _**Giardia lamblia**_ — A tear-drop-shaped intestinal parasite with eight flagella and a suction disk that causes giardiasis (waterborne diarrheal illness).

    - _**Trypanosoma**_ — Bloodborne parasites transmitted by insects, causing African sleeping sickness (via tsetse flies) and Chagas disease (via kissing bugs).

    - _**Trichomonas vaginalis**_ — A common sexually transmitted flagellate parasite.

    - **Termite gut symbionts (e.g., _Trichonympha_)** — Complex, multi-flagellated microbes living inside termite intestines that break down tough wood cellulose into digestible sugars.
