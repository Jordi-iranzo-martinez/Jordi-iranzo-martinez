The bullet ant (*Paraponera clavata*) is a giant, predatory rainforest ant native to  [[Central America]] and [[South America]], famous for possessing the most painful sting of any insect on Earth.

Named because its sting reportedly feels like being shot with a bullet, it ranks at the very top of the Schmidt Sting Pain Index.

Entomologist Justin O. Schmidt rated the bullet ant's sting a 4+ (the highest possible level) on his sting pain index, describing it as "pure, intense, brilliant pain... like walking over flaming charcoal with a three-inch nail embedded in your heel."

Unlike a bee or wasp sting that fades relatively quickly, a bullet ant sting causes waves of throbbing pain, uncontrollable trembling, localized paralysis, and sweating that last for up to 24 hours (which is why locals also call it hormiga veinticuatro, or the "24-hour ant").

Its venom contains a potent neurotoxic peptide called poneratoxin, which blocks synaptic transmission in the central nervous system and locks voltage-gated sodium channels open, causing nerve cells to fire continuously. Despite the agony, the sting is rarely fatal to humans unless a severe allergic reaction occurs.

Workers measure 18 to 30 mm (0.7 to 1.2 inches) long—roughly the size of a large paperclip—and resemble stout, wingless black-and-reddish-brown wasps.

They inhabit humid lowland rainforests from Nicaragua and Honduras down to Paraguay and the Amazon Basin. Colonies typically consist of a few hundred to a couple thousand individuals nesting in the soil right at the base of trees, foraging high up into the forest canopy for nectar, sap, and small arthropods.

Bullet ants are not eager to waste venom on large animals; when threatened, they release a musky warning odor and make an audible stridulation (a chirping or squeaking sound) by rubbing segments of their abdomen together before stinging.

The indigenous Sateré-Mawé people of the Brazilian Amazon incorporate bullet ants into a famous coming-of-age ceremony. Young men place their hands inside woven gloves filled with hundreds of sedated, angry bullet ants for 5 to 10 minutes—enduring dozens of stings—and must repeat the ordeal 20 times over several months to prove their endurance and become warriors.
