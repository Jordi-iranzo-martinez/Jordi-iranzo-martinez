Eared seals (family Otariidae, or otariids) are a family of semiaquatic marine mammals within the pinniped ("fin-footed") group that includes all sea lions and fur seals.

Comprising 15 living species across seven genera (plus the extinct Japanese sea lion), they are named for their small, visible external ear flaps—a trait that sets them apart from "true" earless seals (Phocidae) and walruses (Odobenidae).

[[Mammals|Mammals]]

- **Visible external ear flaps** (*pinnae*). Eared seals have small, rolled outer ear flaps on the sides of their heads, whereas true seals have only tiny ear holes.

- **Walking on all fours**. Eared seals can rotate their large hind flippers forward underneath their bodies, allowing them to prop themselves up, walk, climb rocks, and even run quickly on land. True seals cannot rotate their rear flippers and must bounce or wriggle on their bellies.

- **Front-flipper swimming**. Underwater, eared seals propel themselves using their long, muscular front flippers like underwater wings, reaching bursts of up to 27 km/h (17 mph), while using their hind flippers to steer. (True seals swim by sweeping their hind flippers side-to-side).

- **Social & vocal**. Eared seals are highly social and noisy, gathering in dense, loud colonies and communicating with distinctive barks, roars, and bleats.

Eared seals inhabit coastal and subpolar waters across the Pacific Ocean, Southern Ocean, and southern Atlantic and Indian Oceans—ranging from the subarctic to the tropics (such as the Galápagos)—but are naturally absent from the North Atlantic.

Carnivorous hunters, they feed on fish, squid, octopus, and krill, while serving as prey for orcas, great white sharks, and leopard seals.

Adult males (bulls) are typically 2 to 6 times heavier than females (cows) and often develop thick manes around their necks. During breeding season, bulls establish beach territories and guard harems of females.
