*Cronopio dentiacutus* is an extinct species of small, shrew-like mammal that lived during the Cretaceous period—roughly 95 to 100 million years ago—in what is now Patagonia, Argentina.

*(The name cronopio comes from [[Julio Cortázar]] in his 1962 book Cronopios and Famas)*

Famous for its remarkably long, saber-toothed canines and narrow snout, it gained widespread pop-culture fame upon its description in 2011 for its uncanny resemblance to Scrat, the fictional "saber-toothed squirrel" from the Ice Age films.

*[[Mammalia]]*

Measuring only about 10 to 15 centimeters (4 to 6 inches) in body length with a skull roughly 27 millimeters long, Cronopio was a tiny, nocturnal creature.

It possessed an elongated, narrow snout, a rounded cranium, and extraordinarily long upper and lower canine teeth—a trait virtually unseen in other Mesozoic mammals.

Despite its fierce-looking fangs, _Cronopio_ was not a rodent or a large predator. Its molar structure indicates an insectivorous diet consisting of insects, grubs, worms, and small invertebrates, which it likely hunted at night alongside giant dinosaurs like _Giganotosaurus_ and _Argentinosaurus_.
