The Bald eagle (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*) is a large sea eagle native to [[North America]], ranging from [[Alaska]] and [[Canada]] across the contiguous [[United States of America]] to northern [[Mexico]].

*The name comes from the Old English word balde, meaning "white-headed."*

[[Birds]]

Chosen as the national emblem of the United States in 1782 (and officially designated the U.S. national bird in 2024), it is famous for its snow-white head and tail, fierce yellow beak, and remarkable conservation comeback from the brink of extinction.

Adults have a dark chocolate-brown body and wings contrasted with a pure white head and tail, plus bright yellow eyes, beak, and talons. Juveniles are mostly mottled brown and white, taking 4 to 5 years to develop their iconic adult plumage.

Adult have a wingspan of 1.8 to 2.3 meters (6 to 7.5 feet) and weigh 3 to 6.3 kg (6.5 to 14 lbs). As with most raptors, females are roughly 25% larger than males.

Found near coasts, large lakes, rivers, and reservoirs with tall old-growth trees for nesting, bald eagles feed primarily on fish (70–90% of their diet), swooping down to snatch prey near the water's surface with curved talons and spiny foot pads (spicules).

They also hunt waterfowl and small mammals, scavenge carrion, and frequently harass smaller raptors like ospreys in mid-air to steal their catch.

Bald eagles mate for life and return to the same stick nest year after year, adding new branches each season. They build the largest tree nests of any North American bird—commonly 1.5 to 2.5 meters wide and occasionally weighing over a metric ton.

Despite their intimidating look, a bald eagle's actual voice is a surprisingly high-pitched, chirping whistle.
