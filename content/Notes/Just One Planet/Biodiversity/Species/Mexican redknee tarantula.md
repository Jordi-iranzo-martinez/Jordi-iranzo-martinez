The Mexican redknee tarantula (*Brachypelma hamorii*, formerly grouped with *Brachypelma smithi*) is a striking, docile terrestrial tarantula native to the Pacific coast of [[Mexico]].

Famous for its jet-black body accented with bright orange-red leg joints, it is one of the most recognizable spiders in the world—frequently featured in Hollywood movies and prized as a beginner-friendly pet.

A velvety black body and legs contrasted by vibrant orange-red patches on the "knees" (patellae) and pale pinkish-tan bands lower down the legs.

Adults typically reach a diagonal leg span of 13 to 15 cm (5 to 6 inches), with females being noticeably bulkier than males.

Like many New World terrestrial tarantulas, they grow very slowly. Females can live 25 to 30+ years in captivity, whereas males typically live 5 to 10 years, dying within a year or two of reaching maturity.

Native to southwestern Mexico (states such as Colima, Jalisco, Michoacán, and Guerrero), they inhabit semi-arid deciduous forests and scrublands, spending most of their time in burrows or under rocks and tree roots.

Nocturnal and solitary, they wait near the entrance of their burrow to ambush passing prey—including crickets, beetles, grasshoppers, moths, and occasionally small frogs or lizards.

They are famously calm and slow-moving, rarely biting (their venom is mild, comparable to a bee sting). When threatened, instead of biting, they use their hind legs to flick microscopic, barbed urticating hairs from their abdomen, which irritate a predator's skin and eyes.

*Because wild populations declined due to habitat loss and heavy collection for the exotic pet trade, Brachypelma hamorii is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List and protected under CITES Appendix II—meaning legal pets must be captive-bred rather than wild-caught.*

In captivity, adults thrive in a terrestrial enclosure (around 30×30×20 cm) with deep, dry substrate for burrowing, a hide, a shallow water dish, room temperatures of 22–26°C (72–79°F), and low-to-moderate humidity (50–65%).
