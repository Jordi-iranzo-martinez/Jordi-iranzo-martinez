
The peacock is the male of the peafowl species, a large, brightly colored bird in the pheasant family (_Phasianidae_) famous for its extravagant tail feathers and courtship displays. While "peacock" is commonly used to describe the species as a whole, females are technically called peahens, and young birds are called peachicks.

[[Birds]]

A male's iconic "tail" is actually made up of elongated upper tail coverts rather than true tail feathers. It can make up over 60% of the bird's total body length, reaching up to 1.5 meters (5 feet), and features iridescent eye-spots called _ocelli_.

The vibrant blues, greens, and golds of a peacock's plumage are created by structural coloration—microscopic structures on the feathers that reflect light at different angles rather than relying on pigments.

Peacocks shed their massive train feathers every year after the mating season ends, growing a new, slightly longer set for the following year.

There are three distinct species of peafowl in the world:

- **Indian Peafowl** (*Pavo cristatus*). Native to the Indian subcontinent, this is the most common species, known for its bright blue neck and chest.

	*[[India]]*

- **Green Peafowl** (*Pavo muticus*). Native to Southeast [[Asia]], both males and females of this species have vibrant green and bronze plumage, and they are currently classified as endangered.

- **Congo Peafowl** (*Afropavo congensis*). Native to the [[Congo Basin]] in Africa, this smaller species lacks the long, eye-spotted train seen in its Asian relatives.

During mating season, peacocks fan out their feathers into a wide arch and vibrate them, producing a low-frequency rattling sound to attract peahens.

Despite their heavy, bulky trains, peacocks can fly short distances, usually to escape ground predators or to roost high up in trees at night.

They forage on the ground for seeds, grains, berries, insects, worms, and even small reptiles like lizards and young snakes.
