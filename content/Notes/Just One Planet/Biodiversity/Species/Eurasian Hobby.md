The Eurasian hobby (Falco subbuteo) is a small, fast, and remarkably agile falcon known for its long, scythe-shaped wings and swift-like silhouette. 

Famous for its aerial acrobatics, it catches almost all of its prey—from dragonflies to swallows—in mid-air.

Roughly the size of a kestrel (28–36 cm long with a 70–84 cm wingspan), built with a slimmer body and longer, narrower wings.

- **Adult Plumage**. Slate-grey upperparts, white cheeks and throat framed by a crisp black "moustache" stripe, heavily black-streaked white underparts, and distinctive rusty-red "trousers" (feathers on the thighs and undertail).

- **Juveniles**. Browner above with buff-streaked underparts and pale thighs, lacking the bright rufous leg feathers of adults.

Hobbies are extraordinary flyers capable of outflying even swallows and swifts. 

They feed heavily on large flying insects (especially dragonflies and beetles), which they frequently transfer from their talons to their beak and eat while still flying. During the breeding season, they also hunt small birds (such as martins, swallows, and pipits) and occasionally bats at dusk.

They breed across Europe and Asia in open woodlands, heathlands, farmland, and near wetlands, then migrate south to spend the winter in sub-Saharan Africa, India, and Southeast Asia.

Like other falcons, hobbies do not build their own nests; instead, they take over abandoned tree nests built by crows, magpies, or other raptors, laying 2 to 4 eggs.

*The classic tabletop football game Subbuteo was named after the Eurasian hobby's scientific name (Falco subbuteo) because its creator, Peter Adolph, was an avid ornithologist who originally wanted to name the game "The Hobby".*
