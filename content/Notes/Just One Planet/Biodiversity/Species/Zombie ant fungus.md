
The zombie ant fungus refers to parasitic fungi in the genus Ophiocordyceps—most famously *Ophiocordyceps unilateralis* (and closely related species like *Ophiocordyceps camponoti-floridani*)—that infect carpenter ants, hijack their motor control, and compel them to climb vegetation before killing them to disperse spores.

*It served as the real-life scientific inspiration for the fungal pandemic in the video game and television series The Last of Us.*

Found primarily in tropical rainforests (as well as temperate forests in [[North America]] and [[Asia]]), each _Ophiocordyceps_ species is typically specialized to infect a single species of ant:

*[[Ants]], [[Tropical Rainforest]]*

- **Spore penetration**. Microscopic fungal spores land on a foraging ant's exoskeleton, releasing enzymes that bore through its outer shell and enter its body cavity.

- **Hijacking the body** (not the brain). Once inside, the fungus multiplies into yeast-like cells that form a dense, interconnected network throughout the ant's body—surrounding muscle fibers and the nervous system while leaving the brain physically intact.

- **Chemical manipulation**. Researchers believe the fungus secretes specific bioactive compounds and neurotoxins (such as guanidinobutyric acid and sphingosine) that disrupt the ant's circadian rhythm and trigger erratic convulsions, forcing it to leave its high canopy nest and descend to the humid forest understory.

- **The "death grip."** At roughly 25 centimeters (10 inches) above the forest floor—a microclimate with the ideal temperature and humidity for fungal growth—the ant bites down with extreme force onto the main vein on the underside of a leaf or twig. The fungus causes the ant's jaw muscles to atrophy and lock permanently in place.

After the ant dies, the fungus consumes its internal tissues and sprouts a long, stalk-like stroma (fruiting body) directly out of the back of the ant's head. Within a couple of weeks, this stalk releases spores onto the forest floor below, creating a "killing zone" that infects passing ants.

Carpenter ants have evolved sophisticated defenses against the parasite: healthy workers recognize early behavioral changes in infected nestmates, carry them far away from the colony to die in isolation, and wall off infected nest chambers. Additionally, hyperparasitic fungi often attack Ophiocordyceps itself, preventing it from wiping out entire ant colonies.

*Ophiocordyceps fungi are hyper-specialized to insect physiology and cannot survive at human body temperature (around 37 °C / 98.6 °F)—they thrive in cool, humid forest conditions around 20 to 25 °C.*

*(It is distinct from Cordyceps militaris and Ophiocordyceps sinensis, the caterpillar fungi harvested in traditional Tibetan and Chinese medicine.)*
