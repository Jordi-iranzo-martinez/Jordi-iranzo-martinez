European bison (*Bison bonasus*), also known as the wisent or żubr, is the heaviest surviving wild land mammal in Europe and one of conservation's greatest comeback stories.

[[Mammalia|Mammalia]], [[Europe]]

Adult males (bulls) typically weigh between 600 and 1,000 kg (1,300–2,200 lbs) and stand nearly 2 meters (6.5 feet) tall at the shoulder.

Compared to their American cousins, European bison are slightly taller with longer legs, a shorter mane, less woolly hair on their heads, and horns that curve forward (making them better suited for browsing in forests rather than solely grazing on open plains).

Unlike American plains bison, wisents thrive in deciduous and mixed forests. By stripping bark, knocking down small trees, and grazing on grasses, shrubs, and acorns, they open up forest canopies and boost local biodiversity.

Centuries of hunting and habitat loss wiped out every wild European bison by 1927.

Every living European bison today descends from just 54 individuals kept in European zoos and private parks (tracing back to only 12 genetic founders).

Thanks to intensive breeding and reintroduction programs—most famously in Białowieża Forest (Poland and Belarus), as well as reserves in Romania, Germany, the UK, and semi-wild reserves in Spain (such as in Palencia, Burgos, and Andalucía)—the global population has rebounded to over 10,000 individuals, improving their conservation status from _Vulnerable_ to Near Threatened.
