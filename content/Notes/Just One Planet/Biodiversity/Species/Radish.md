The radish (*Raphanus sativus*) is an edible root vegetable belonging to the mustard and cabbage family (Brassicaceae).

*[[Plants]]*

Domesticated in [[Asia]] thousands of years ago and now cultivated worldwide, radishes are prized for their crisp texture and sharp, peppery bite—which comes from natural sulfur compounds (glucosinolates and isothiocyanates) shared with horseradish and mustard.

Popular Varieties

- **Red globe radishes** (e.g., Cherry Belle). The classic small, round spring radish with bright crimson skin and crisp white flesh, known for its juicy crunch and mildly spicy flavor.

- **French breakfast radishes**. Elongated, cylindrical roots with scarlet tops and white tips, offering a slightly milder, sweeter flavor than round red varieties.

- **Daikon** (white radish). A large, long white Asian radish (often growing 20–35 cm long) that is milder, sweeter, and juicier than European varieties. Widely used in Japanese, Korean, and Chinese cooking—raw, pickled, or simmered in soups.

- **Watermelon radish**. An heirloom Chinese daikon variety with pale green-and-white skin and a striking magenta-pink interior, combining a mild sweetness with a subtle peppery finish.

- **Black Spanish radish**. A large winter variety with rough, matte black skin and dense, pungent white flesh.

Most commonly sliced raw into salads, tacos, and sandwiches—or served whole the classic French way with salted butter and flaky sea salt. Quick-pickling softens their sharpness, while roasting or sautéing mellows the peppery bite into a tender, slightly sweet side dish.

Spring radishes are famous among gardeners for their rapid growth, often maturing from seed to harvest in just 21 to 30 days.

The leafy green tops are completely edible and nutritious, working well sautéed with garlic, blended into pesto, or tossed into soups.
