The Pangolin (*Manis javanica*)

A pangolin (order Pholidota, family Manidae) is the only mammal in the world wholly covered in protective scales.

Often nicknamed "scaly anteaters" (though they are actually more closely related to carnivores such as dogs, cats, and bears than to true anteaters), pangolins inhabit tropical forests, savannas, and grasslands across sub-Saharan Africa and South and Southeast Asia.

From head to tail tip, a pangolin's body is shingled with overlapping plates made of keratin—the exact same protein found in human fingernails and rhinoceros horns—which make up roughly 20% of its body weight.

The word _pangolin_ comes from the Malay _pengguling_, meaning "one who rolls up." When threatened by predators like lions, tigers, or leopards, it curls tightly into an armored sphere that even big cats cannot pry open, while lashing out with its sharp-edged tail scales and releasing a foul-smelling musk from anal glands.

Pangolins have no teeth and cannot chew. Instead, they use powerful curved foreclaws to tear open termite mounds and ant hills, extending an extraordinarily sticky tongue—which can grow longer than their entire body and anchors deep inside their chest cavity near the pelvis—to lap up tens of thousands of insects a day. Their stomachs contain muscular, keratin-lined ridges and swallowed pebbles that grind insects like a bird's gizzard.

Ground-dwelling species walk primarily on their hind legs, using their thick tail for balance while holding their large front claws curled inward to protect them from wearing down.

There are eight recognized living species (with genetic research also proposing a ninth cryptic Asian species, *Manis mysteria*):

- **African Species**: 
	- Giant pangolin (*Smutsia gigantea*)
	- Ground pangolin (*Smutsia temminckii*)
	- White-bellied pangolin (*Phataginus tricuspis*)
	- Black-bellied pangolin (*Phataginus tetradactyla*)

- **Asian Species:** 
	- Indian pangolin (*Manis crassicaudata*)
	- Chinese pangolin (*Manis pentadactyla*)
	- Sunda pangolin (*Manis javanica*)
	- Philippine pangolin (*Manis culionensis*).

Tragically, the pangolin holds the title of the most heavily trafficked wild mammal on Earth. More than a million individuals have been poached from the wild over the last decade

Although curling into a ball protects pangolins from wild predators, it makes them tragically easy for poachers to pick up and bag. All eight species are listed as Vulnerable, Endangered, or Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List and are protected under Appendix I's [[CITES Convention]] (banning all international commercial trade).

Millions of pangolins have been poached over recent decades, driven primarily by illicit demand for their scales in traditional Asian medicine (despite having zero proven medicinal properties) and for their meat, which is considered a luxury delicacy in parts of Asia and a source of bushmeat in Africa.

A single adult pangolin consumes up to 70 million ants and termites per year, acting as a vital natural pest controller and soil aerator in forest and grassland ecosystems.
