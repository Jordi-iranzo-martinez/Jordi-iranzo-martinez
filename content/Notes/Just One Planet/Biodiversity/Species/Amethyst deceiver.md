The amethyst deceiver (*Laccaria amethystina*) is a small, vibrantly purple edible woodland mushroom found throughout temperate forests of [[Europe]], [[Asia]], [[North America]] and [[South America]]. 

*It gets the name "deceiver" because its striking amethyst-violet color fades dramatically to pale lilac, grey, or tan as it dries out and ages.*

[[Fungi]]

Identification Features:

- **Cap**. Measuring 1.5 to 6 centimeters (0.6–2.4 in) across, the cap starts convex with an inward-rolled margin before flattening out and developing a slight central depression and wavy edges. It is hygrophanous, meaning it appears deep violet when damp and fades to pale buff or off-white in dry weather.

- **Gills**. Thick, waxy, and widely spaced, the gills match the deep purple hue of the cap and are attached squarely to the stem (adnate to slightly decurrent).

- **Stem**. Slender, fibrous, and often twisted, growing 4 to 10 centimeters (1.6–4 in) tall. It is the same purple color as the cap, lacks a ring, and typically has white cottony mycelium at its base.

- **Spore print**. White to very pale lilac.

*Laccaria amethystina* forms mutually beneficial ectomycorrhizal relationships with the roots of both deciduous and coniferous trees—showing a particularly strong preference for beech and oak woodlands.

Fruiting bodies typically emerge from late summer through late autumn (August to November in the Northern Hemisphere), growing scattered or in small clusters among fallen leaves and moss.

While small and delicate, the caps are edible with a mild, pleasant, woodsy flavor (the tough, fibrous stems are usually discarded).

Faded, older specimens can easily be mistaken for dangerously poisonous purple mushrooms—most notably the lilac fibrecap (Inocybe lilacina, which contains lethal muscarine and has brownish-grey gills and a brown spore print) and the lilac bonnet (Mycena pura, which smells strongly of radishes).

Amethyst deceivers naturally absorb and concentrate arsenic from the soil, so foragers avoid harvesting them near industrial sites, mines, or treated land.
