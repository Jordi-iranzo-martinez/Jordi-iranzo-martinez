
The Siberian flying squirrel (*Pteromys volans*) is a small, nocturnal, tree-dwelling rodent native to the boreal forests of northern Eurasia.

*[[Europe]], [[Asia]], [[Mammalia]]*

It is the only species of flying squirrel native to Europe, famous for its large black eyes, soft gray-white fur, and ability to glide long distances between trees.

Adults measure about 13 to 20 centimeters (5 to 8 inches) in body length, with a flat, bushy tail adding another 9 to 14 centimeters (3.5 to 5.5 inches), and weigh only 95 to 170 grams (3.3 to 6 ounces).

Rather than true powered flight, it glides using a furry skin membrane stretching between its front wrists and hind ankles.

It has remarkably large, dark eyes specialized for low-light vision and thick, pale silvery-gray fur that turns slightly lighter in winter for camouflage.

By leaping from high branches and stretching out its limbs, it can glide over 100 meters (330 feet) across forest clearings, using its flattened tail as a rudder to steer and brake before landing vertically on a tree trunk.

It spends almost its entire life in the forest canopy, emerging at dusk to forage and sleeping during the day inside abandoned woodpecker holes, natural tree cavities, or birdhouses.

It does not hibernate, though it may sleep for several days straight during extreme cold snaps and relies on food caches (such as alder catkins) stored in tree hollows during autumn.

Its range stretches from Finland and Estonia across Russia and Siberia all the way to the Pacific coast, northeastern China, Korea, and the Japanese island of Hokkaido.

It feeds mainly on leaves, needles, buds, shoots, seeds, berries, and nuts—with birch and alder catkins forming a staple of its winter diet.

While listed as Near Threatened globally, populations in the European Union (Finland and Estonia) are strictly protected because intensive logging and fragmentation of old-growth spruce and birch forests threaten their nesting cavities.
