Homo floresiensis (popularly known as the "Flores Man" or the "hobbit") is an extinct species of small-bodied hominin that lived on the island of Flores, [[Indonesia]], until about 50,000 years ago.

Discovered in 2003 in Liang Bua cave, it transformed the study of human evolution by proving that another archaic human species survived alongside Homo sapiens.

Adults stood only about 1 meter (3.3 feet) tall and weighed roughly 25 kilograms (55 pounds).

Its cranial capacity was around 380 to 420 cm³ (comparable to a chimpanzee or *Australopithecus*, and roughly one-third the size of a modern human brain). Despite this, scans of its prefrontal cortex suggest it possessed advanced cognitive abilities.

It had relatively long arms, short legs, and disproportionately large, flat feet compared to its lower limbs.

The leading hypothesis suggests _Homo floresiensis_ evolved from an Asian population of _[[Homo erectus]]_ that became isolated on Flores over a million years ago. Faced with limited food resources and few large terrestrial predators, the species shrank over generations—an evolutionary process known as island dwarfism.

Fossil discoveries at the Mata Menge site on Flores, dated to about 700,000 years ago, revealed individuals even smaller than those at Liang Bua, showing that body-size reduction happened very rapidly after their ancestors arrived on the island.

Despite their small brains, they crafted stone tools to butcher meat. They shared the island with unusual fauna, including pygmy elephants (_Stegodon_), giant rats, and Komodo dragons, which they hunted or scavenged.

The youngest skeletal remains and stone tools date to roughly 50,000 years ago. Their extinction coincides closely with the arrival of modern _Homo sapiens_ in Southeast Asia and Australia, suggesting that resource competition (potentially combined with volcanic eruptions or environmental shifts) led to their demise.
