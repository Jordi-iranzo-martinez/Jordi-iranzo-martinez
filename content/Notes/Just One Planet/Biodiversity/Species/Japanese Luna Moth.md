The Japanese luna moth (*Actias artemis*) is a large, striking silk moth native to East Asia. Closely related to the [[North America]] luna moth (*Actias luna*), it is prized by naturalists for its delicate pale-green wings, long sweeping tails, and nocturnal habits.

[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Taxonomy/Zoology/Insects|Insects]]

Adults feature translucent, pale celadon-green or bluish-green wings spanning 8 to 12 cm (3.1 to 4.7 inches). Each wing displays a prominent, crescent-shaped eyespot ringed in yellow, black, and reddish-brown, while the leading edge of the forewings is bordered by a velvety maroon-brown band.

Its hindwings taper into long, graceful tails that twist slightly in flight. Like other moon moths, these tails serve an acoustic defense role by deflecting the echolocation calls of hunting bats away from the moth's vital body.

Adult males possess large, broad, bipectinate (feather-like) antennae packed with sensitive chemoreceptors capable of detecting female pheromones from miles away.

It inhabits deciduous and mixed forests across [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Humankind/Just One World/Japan]] (where it is known as _Ō-mizuao_), the Korean Peninsula, eastern [[China]], and the Russian Far East ([[Russia]])

The plump, bright-green larvae feed voraciously on the leaves of a wide variety of broadleaf trees, including maple, alder, birch, willow, oak, cherry, and walnut.

Like all giant silk moths (family Saturniidae), adult Japanese luna moths emerge from their cocoons without functional mouthparts or a digestive system.

Surviving entirely on fat reserves stored during their caterpillar stage, adults live for only about one week, dedicating all their energy to mating and laying eggs.

Upon emerging from its silk cocoon, the adult moth possesses no mouthparts and no digestive system. It cannot eat, drink, or sip nectar.
