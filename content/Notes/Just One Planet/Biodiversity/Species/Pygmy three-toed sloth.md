
The pygmy three-toed sloth (*Bradypus pygmaeus*), also known as the monk sloth or dwarf sloth, is the smallest and rarest of all living sloth species.

Endemic to a single tiny island off the [[Caribbean]] coast of Panama, it is an example of insular dwarfism and is listed as Critically Endangered by the [[IUCN]] and protected under Appendix II on the [[CITES Convention]].

It evolved after rising sea levels separated Isla Escudo de Veraguas from mainland Panama roughly 8,900 years ago. Measuring just 48 to 53 cm (19–21 in) in length and weighing 2.5 to 3.5 kg (5.5–7.7 lb), it is roughly 40% smaller in body mass than its closest mainland relative, the brown-throated three-toed sloth (_Bradypus variegatus_).

It has a pale gray-brown face framed by a dark band across the forehead and eyes—giving the appearance of a monk's hood—along with long, shaggy fur that often takes on a greenish tint due to symbiotic algae growing in the damp hair shafts, providing camouflage. Males feature a bright orange-yellow dorsal patch with a dark central stripe.

The species is found only on Isla Escudo de Veraguas (an uninhabited, 4.3 km² island in the Bocas del Toro archipelago), where it lives almost exclusively within coastal red mangrove (*Rhizophora mangle*) forests.

*[[Mangrove Forests]]*

Adapted to a low-energy diet consisting almost entirely of tough red mangrove leaves, which take weeks to digest in its multi-chambered stomach.

Although extremely slow-moving in the canopy, pygmy three-toed sloths are proficient swimmers, frequently dropping into tidal waters to swim between mangrove patches.
