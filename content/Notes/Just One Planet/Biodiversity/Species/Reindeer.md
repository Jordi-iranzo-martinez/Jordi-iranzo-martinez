The reindeer (*Rangifer tarandus*), known as caribou in North America, is a species of [[Arctic]] and subarctic deer uniquely adapted to survive extreme cold, snow, and long-distance migrations across Northern [[Europe]], [[Siberia]], and [[North America]].

Reindeer are the only deer species in which both sexes grow antlers. Males shed theirs in late autumn after the mating season, while pregnant females keep theirs throughout winter to defend prime feeding spots in the snow (which is why Santa's sleigh team—depicted with antlers in December—would technically all be female!).

Their eye structure (_tapetum lucidum_) shifts from golden-yellow in summer (reflecting endless Arctic daylight) to deep blue in winter, increasing sensitivity to dim light and ultraviolet rays so they can spot white predators and lichen against the snow.

Their large, crescent-shaped hooves spread wide to act like snowshoes on soft snow and harden in winter to dig through ice for reindeer moss (lichen). Tendons snapping over foot bones also make a distinct clicking sound when they walk, helping herds stay together in blizzards.

Wild herds (especially barren-ground caribou) travel up to 5,000 km (3,000 miles) a year—the longest terrestrial migration of any mammal—while semi-domesticated reindeer have been herded for centuries by Indigenous Arctic peoples like the Sámi of Lapland.
