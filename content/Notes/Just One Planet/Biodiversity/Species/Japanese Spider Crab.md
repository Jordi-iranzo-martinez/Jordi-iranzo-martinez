
The Japanese spider crab (*Macrocheira kaempferi*) is a deep-sea marine crab native to the waters around Japan, famous for having the largest leg span of any known arthropod. 

Known in Japanese as taka-ashi-gani (literally "tall-legged crab"), it is considered a "living fossil" because it is the sole surviving member of the ancient genus Macrocheira.

Adults can reach a leg span of up to 3.7 to 3.8 meters (over 12 feet) from claw to claw, while their round, spiky carapace (body shell) grows up to 40 centimeters (16 inches) wide.

Despite their towering, spindly legs, they are relatively lightweight for their size, weighing up to 19 kilograms (42 pounds). Scientists estimate they can live up to 100 years in the wild.

Their bumpy, orange-and-white exoskeletons blend into the rocky ocean floor, and younger crabs often decorate their shells with sponges, anemones, and kelp to hide from predators.

They inhabit the [[Pacific Ocean]] side of the Japanese archipelago (especially around Honshu Island), living on sandy and rocky seabeds at depths between 50 and 600 meters (160 to 2,000 feet). During spring spawning season, they migrate to shallower waters (around 50 meters) to lay eggs.

Despite their intimidating size and name, Japanese spider crabs are slow-moving, docile omnivores. They crawl along the seafloor foraging for dead animals, shellfish, algae, and small invertebrates.

They are harvested on a small scale in Japan and considered a delicacy, though fishing is strictly regulated during their spring breeding season to protect populations.
