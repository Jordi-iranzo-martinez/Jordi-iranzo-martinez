
A wormlion is the predatory larva of a slender, long-legged true fly in the family Vermileonidae (order Diptera). 

*[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Taxonomy/Zoology/Insects|Insects]]*

Wormlion larvae dig conical pit traps in fine sand or dust to ambush ants and other small crawling insects—independently evolving the exact same hunting strategy as the unrelated antlion (a lacewing larva).

Spending most of their lives (typically one to two years) in the larval stage, wormlions thrive in sheltered, dry, dusty microhabitats such as rock overhangs, caves, tree hollows, and the dry soil beneath buildings.

- **Building the pit trap**. Unlike antlions, which walk backward in spirals and flick sand with their flat heads, a legless wormlion larva curls its front end into a "slingshot" and flings sand grains out of the pit with rapid flicks of its body.

- **Ambush and "cobra" strike**. Once the funnel-shaped pit is dug, the wormlion lies buried beneath the bottom center, anchored by specialized hooks on its rear segments. When an ant slips down the loose sand walls, the larva lashes upward like a cobra, wraps its body around the prey to constrict it, and injects paralyzing venom.

- **Feeding and cleanup**. After liquefying and sucking out the prey's internal fluids, the wormlion uses its body like a catapult to fling the empty exoskeleton out of the crater before repairing the sand trap.

After pupating in the sand, the wormlion emerges as a delicate, mosquito- or wasp-like adult fly. Adults live for only a few days—some feed lightly on flower nectar while others do not feed at all—focusing entirely on mating and laying eggs in dry soil.

Fewer than 80 species are known worldwide across roughly 11 genera (such as _Vermileo_ and _Lampromyia_). 

They are primarily found in arid and [[Mediterranean]] climates—including the Iberian Peninsula, southern [[Europe]], North and Southern [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Wonderful places/Africa]], western [[North America]], and parts of [[Asia]] (where a giant new Himalayan species was described in 2026)
