
Termites (infraorder Isoptera) are a group of eusocial insects that feed primarily on wood and other cellulose-rich materials.

*[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Taxonomy/Zoology/Insects|Insects]]*

*Although they are sometimes called "white ants," they are not ants at all and are actually closely related to cockroaches.*

Termites live in highly organized colonies ranging from a few thousand to several million individuals, divided into distinct castes with specialized roles:

- **King and Queen**. They are the primary reproductive pair of the colony. A mature queen can live for decades and lay thousands of eggs per day to grow the population.

- **Workers**. Making up the vast majority of the colony, workers are pale, blind, and wingless. They forage for food, dig tunnels, build and repair the nest, and feed all other castes.

- **Soldiers**. Soldiers have enlarged, darkened, armored heads equipped with powerful jaws or chemical-spraying nozzles. Their sole job is to defend the colony from predators, especially ants.

- **Alates** (Swarmers). These are winged reproductive termites that leave the nest in large swarms during specific seasons to mate and establish new colonies.

Termites eat dead wood, leaf litter, roots, paper, and cardboard. To break down tough plant fibers, they rely on symbiotic microorganisms (protozoa and bacteria) living inside their gut.

In natural ecosystems, termites play a vital role by decomposing dead wood and returning essential nutrients to the soil. Their underground tunneling also aerates the ground and improves water drainage.

Subterranean Termites build their nests underground and travel up into buildings through protective mud tubes to maintain moisture. They cause the most severe structural damage to homes.

Drywood Termites live directly inside the wood they consume (such as furniture, framing, and floorboards) and require no contact with soil.
