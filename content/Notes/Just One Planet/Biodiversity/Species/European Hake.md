The European hake (*Merluccius merluccius*) is a slender, predatory marine fish belonging to the cod order (Gadiformes). 

[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Taxonomy/Zoology/Fishes|Fishes]]

Highly prized for its mild, flaky white flesh, it is one of the most commercially important and widely consumed food fish across Western Europe.

It has an elongated, torpedo-shaped body with a steel-grey or brownish back, silvery flanks, and a white belly.

Its large head features a wide mouth lined with sharp, curved teeth, along with a distinctive black lining inside the mouth and gill cavities. Unlike true cod, it lacks a chin barbel.

Adults commonly measure between 30 and 70 cm (12 to 28 inches) in length, though large individuals can reach up to 1.4 meters (4.6 feet) and weigh over 15 kg (33 lbs).

The European hake inhabits the northeastern [[Atlantic Ocean]]—from [[Norway]] and [[Iceland]] down to Mauritania—as well as the entire [[Mediterranean Sea]] and the southern [[Black Sea]].

Adults typically live near sandy or muddy seafloors on the continental shelf and slope at depths of 70 to 400 meters (230 to 1,300 feet), though they can venture deeper than 1,000 meters.

During the day, hake stay close to the seabed. At night, they swim up into the water column to hunt.

Juveniles feed mainly on crustaceans like krill and shrimp, while adults prey on schooling fish such as anchovies, sardines, herring, mackerel, and smaller hake.

*Historically subjected to intense overfishing, northern Atlantic stocks of European hake have recovered significantly over the past decade due to strict European Union catch quotas and gear regulations, though Mediterranean populations remain under heavier fishing pressure.*
