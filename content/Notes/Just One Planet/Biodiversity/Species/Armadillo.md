An armadillo (Spanish for "little armored one") is a placental mammal native to the Americas (order Cingulata), famous for its leathery, bony shell made of overlapping armor plates.

Their shell (_carapace_) is made of genuine bone plates (_osteoderms_) covered by tough keratin scales, protecting their back, head, legs, and tail.

Despite popular belief, only the three-banded armadillos (_Tolypeutes_) can curl completely into a sealed, impenetrable sphere. Most other species—like the nine-banded armadillo—escape predators by sprinting away, jumping straight up into the air when startled, or rapidly digging a burrow.

Armadillos are heavy, yet they can cross rivers either by holding their breath for up to 6 minutes to walk along the riverbed, or by gulping air into their stomachs to inflate themselves and float!

The nine-banded armadillo (_Dasypus novemcinctus_)—the only species found in the United States—always gives birth to four genetically identical quadruplets from a single fertilized egg.

There are roughly 21 living species, ranging from the tiny pink fairy armadillo (_Chlamyphorus truncatus_, about 13 cm / 5 inches long) to the giant armadillo (_Priodontes maximus_, up to 1.5 meters / 5 feet long and 54 kg / 120 lbs).
