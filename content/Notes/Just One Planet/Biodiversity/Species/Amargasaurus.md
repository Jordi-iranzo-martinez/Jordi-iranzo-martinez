*Amargasaurus cazaui* is a genus of herbivorous sauropod dinosaur that lived during the Early [[Cretaceous]] period (about 130 to 122 million years ago) in what is now [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Humankind/Places/Patagonia]] ([[Argentina]]).

*[[Reptiles]], [[Dinosaurs]]*

It is immediately recognizable by two parallel rows of extremely tall, bifurcated neural spines rising from its neck and back vertebrae—reaching up to 60 centimeters (2 feet) in height. Paleontologists debate whether these spines supported a skin sail, were encased in keratin horns, or were covered by soft tissue.

As a member of the dicraeosaurid family (related to _Diplodocus_), it was relatively small and short-necked for a sauropod, measuring roughly 9 to 13 meters (30 to 43 feet) long and weighing around 2.6 to 4 metric tons.

The only known, nearly complete skeleton was discovered in February 1984 in the [La Amarga Formation](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESiAEBxCJF11dGWCe2ijGFTxnFlxeKWwvDXFusylmHYZmbUBq2U1q70D1IpS1rIkn_uIJe2JLjbgft71Xka_6zNn4YmkWcctfjYBeIoNrza0S0pyo4owuiZUxCiDP9Ctr3s5rlYuqJPBJXqUDKYmuMN3O_rY9C_5F55lToI-c2avTv3VkOisUyjG5w) (Neuquén Province, Argentina) by Guillermo Rougier during an expedition led by José Bonaparte. It was formally named in 1991 after the discovery site and geologist Luis Cazau.

Its shorter neck and downward-sloping snout suggest it browsed on low- to mid-level vegetation such as ferns, cycads, and conifers rather than high treetops.
