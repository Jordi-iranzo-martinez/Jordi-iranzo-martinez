The Largemouth Bass (*Micropterus nigricans*) is a carnivorous freshwater fish in the sunfish family (Centrarchidae). Native to eastern and central [[North America]], it is the most popular freshwater game fish in the world and has been widely introduced across Europe (where it is commonly known as black bass or perca americana), Asia, Africa, and South America.

[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Taxonomy/Zoology/Fishes|Fishes]]

Its defining feature is an oversized mouth whose upper jaw (_maxilla_) extends clearly behind the rear edge of the eye when closed—distinguishing it from the smallmouth bass (_Micropterus dolomieu_), whose jaw stops directly below the eye.

It features an olive-green to dark-green back, silvery-green flanks with a prominent dark horizontal stripe running from the gill cover to the tail, and a pale white or yellowish belly.

Adults typically measure 40 to 60 centimeters (16–24 in) and weigh 1 to 5 kg (2–11 lbs), though trophy individuals in warm climates can exceed 10 kg (22 lbs). The official IGFA all-tackle world record stands at 10.12 kg (22 lb 4 oz), tied between George Perry's 1932 catch in Georgia and Manabu Kurita's 2009 catch in Lake Biwa, Japan.

Largemouth bass favor calm, warm, vegetated freshwater habitats with plenty of cover—such as lakes, ponds, reservoirs, marshes, and slow-moving rivers containing submerged weeds, lily pads, sunken logs, and rocky drop-offs.

Young bass feed on zooplankton and aquatic insects, while adults are aggressive apex ambush predators that consume smaller fish (such as bluegill and shad), crayfish, frogs, large insects, and occasionally small birds or rodents.

In spring when water temperatures reach 15–20 °C (60–68 °F), males fan out circular nests in shallow gravel or sand. After the female lays thousands of eggs, the male fiercely guards both the eggs and newly hatched fry against predators until the young disperse.

Valued for its explosive strikes and acrobatic leaps when hooked, the largemouth bass supports a massive recreational fishing industry.

Outside its native North American range—including in Spain and across the Mediterranean basin—it is classified as an invasive alien species because its voracious predation threatens native freshwater fish and amphibians.
