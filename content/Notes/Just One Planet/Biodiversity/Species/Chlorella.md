*Chlorella* is a genus of single-celled, spherical green microalgae belonging to the division Chlorophyta. 

*[[Algae]]*

Living primarily in freshwater environments, these microscopic organisms are renowned for their high photosynthetic efficiency, rapid reproduction, and dense nutritional profile, making them widely studied for both space travel and dietary supplements.

Individual Chlorella cells are tiny spheres measuring roughly 2 to 10 micrometers in diameter—comparable in size to a human red blood cell—and lack flagella (meaning they cannot swim actively).

Each cell contains a single large, cup-shaped chloroplast packed with chlorophyll-a and chlorophyll-b (the same photosynthetic pigments found in higher plants), giving Chlorella its deep emerald-green color and high photosynthetic rate.

Chlorella has a rigid, multi-layered cell wall composed of cellulose and sporopollenin-like polymers. Because humans cannot digest this tough outer wall, commercial Chlorella supplements undergo mechanical "cell-wall cracking" so the body can absorb the nutrients inside.

Under optimal light and nutrient conditions, Chlorella reproduces rapidly via autosporulation—a single mother cell divides internally into four (or sometimes eight) identical daughter cells within 24 hours.

Found in ponds, lakes, damp soil, and nutrient-rich freshwaters worldwide, Chlorella forms the base of aquatic food webs and generates substantial amounts of oxygen.

Certain species, such as *Chlorella variabilis*, live inside the bodies of larger aquatic organisms—most famously the green ciliate *Paramecium bursaria*, freshwater hydras (Hydra viridissima), and freshwater sponges. Inside these hosts, Chlorella trades sugars and oxygen produced via photosynthesis in exchange for shelter, carbon dioxide, and nitrogen.

*Today, Chlorella is cultivated commercially in open raceway ponds and closed photobioreactors for health supplements, aquaculture feed, wastewater treatment (absorbing heavy metals and excess nitrates), and experimental algal biofuel production.*
