
The red-shanked douc langur (*Pygathrix nemaeus*) is an Old World monkey (order [[Primates]], class [[Mammalia|Mammalia]]) widely celebrated as one of the most strikingly colorful primates in the world.

Native entirely to the dense canopy forests of Indochina (primarily Laos, Vietnam, and Cambodia), this arboreal monkey is often called the "costumed ape" due to its extraordinarily vibrant, multi-colored fur.

Its coat looks as though it were painted in distinct blocks. It features a bright golden-orange face framed by long white whiskers, a dappled grey torso, white forearms, black thighs, and striking maroon-red lower legs ("shanks")

Adults measure roughly 55 to 63 cm (21 to 25 inches) in body length, with a long, slender white tail of similar length used for balance in the treetops. Males weigh between 8 and 11.6 kg (18 to 26 lbs), making them noticeably larger than females.

Native to the tropical evergreen and semi-evergreen forests of Vietnam, Laos, and northeastern Cambodia, these primates spend nearly their entire lives high up in the forest canopy. They are agile leapers capable of jumping up to 6 meters (20 feet) between branches.
