The giant malaysian pitcher plant (*Nepenthes rajah*) is the largest carnivorous pitcher plant in the world by trap volume. Endemic to the cloud forests of Mount Kinabalu and neighboring Mount Tambuyukon in Sabah, Malaysian [[Borneo]], it is famous for its massive, reddish-purple urn-shaped traps capable of holding over 3.5 liters (nearly a gallon) of rainwater and digestive fluid.

*Named after a "White Rajah." Described by botanist Joseph Dalton Hooker in 1859, the species was named in honor of James Brooke, the first British "White Rajah" of Sarawak.*

*[[Plants]]*

Its lower pitchers are modified leaves shaped like rugby-ball-sized urns, measuring up to 41 centimeters (16 in) tall and 20 centimeters (8 in) wide. They are crowned by a large, vaulted lid (_operculum_) that prevents rain from overflowing the trap and a scalloped, glossy rim (_peristome_) coated in sweet nectar.

The plant grows as a woody, scrambling vine up to 6 meters (20 ft) long, with thick, leathery leaves up to 80 centimeters long. Uniquely, the tendril supporting each pitcher attaches to the underside of the leaf blade (_peltate_ attachment) rather than the tip.

Named after a "White Rajah." Described by botanist Joseph Dalton Hooker in 1859, the species was named in honor of James Brooke, the first British "White Rajah" of Sarawak.

While its primary prey consists of arthropods—especially ants—its enormous pitchers have occasionally drowned and digested small vertebrates such as rats, frogs, lizards, and small birds.

Scientists discovered that _Nepenthes rajah_'s oversized, sturdy pitchers evolved largely as an animal latrine. The underside of the pitcher lid secretes carbohydrate-rich nectar that attracts mountain tree shrews (_Tupaia montana_) during the day and summit rats (_Rattus baluensis_) at night.

The pitcher's dimensions match the animals' body length so they straddle the rim to lick the lid while defecating directly into the cup—supplying the plant with essential nitrogen in nutrient-poor mountain soils.

The fluid inside the pitchers also hosts specialized organisms adapted to survive digestive enzymes, including mosquito larvae that live nowhere else on Earth.

*[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Species/Mosquito]], [[Mammalia]]*

It grows exclusively on moist, mineral-rich serpentine (ultramafic) soils at elevations between 1,500 and 2,650 meters (4,900–8,700 ft).

Because of its tiny geographic range, severe El Niño droughts, and illegal poaching by plant collectors, _Nepenthes rajah_ is listed as Endangered by the IUCN and protected under CITES Appendix I (banning commercial trade in wild-collected specimens).
