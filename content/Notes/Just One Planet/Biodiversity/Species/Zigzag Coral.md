Zigzag coral (*Madrepora oculata*) is a widespread deep-sea stony coral named for the distinctive zigzag pattern formed by its alternating branches and polyps.

Found across the world's oceans outside the polar regions—including the [[Mediterranean Sea]] and the Northeast [[Atlantic Ocean]]—it thrives in dark, cold waters at depths between 50 and 1,500+ meters.

It grows into bushy, fan-shaped colonies roughly 30 to 50 cm tall, with a white, pink, or pale orange calcium carbonate skeleton that bends back and forth as new polyps bud alternately.

Its branches are delicate and brittle, growing only 3 to 18 mm per year. Because it breaks easily, it often grows intertwined with sturdier reef-building cold-water corals like Lophelia pertusa (*Desmophyllum pertusum*) for structural support.

Unlike many stony corals, zigzag coral secretes a thick layer of slimy mucus that shields its tissues from sediment, parasites, and predators.

Because it lives far below the sunlit zone, zigzag coral lacks photosynthetic algae (zooxanthellae). Instead, its polyps catch zooplankton and organic particles drifting in deep ocean currents.

It is one of only a dozen coral species with a near-global distribution, dominating deep-water coral communities in places like the submarine canyons of the northwestern Mediterranean (such as off Spain and France) and the North Atlantic.

*Listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List, zigzag coral faces serious threats from deep-sea bottom trawling, oil and gas drilling, submarine cable laying, and ocean acidification, with its slow growth making recovery extremely difficult.*
