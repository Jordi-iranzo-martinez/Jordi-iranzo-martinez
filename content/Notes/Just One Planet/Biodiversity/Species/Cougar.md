The cougar (*Puma concolor*), also widely known as the mountain lion, puma, or panther, is a large, adaptable wild cat native to the Americas ([[North America]] and [[South America]])

*Its scientific name, concolor, means "of uniform color," referring to its unspotted tawny, tan, or silvery-grey adult coat (kittens are born with dark spots and blue eyes that fade as they mature).* 

*It holds the Guinness World Record for the animal with the greatest number of common names (over 40 in English alone) and has the widest geographic distribution of any wild terrestrial mammal in the Western Hemisphere, stretching from the Canadian Yukon to the southern Andes.*

Despite its large size, the cougar belongs to the subfamily _Felinae_ (small cats) rather than _Pantherinae_. Because of its vocal anatomy, it cannot roar; instead, it communicates through purrs, growls, hisses, chirps, and eerie, high-pitched screams.

Adult males typically weigh between 53 and 100 kg (115–220 lbs) and measure up to 2.4 meters (8 ft) from nose to tail tip, while females are smaller (29–64 kg). It is the second-heaviest cat in the Americas, after the jaguar.

It possesses muscular hind legs—the longest proportionally in the cat family—enabling vertical leaps of over 5 meters (16 ft) and horizontal bounds of up to 12 meters (40 ft).

Cougars thrive in nearly every habitat type across the Americas, including boreal forests, rocky mountains, deserts, tropical jungles, and coastal swamps (such as the endangered Florida panther population).

Aside from mothers with cubs, cougars are strictly solitary and territorial. They stalk prey silently before delivering a powerful bite to the back of the neck. Their primary prey consists of deer (such as mule deer, white-tailed deer, and elk), though they also hunt guanacos, wild pigs, rabbits, and rodents.

As apex predators (alongside wolves, jaguars, and bears), cougars regulate herbivore populations and help maintain healthy forest and grassland ecosystems.
