A rhinoceros beetle (subfamily Dynastinae) is one of the largest and strongest beetles on Earth, belonging to the scarab family and named for the dramatic, rhino-like horns on the heads of males.

[[Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Taxonomy/Zoology/Insects|Insects]]

Rhinoceros beetles are among the strongest creatures on the planet relative to body size—capable of lifting up to 850 times their own body weight (roughly equivalent to a human lifting 65 tons).

Only males grow large horns, which they use like wrestling forks to pry rival males off tree branches or females during mating battles. Despite their intimidating armor, they are completely harmless to humans (they cannot bite or sting).

Adults feed mainly on tree sap, nectar, and rotting fruit, while their large white larvae (_grubs_) spend 1 to 3 years underground or inside decaying wood before pupating into adults for a single summer season.

Famous Species

- [[Hercules Beetle]] (_Dynastes hercules_). Native to Central and South American rainforests, it is one of the longest beetles in the world (reaching up to 17 cm / 7 inches including its massive pincer-like horn).

- [[Japanese Rhinoceros Beetle]] (_Trypoxylus dichotomus_ / _Kabutomushi_). Named after samurai helmets (_kabuto_), it is a beloved summer icon and popular pet in Japan.

- [[European Rhinoceros Beetle]] (_Oryctes nasicornis_). A glossy reddish-brown species native across Europe (including Spain).
