The Great horned owl (*Bubo virginianus*) is a large, powerful, and highly adaptable owl native to the Americas. Often nicknamed the "tiger of the sky," it is famous for its prominent ear tufts, piercing yellow eyes, and fierce hunting ability across nearly every habitat from the [[Arctic]] tree line to [[South America]].

[[Birds]]

*Its name comes from two prominent tufts of feathers called plumicorns on top of its head. They have nothing to do with hearing and are instead used for camouflage and visual communication with other owls.*

Adults measure 46 to 63 cm (18 to 25 inches) in length with a wingspan up to 145 cm (nearly 5 feet), weighing roughly 1.2 to 1.6 kg (2.6 to 3.5 lbs). Although shorter in length than the great grey owl, the great horned owl is much heavier and more muscular.

Its large, feathered feet end in curved talons capable of exerting around 28 to 30 pounds of crushing force—enough to sever the spine of large prey instantly.

Soft, fringed flight feathers muffle wing noise for silent ambushes, while massive forward-facing yellow eyes provide exceptional night vision.

It has the widest distribution of any true owl in the Americas, thriving in boreal forests, deserts, wetlands, tropical rainforests, grasslands, and even suburban parks and golf courses.

Unlike the great grey owl, which specializes almost exclusively in small rodents, the great horned owl has the most diverse diet of any North American raptor. It hunts rabbits, hares, squirrels, mice, ducks, geese, hawks, and is one of the few predators that regularly hunts skunks (thanks to having almost no sense of smell).

Its deep, resonant five-note hoot ("hoo-h'HOO-hoo-hoo") is the classic owl call heard in movies. They are among the earliest nesting birds in [[North America]], often laying eggs in January or February inside abandoned hawk, crow, or squirrel nests.
