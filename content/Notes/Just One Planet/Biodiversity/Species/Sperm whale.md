The sperm whale (_Physeter macrocephalus_) is the world's largest toothed predator== and possesses the largest brain of any known animal

*[[Cetaceans]]*

Famous for its massive, block-shaped head, extreme deep-diving abilities, and complex social culture, it inhabits deep oceans around the globe.

Sperm whales show extreme sexual dimorphism. Adult males grow up to 16 to 20.5 meters (52 to 67 feet) long and weigh 45 to 57 metric tons, whereas females are noticeably smaller, averaging 11 meters (36 feet) and 15 metric tons.

Its enormous head accounts for up to one-third of its total body length and houses a brain weighing up to 9 kg (2b0 lbs)—more than five times heavier than a human brain.

Inside the head sits the spermaceti organ, a large reservoir filled with a waxy liquid oil, alongside a lens-shaped fat body called the "junk." Together, they act as a powerful acoustic focusing system for echolocation, allowing the whale to project directional clicks reaching 230 decibels—the loudest biological sounds on Earth—to stun or locate prey in pitch-black water.

Unlike baleen whales that filter krill, sperm whales have 20 to 26 pairs of large conical teeth exclusively on their narrow lower jaw, which fit into sockets in the upper jaw.

Sperm whales are among the deepest-diving mammals on the planet, routinely plunging 1,000 to 2,000 meters (3,300 to 6,600 feet)—and occasionally deeper than 3,000 meters (nearly 2 miles)—while holding their breath for 60 to 90 minutes.

To survive extreme ocean depths without suffering decompression sickness ("the bends"), their flexible ribcages collapse under pressure, their heart rate slows dramatically (bradycardia), blood is redirected strictly to vital organs, and their muscles contain exceptionally high concentrations of myoglobin (an oxygen-storing protein that makes their muscle tissue nearly black).

In the pitch-black abyssal zone, they hunt deep-sea cephalopods—including giant squid (Architeuthis) and colossal squid (Mesonychoteuthis)—as well as deep-water sharks, skates, and fish. Many adult sperm whales bear circular scars on their heads from the serrated suction cups of giant squid fighting back.
