Sanctacaris ("saint crab," nicknamed "Santa Claws") is an extinct genus of marine arthropod that lived during the Middle [[Cambrian]] period, roughly 505 million years ago. Its fossils were discovered in the famous [[Burgess Shale]] formation in British Columbia, [[Canada]], with a single known species, Sanctacaris uncata.

It measured between 4.6 and 9.3 cm (1.8 to 3.7 inches) in length. Its body consisted of a broad head shield with triangular lateral extensions, a trunk made up of 11 segments, and a wide, flat, paddle-like tail (telson) used for steering.

Its most distinctive feature was a set of six pairs of specialized head appendages. Each limb branched into a spiny, claw-like inner section for grasping prey and a slender, antenna-like outer branch used for sensing its surroundings.

It had a pair of forward-facing eyes positioned along the front edge of its head shield. Along its trunk, it possessed biramous limbs equipped with gill flaps that allowed it to both breathe and swim actively above the seafloor.

Unlike many Cambrian arthropods that simply sifted through seafloor mud, Sanctacaris was an agile swimmer and hunter.

It cruised just above the seabed locating prey with its eyes and sensory feelers, using its spiny head claws to seize and crush hard-shelled organisms.

*Although it lacked the classic pincer-like mouthparts (chelicerae) of modern spiders and scorpions, phylogenetic studies place Sanctacaris as one of the earliest basal members of the chelicerate lineage. This makes it an important evolutionary link for understanding how the marine ancestors of arachnids and horseshoe crabs diversified during the Cambrian explosion.*
