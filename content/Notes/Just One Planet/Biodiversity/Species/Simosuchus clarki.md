Simosuchus clarki (meaning "Clark's pug-nosed crocodile") is an extinct, small, plant-eating crocodyliform that lived in [[Madagascar]] during the Late [[Cretaceous]] period, roughly 70 to 66 million years ago. 

Unlike modern ambush-hunting crocodiles with long, toothy snouts, *Simosuchus* was a terrestrial, heavily armored animal with an unusually short, blunt snout and leaf-shaped teeth.

Instead of an elongated jaw built for snapping at prey, its skull was short, deep, and angled downward at about 45 degrees—making it well suited for cropping low-growing vegetation.

Its teeth were multicuspid and shaped like cloves or maple leaves rather than conical meat-tearing fangs.

Measuring only about 0.75 to 1 meter (2.5 to 3.3 feet) long, it had a stocky, barrel-shaped body, a noticeably short tail (indicating it did not swim), and interlocking shields of bony armor plates (*osteoderms*) covering its back, belly, tail, and even its limbs.

Unlike the sprawling posture of modern crocodiles, its robust limbs were held more upright beneath its body for walking on land.

Paleontologists conclude from its weak bite force, downward-tilted snout, and leaf-shaped teeth that *Simosuchus* was predominantly or exclusively herbivorous—feeding on ferns, cycads, and low shrubs.

Discovered in the Maevarano Formation of northwestern Madagascar (and named in 2000 in honor of crocodyliform specialist James M. Clark), it shared its semi-arid floodplain habitat with bizarre dinosaurs like *Majungasaurus*, *Masiakasaurus*, and the giant frog *Beelzebufo*.

Built like a miniature living tank rather than a fast runner, it relied on its dense osteoderm armor—and possibly shallow burrowing—to deter predators.
