
*Homo sapiens* (from Latin homo meaning "man" and sapiens meaning "wise") is the only surviving species of the genus Homo and the species to which all modern humans belong.

Emerging in [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Wonderful places/Africa]] roughly 300,000 years ago, *Homo sapiens* is distinguished by a highly developed brain, complex symbolic language, and an unmatched capacity for culture, technology, and large-scale social cooperation.

The oldest known *Homo sapiens* fossils come from Jebel Irhoud in Morocco, dated to approximately 315,000 years ago, alongside key early remains in [[Ethiopia]] (Omo Kibish and Herto) dating between 195,000 and 230,000 years ago.

Major waves of _Homo sapiens_ migrated out of Africa around 60,000 to 70,000 years ago, spreading across Eurasia, Oceania, and eventually the Americas. Along the way, they encountered and interbred with other archaic human species, including Neanderthals (_Homo neanderthalensis_) and Denisovans, leaving trace amounts of their DNA in modern non-African populations today.

[[Homo neanderthalensis]], [[homo deni]]

Compared to earlier hominins, _Homo sapiens_ has a high, rounded (globular) braincase, a vertical forehead, reduced brow ridges, and a prominent chin. Average brain volume is roughly 1,300 to 1,400 cm³, featuring notably expanded frontal and parietal lobes.

Modern humans possess a lighter, more slender skeletal build than archaic relatives like Neanderthals, optimized for efficient bipedal walking, endurance running, and fine manual dexterity.

Complex spoken language allowed humans to share abstract ideas, coordinate in large groups, and pass cumulative knowledge across generations, reflected in early cave paintings, sculptures, musical instruments, and personal ornaments.

After living as nomadic hunter-gatherers for hundreds of millennia, human populations independently developed agriculture and animal domestication around 12,000 years ago (the Neolithic Revolution), leading to permanent settlements, writing systems, cities, and modern civilization.
