
Aristostomias scintillans, commonly known as the shiny loosejaw, is a deep-sea barbeled dragonfish (family Stomiidae) native to the eastern Pacific Ocean, famous for its invisible "night-vision" red bioluminescence and nanostructured transparent teeth.

Growing up to about 23 cm (9 inches) in length, it has an elongated, scaleless black body that absorbs ambient light, helping it vanish into the deep ocean darkness.

Like other members of the subfamily Malacosteinae, its lower jaw lacks a muscular floor (intermandibular membrane) and features a flexible head joint capable of opening up to 120 degrees. Without water resistance against a jaw floor, its mouth can snap shut at blinding speed like a bear trap.

A [2019 materials science study](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESgQEBxCJF10mPGPBwMa8qar5sOvsYTby-o3Cf1YqYLPUiQWJVN6qyEOAd6DJPn0v2VEa2L1gGxmM_ZO-o8Rrt6kktpNVZFQZkaqt0hORiFP8uUA5AqzOEVdIjILaY9Q9pQlXgfeXhd_1OHCVsm5uUatCxw72xKlVCuxQB9KKleQt17IA) revealed that its dagger-like teeth are made of nanocrystals (roughly 20 nanometers wide) embedded in an amorphous matrix that matches the refractive index of seawater. This eliminates light scattering, keeping its open jaws completely invisible even when illuminated by bioluminescent flashes.

Beneath each eye sits a large suborbital photophore that emits far-red bioluminescence (>700 nm), paired with a smaller green postorbital photophore and a pink-tipped chin barbel lure. Because almost all deep-sea creatures only see blue-green light, _A. scintillans_ can illuminate red-pigmented prey (like deep-sea shrimp) and communicate with mates in total secrecy.

It inhabits the mesopelagic and bathypelagic zones (the twilight and midnight zones) of the eastern Pacific—from the Bering Sea and British Columbia down to Baja California—typically at depths between 200 and 1,200 meters (650 to 4,000 feet).

It is an ambush predator that performs daily vertical migrations toward shallower waters at night to feed on midwater fishes (such as lanternfishes) and crustaceans.

Despite its alien appearance and extreme adaptations, it has a wide distribution and is listed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List.
