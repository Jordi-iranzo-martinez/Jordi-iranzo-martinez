*Aloe vera* is an evergreen succulent plant species (*Aloe vera*, family Asphodelaceae) renowned for the clear, soothing gel stored inside its thick, serrated leaves.

Key Characteristics

- **Water-Storing Leaves.** Its thick, fleshy green leaves (often dotted with white spots when young) hold a clear, nutrient-rich gel that helps the plant survive arid climates.

- **Origins & Habitat.** Originally native to the Arabian Peninsula, it now thrives worldwide in tropical, subtropical, and Mediterranean climates (including across Spain) as well as indoors as a popular houseplant.

- **Tubular Blooms.** Mature plants (typically 4+ years old) grown in bright sunlight produce tall stalks topped with yellow or orange tubular flowers.

Common Uses & Care

- **Skin Soothing.** The inner gel is widely used topically to cool and hydrate minor burns, sunburns, dry skin, and mild irritations.

- **Low-Maintenance Care.** Indoors, it prefers bright, indirect sunlight and well-draining cactus/succulent soil—let the soil dry out completely between waterings to prevent root rot.

- **Safety Note.** While the clear inner gel is safe for topical use, the yellowish sap just beneath the skin (_aloin_) is a strong laxative and can cause digestive upset if ingested (and is toxic to pets like cats and dogs).
