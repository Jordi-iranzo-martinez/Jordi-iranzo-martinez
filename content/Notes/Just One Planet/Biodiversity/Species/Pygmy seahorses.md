
Pygmy seahorses are a group of minuscule marine fish in the genus *Hippocampus*—the smallest seahorse species in the world, measuring less than 2.7 cm (about 1 inch) long. 

*[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Taxonomy/Zoology/Fishes|Fishes]]*

Renowned as masters of disguise, they spend nearly their entire adult lives clinging to specific corals, sponges, or algae, matching their host's color and texture so precisely that the first species wasn't discovered until 1969—purely by accident in a laboratory aquarium.

Unlike larger seahorses, pygmy seahorses have evolved specialized anatomical traits for life anchored to reef organisms:

- **Living mimicry**. Species like Bargibant's pygmy seahorse (_Hippocampus bargibanti_) grow fleshy, bulbous bumps called tubercles across their bodies that mimic the exact color and shape of the polyps on their host gorgonian sea fan (_Muricella_)—appearing pale grey-purple with pink bumps on purple fans, or yellow with orange bumps on yellow fans.

- **Unique physical features**. Beyond their fingernail-sized bodies, true pygmy seahorses differ from larger seahorses by having a single gill opening on the back of the head (rather than two behind the cheeks) and incubating their young inside their main trunk rather than a separate tail pouch.

- **Prehensile tail and tubular snout**. Because they are weak swimmers without a swim bladder, they wrap their prehensile tails tightly around coral branches and use toothless, straw-like snouts to suck in passing zooplankton and tiny crustaceans,

Most of the eight recognized species live in the [[Coral Triangle]] of Southeast [[Asia]] (Indonesia, the Philippines, Malaysia, and Papua [[New Guinea]]), though species also occur in [[Japan]], [[Australia]], and [[South Africa]]:

- **Obligate coral dwellers**. _Hippocampus bargibanti_ and Denise's pygmy seahorse (_H. denise_) live exclusively on specific gorgonian sea fans at depths of 10 to 50 meters.

- **Free-living reef pygmie**s. Smaller species like Satomi's pygmy seahorse (_H. satomiae_, the world's smallest seahorse at just 1.4 cm long) and Pontoh's pygmy seahorse (_H. pontohi_) roam among hydroids, bryozoans, and coralline algae.

Just like larger seahorses, females deposit eggs into the male's brood pouch, where he fertilizes and carries them for 10 to 14 days before giving birth to roughly a dozen miniature, fully independent young (each around 2 mm long).

 Because many pygmy seahorses cannot survive without their specific host corals, marine biologists warn that coral bleaching, ocean acidification, and physical disturbance from careless scuba divers pose severe threats to their survival.
