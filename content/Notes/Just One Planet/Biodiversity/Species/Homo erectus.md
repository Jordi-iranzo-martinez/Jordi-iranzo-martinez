*Homo erectus* (Latin for "upright man") is an extinct species of archaic human that lived throughout most of the Pleistocene epoch, appearing roughly 1.9 to 2 million years ago and surviving until around 110,000 years ago.

With a lifespan of nearly two million years—more than six times longer than Homo sapiens has existed—it is the longest-surviving known human species and the first hominin to develop modern human-like body proportions and migrate out of Africa across Eurasia.

Unlike earlier hominins such as _Australopithecus_ and _Homo habilis_, which retained tree-climbing adaptations, _Homo erectus_ had long legs, shorter arms, a narrow pelvis, and arched feet—an anatomy fully adapted for efficient long-distance bipedal walking and endurance running on open grasslands.

Individuals typically stood between 1.45 and 1.85 meters (4 ft 9 in to 6 ft 1 in) tall, exemplified by the remarkably complete 1.5-million-year-old adolescent skeleton known as Turkana Boy (KNM-WT 15000) found in Kenya.

They had a long, low braincase with thick cranial bones, a prominent brow ridge, a projecting nose, and no chin. Their brain capacity expanded significantly over time, ranging from roughly 550–600 cm³ in early specimens to over 1,100–1,250 cm³ in later populations (approaching the lower range of modern humans).

Homo erectus_ was the first hominin species to disperse beyond Africa, spreading rapidly into the Caucasus (Dmanisi, Georgia, ~1.8 million years ago), East Asia (China), and Southeast Asia (Indonesia), where the last known populations persisted on the island of Java until roughly 108,000–117,000 years ago. _(Note: Some paleoanthropologists classify the early African specimens as a distinct sister species, Homo ergaster)._

They pioneered the Acheulean tool industry, crafting symmetrical, teardrop-shaped stone handaxes and cleavers that required advanced spatial planning and remained in use for over a million years.

_Homo erectus_ is widely associated with the earliest controlled use of fire (with contested evidence dating back 1 to 1.5 million years). Cooking meat and tubers made nutrients easier to digest, fueling the metabolic demands of a larger brain while allowing jaws and teeth to shrink.
