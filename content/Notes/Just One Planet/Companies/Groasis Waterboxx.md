
The Groasis Waterboxx is an eco-friendly planting device designed to grow trees, shrubs, and vegetables in arid, eroded, or desert environments without continuous irrigation or electricity. 

*Invented by Dutch entrepreneur Pieter Hoff, it acts as an "intelligent water battery" that mimics natural condensation and soil insulation processes.*

*[[Soil|Soil]], [[Desertification]], [[Food]], [[Biodiversity]], [[Water]]*

The device is a donut-shaped polypropylene bucket placed around a newly planted sapling (often paired with mycorrhizal fungi to stimulate root growth).

- **Water Harvesting.** Its grooved lid collects rainwater and uses day-to-night temperature shifts to condense atmospheric dew, channeling the moisture down into a sealed internal reservoir.

- **Controlled Wick Delivery.** A small cotton wick at the base releases roughly 50 milliliters (1.7 ounces) of water per day via capillary action directly to the root zone. This provides just enough moisture to keep the sapling alive without waterlogging it, encouraging its taproot to grow deep downward to reach natural groundwater.

- **Microclimate Protection.** By covering the soil around the stem, the box prevents capillary evaporation of groundwater, shields the young plant from scorching sun, drying winds, and grazing rodents, and blocks competing weeds.

It uses roughly 90% less water than traditional drip irrigation and achieves tree survival rates above 90% even in harsh desert conditions (such as reforestation trials in Morocco and Spain's arid Los Monegros region).

Once the sapling's roots reach deep groundwater after about a year, the original plastic [Groasis Waterboxx](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESVwHEIkXXGmWmyszoP3ZLW_vmSnpdV8qNfgnzNTQ8cBV9di0un30TEKEXypwD6ORDZj9K85Sc7kOXU259w1pHPZY5COsikrzWy8iljL1i8ecAv0RP9H5pGw) can be lifted off and reused up to 10 times. Groasis also produces the Growboxx, a biodegradable version made from recycled paper pulp that breaks down naturally into organic fertilizer.
