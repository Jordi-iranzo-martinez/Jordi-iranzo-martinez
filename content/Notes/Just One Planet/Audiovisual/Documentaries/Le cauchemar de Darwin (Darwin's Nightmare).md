The film examines the unintended consequences of introducing the predatory Nile perch into [Lake Victoria](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Lago+Victoria/@-1.2649662,30.5780272,7z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x19d18e2143b96ce1:0x5df3e171f5566538!8m2!3d-0.7557754!4d33.438353!16zL20vMGptdDc?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MTExMC4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D) during the 1950s and 1960s as a scientific experiment.

[[Invasive species]]

The giant fish wiped out hundreds of native cichlid species, permanently destroying the lake's natural biodiversity.

Today, massive processing plants fillet the perch and fly them out on Soviet cargo planes to European supermarkets, generating millions of dollars.

Despite this lucrative export industry, the local Tanzanian population faces severe poverty, famine, and a devastating HIV/AIDS epidemic, subsisting largely on the rotting fish carcasses discarded by the factories.

*The documentary investigates a dark rumor regarding what the cargo planes carry on their flights into Tanzania before loading up with fish. Through interviews with pilots, watchmen, and locals, the film strongly suggests the planes are smuggling weapons and ammunition into active African war zones.*
