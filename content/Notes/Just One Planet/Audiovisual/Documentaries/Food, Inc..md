Food, Inc. is a 2008 American documentary film directed by Robert Kenner and co-produced by investigative journalists Eric Schlosser (author of Fast Food Nation) and Michael Pollan (author of The Omnivore's Dilemma)

The documentary is divided into three main segments exploring different facets of the industrial food system:

- **Industrial Meat Production**. Examines factory farming (CAFOs) for chickens, cattle, and pigs, highlighting cramped living conditions, the heavy use of antibiotics, and how feeding cheap, subsidized corn to grass-eating cattle promotes dangerous pathogens like toxic _E. coli_ O157:H7 (illustrated through the tragic story of two-year-old Kevin Kowalcyk).

- **Corporate Monopolies and Monoculture**. Explores how a few giant corporations (such as Tyson, Cargill, JBS, and Monsanto) dominate meat processing and seed patents. It documents how Monsanto used intellectual property lawsuits to force soybean and corn farmers into buying patented, genetically modified seeds every year rather than saving seeds from their harvests.

- **Economics, Health, and Labor**. Investigates why heavily processed, high-fructose corn syrup foods are cheaper than fresh fruits and vegetables, linking cheap industrial calories to rising rates of obesity and Type 2 diabetes among low-income families. It also exposes harsh working conditions and union-busting at massive slaughterhouses like Smithfield Foods.

Released widely in 2009 after premiering at the 2008 Toronto International Film Festival, Food, Inc. sparked widespread debate over food labeling, farm subsidies, and sustainable agriculture, helping fuel interest in local farmers' markets and organic food.
