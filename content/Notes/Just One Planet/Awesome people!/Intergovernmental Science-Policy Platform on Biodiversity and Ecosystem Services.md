The [Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services](https://www.ipbes.net/) (IPBES) is an independent intergovernmental body established in 2012 to strengthen the science-policy interface for biodiversity and ecosystem services.

Often described as the "IPCC for biodiversity," it provides policymakers with objective, peer-reviewed scientific assessments regarding the state of the planet's nature and its contributions to people.

Structure and Governance

- **Headquarters and secretariat**. Based in Bonn, Germany, its secretariat is administered by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP).

- **Membership**. Open to all United Nations member states, IPBES has grown to over 150 member governments. Decisions are made annually by the IPBES Plenary, the platform's governing body, where member states negotiate and approve Summaries for Policymakers line by line.

- **Inclusive knowledge**. IPBES explicitly integrates Indigenous and local knowledge alongside multidisciplinary natural and social sciences, using the conceptual framework of "Nature's Contributions to People" (NCP).

According to the IPBES Secretariat, the organization focuses on four main pillars:

1. **Scientific assessments**. Producing comprehensive global, regional, and thematic evaluations of biodiversity, ecosystem health, and policy options.

2. **Policy support**. Identifying policy-relevant tools and methodologies to help governments implement international commitments like the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.

3. **Capacity building**. Supporting developing countries and experts in generating and using biodiversity data.

4. **Knowledge generation**. Catalyzing research to fill critical gaps in ecological and socioeconomic understanding.

IPBES reports have shaped global environmental policy and corporate sustainability standards:]

- **Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services** (2019). The first comprehensive global synthesis since 2005, warning that roughly 1 million animal and plant species face extinction within decades due to human activity. (A Second Global Assessment is scheduled for completion in 2028).

- **Invasive Alien Species Assessment** (2023). Documented the severe ecological and economic costs of invasive species worldwide.

- **Nexus & Transformative Change Assessments** (December 2024). Two major reports approved at IPBES-11 examining the interlinkages among biodiversity, water, food, health, and climate, alongside the systemic economic and social shifts required to achieve the 2050 Vision for Biodiversity.

- **Business and Biodiversity Assessment** (February 2026). Approved at IPBES-12 in Manchester, this [methodological assessment](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYgHEIkXXaM1VlAV3v4zsk8sY8ipZc8Ci_gDky1FBRQlWCtUdZdi44JpBgs20pD9oTJ_qXIxOAhduLac1IZm1WBKT4F_xLMxizSSShXw5ZagPkY8U6zOa6w6UPE1roi1OwO53) provides frameworks for measuring how businesses both impact and depend upon nature.
