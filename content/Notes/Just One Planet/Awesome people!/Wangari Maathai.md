>*When resources are degraded, we start competing for them. [...] So one way to promote peace is to promote sustainable management and equitable distribution of resources.*  

Wangarĩ Muta Maathai (1940–2011) was a Kenyan environmentalist, biologist, women's rights advocate, and political activist who founded the Green Belt Movement and became the first African woman and first environmentalist to win the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004.

Born in rural Nyeri, Kenya, Maathai broke numerous academic and gender barriers early in her career: 

- **Higher education**. After studying biology in the United States at Mount St. Scholastica College (B.S., 1964) and the University of Pittsburgh (M.S., 1966), she earned a Ph.D. in veterinary anatomy from the University of Nairobi in 1971—becoming the first woman in East and Central [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Life!/Wonderful Places/Africa]] to earn a doctorate.

- **University leadership**. She went on to become the first female department chair (1976) and first female associate professor in Kenya at the University of Nairobi.

Recognizing that rural Kenyan women were suffering from dried-up streams, soil erosion, and severe shortages of firewood and nutritious food due to deforestation, Maathai founded the Green Belt Movement in 1977:

- **Grassroots reforestation**. The organization trained and paid rural women to gather seeds, establish tree nurseries, and plant indigenous trees across degraded landscapes, linking environmental restoration directly to poverty reduction and women's economic empowerment.

- **Scale of impact**. Under her leadership, communities planted more than 50 million trees across Kenya and inspired the Pan-African Green Belt Network.

Maathai famously stated that planting trees was planting the seeds of peace and democracy, frequently clashing with President Daniel arap Moi's authoritarian regime:

- **Defending public spaces**. In 1989, she led successful protests that halted the construction of a 60-story skyscraper complex in Nairobi's Uhuru Park, as well as campaigns to save Karura Forest from illegal land grabbing—enduring beatings, smear campaigns, and multiple arrests.

- **Political career**. Following Kenya's democratic transition in 2002, she was elected to Parliament with 98% of the vote and served as Assistant Minister for Environment and Natural Resources from 2003 to 2007.

In 2004, the Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded Maathai the Nobel Peace Prize for her "*holistic approach to sustainable development that embraces democracy, human rights and women's rights in particular"*
