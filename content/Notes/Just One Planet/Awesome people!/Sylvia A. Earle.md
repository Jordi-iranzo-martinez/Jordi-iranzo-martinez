Dr. Sylvia A. Earle (born August 30, 1935) is a trailblazing American marine biologist, oceanographer, aquanaut, and explorer affectionately nicknamed "Her Deepness".

Over a career spanning more than seven decades, she has logged over 7,500 hours underwater, led more than 100 ocean expeditions, and founded the global conservation initiative Mission Blue.

After earning her Ph.D. in phycology (the study of algae) from Duke University in 1966, Earle broke numerous gender and technological barriers in deep-sea exploration:]

- **Tektite II Mission** (1970). After being rejected from the first all-male Tektite mission, she led the first all-female team of aquanauts, living and conducting marine research in an underwater habitat 15 meters (50 feet) below the surface off the U.S. Virgin Islands for two weeks.

- **Record-setting sea walk** (1979). Wearing a pressurized JIM diving suit, she walked untethered on the ocean floor at a depth of 381 meters (1,250 feet) off Oahu, Hawaii, for two and a half hours—setting a women's solo dive depth record that still stands.

- **Subsea engineering**. In the 1980s, she co-founded Deep Ocean Engineering and DOER Marine, helping design and pilot deep-sea submersibles capable of reaching depths of 1,000 meters.

- **NOAA Chief Scientist** (1990–1992). She became the first woman to serve as Chief Scientist of the U.S. [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration](https://www.noaa.gov/) (NOAA).

Named _Time_ magazine's first "Hero for the Planet" in 1998 and serving as a National Geographic Explorer at Large, Earle has dedicated her later career to protecting the world's oceans from overfishing, pollution, and climate change:

- **Hope Spots**. Using her 2009 TED Prize wish, she founded [Mission Blue](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESVQHEIkXXh-VvC31pA4ddsgjssMDgu75DilCs5RFjRBfF1CehogNzsAs-vctfR8FT7lewQiTj0RV-6w2D-t09qinSoJ92v1gqQ-CmjG1Gxc74Emy_ZiE), a global alliance that establishes Hope Spots—ecologically critical marine areas championed by local communities and scientists to gain formal protection. Over 150 Hope Spots have been designated worldwide.

- **Global recognition**. She has authored more than 200 scientific and popular publications (including _Sea Change_ and _The World Is Blue_) and received top international honors, including the National Geographic Hubbard Medal (2013) and Spain's Princess of Asturias Award for Concord (2018).
