The [Recycled Orchestra of Cateura](https://www.recycledorchestracateura.com/) (Orquesta de Instrumentos Reciclados de Cateura) is a youth orchestra from Asunción, Paraguay, whose musicians play instruments handcrafted entirely from garbage salvaged from a massive landfill.

Formed in 2006 in the impoverished Cateura neighborhood—where a real violin costs more than a family's home—the project has become an international symbol of environmental sustainability, resilience, and social transformation through music.

Cateura is a low-income community built directly beside Asunción's largest municipal dump, where thousands of families earn a living sorting recyclable materials.

In 2006, environmental technician and music educator Favio Chávez began offering free music lessons to local children. When student demand quickly exceeded the few donated instruments available, Chávez teamed up with local trash picker and carpenter Nicolás "Cola" Gómez to build playable instruments from scrap materials found in the landfill.

Using ingenuity and lutherie techniques, Gómez and local artisans transform discarded everyday items into functional orchestral instruments:

- **Violins, violas, and cellos**. Built from flattened metal paint cans, oil drums, baking tins, wooden packing crates, discarded forks or spoons (as tailpieces), and old coins.

- **Double basses**. Constructed out of large metal oil barrels combined with reclaimed cedar wood necks and tuning pegs.

- **Wind and brass instruments**. Saxophones, flutes, and trumpets are fashioned from metal water pipes, drainage tubes, bottle caps, spoons, and old X-ray film (used for drumheads and instrument membranes).

The orchestra gained worldwide fame following the release of the award-winning 2015 documentary film *Landfill Harmonic*, which chronicled their journey from the slums of Asunción to international concert halls.

The ensemble has performed across five continents, sharing stages with major artists such as Metallica (during their South American tour), Megadeth, Stevie Wonder, and Spanish violinist Ara Malikian, as well as playing for Pope Francis and European royalty.

Today, the [Recycled Orchestra of Cateura](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESZgHEIkXXeRYhyEp-otQTJLK85yvz3SrJFIeoPxUgA99H4ky4fDGx00fA0c6ddaDde1_uVOiIJTbrqHpwPsVLx6hP28VngluwVb6dE8IrbdRmQzKLITBS8XqSCxx8cs-9X2ovlADN7w) operates a permanent music school in Cateura serving hundreds of children and youths, using touring proceeds to build flood-resistant homes for families in the community and inspire sister projects worldwide (such as _La Música del Reciclaje_ in Spain).
