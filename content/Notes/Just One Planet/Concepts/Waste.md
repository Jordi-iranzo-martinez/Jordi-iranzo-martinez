
Waste refers to any unwanted, discarded, or leftover material that is no longer useful or needed by its owner. Managing waste effectively is one of the central environmental, economic, and public health challenges of modern society.

Major Categories of Waste

- **Municipal Solid Waste** (MSW). Everyday trash generated by households, schools, and businesses, including food scraps, packaging, paper, plastics, glass, and textiles.

- **Industrial and Construction Waste**. Scrap metal, concrete, wood, ash, and chemical byproducts produced during manufacturing, demolition, and building projects.

- **Hazardous Waste**. Materials that pose substantial threats to human health or the environment due to toxicity, flammability, or corrosiveness—such as batteries, paints, solvents, pesticides, and medical waste.

- **Electronic Waste** (E-Waste). Discarded electrical and electronic devices like smartphones, computers, and appliances, which contain both valuable metals (gold, copper) and toxic substances (lead, mercury).

- **Agricultural Waste**. Crop residues, animal manure, and agrochemical containers generated by farming and livestock production.

The Waste Management Hierarchy

Modern environmental policy prioritizes waste handling from most favorable to least favorable:

- **Prevention and Reduction**. Avoiding waste generation in the first place through minimal packaging, durable product design, and conscious consumption.

- **Reuse**. Extending the life of items by repairing, donating, refilling, or repurposing them without industrial reprocessing.

- **Recycling and Composting**. Processing materials (paper, glass, metals, certain plastics) into new products, and turning organic food and yard waste into nutrient-rich compost.

- **Energy Recovery** (Waste-to-Energy). Burning non-recyclable waste in controlled facilities to generate electricity or heat, or capturing biogas from anaerobic digestion.

- **Disposal** (Landfilling). Burying residual waste in engineered sanitary landfills—the least desirable option, as decomposing organic matter emits methane (a potent greenhouse gas) and can produce toxic liquid leachate.

Global Environmental Impacts

- **Plastic Pollution**. Millions of tons of single-use plastics enter rivers and oceans annually, breaking down into microplastics that enter food chains and harm marine wildlife.

- **Climate Change**. Decomposing waste in landfills and the energy required to extract raw materials for single-use goods contribute significantly to global greenhouse gas emissions.

- **Circular Economy**. As an alternative to the traditional "take-make-dispose" linear model, a circular economy aims to eliminate waste entirely by keeping materials continuously circulating in biological or technical loops.
