Time is the indefinite continued progress of existence and events that occur in an apparently irreversible succession from the past, through the present, into the future.

In classical physics, time was viewed as absolute and independent of space.

However, Albert Einstein’s Theory of Relativity proved that time and space are woven into a single continuum known as "spacetime". Time is relative; it slows down or speeds up depending on gravitational forces and speed.

This concept describes the one-way directional flow of time from the past to the future. It is driven by the Second Law of Thermodynamics (Entropy), which states that isolated systems naturally progress from a state of order to disorder over time. This explains why biological bodies age, mountains erode over geological eras, and fallen trees naturally turn to compost.

Living organisms have evolved internal biological clocks synchronized with the 24-hour rotational period of the Earth. In humans, this internal clock regulates metabolic [[Energy]], hormone release, and the natural pressure for restorative [[Sleep]].
