Insects are a class of invertebrates within the arthropod phylum, characterized by a three-part body, three pairs of jointed legs, compound eyes, and one pair of antennae.

They are the most diverse group of animals on Earth, representing more than 70% of all known living species, and are absolutely essential for maintaining the balance of global ecosystems.

- **The Three-Part Body:** Their bodies are divided into a **head** (for sensory input), a **thorax** (the anchor for legs and wings), and an **abdomen** (for digestion and reproduction). This structure is protected by a tough, waterproof exoskeleton made of chitin.

- **Metamorphosis:** Most insects undergo a complete transformation (egg, larva, pupa, adult). This evolutionary strategy prevents adults and their young from competing for the same **food** resources and shelters.


Without insects, terrestrial food webs and ecosystems would completely collapse:

[[Pollination]]: Insects—especially bees, butterflies, and beetles—are the primary pollinators of the world's flowering plants. They are responsible for pollinating roughly **75% of global crop species**, making them vital for human nutrition and food security.

Decomposition and [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Concepts/Soil]] Health: Many insects act as nature's recycling crew. Beetles and ants break down dead organic matter and animal waste, accelerating the formation of nutrient-rich humus and **compost** to enrich the **soil**.

The Foundation of the [[Food]] Chain: Insects are a primary source of nutrients for countless animals. For instance, the larvae of many insects serve as the main food source for developing tadpoles and adults in amphibian ponds.



According to FAO, one third of food depends on insect pollinization, particularly by bees.
