
The Middle Bronze Age is a major archaeological period marking the peak of early bronze metallurgy, urban expansion, and long-distance trade networks across the ancient world.

Because metallurgy developed at different speeds globally, its exact dates vary by region, generally spanning from around 2100 to 1500 BCE in the Near East and Europe.

- **Ancient Near East and Levant** (c. 2100–1550 BCE). This era saw the rise of fortified Canaanite city-states, the Middle Kingdom of Egypt, and the Old Babylonian Empire under rulers like Hammurabi.

- **Aegean and Greece** (c. 2000–1600 BCE). Known as the Middle Helladic period on the mainland and the Middle Minoan period on Crete, this era featured the construction of the first grand Minoan palaces, such as Knossos.

- **Continental Europe** (c. 1600–1300 BCE). Metallurgy arrived slightly later in central and northern Europe, characterized by the Tumulus culture, named for its practice of burying the dead beneath prominent earthen mounds.

- **[[China]]** (c. 1600–1046 BCE). Coinciding largely with the Shang Dynasty, this period produced some of the finest cast-bronze ritual vessels and earliest known Chinese writing (oracle bone script).

Key Characteristics and Innovations

- **True Tin-Bronze Metallurgy**. Artisans shifted from softer copper or arsenic-copper alloys to tin-bronze (roughly 90% copper and 10% tin). This produced significantly harder, sharper, and more durable weapons, armor, and agricultural tools.

- **Long-Distance Trade Networks**. Because copper and tin rarely occur together naturally, civilizations built vast trade routes by land and sea to source raw materials—connecting places like Cornwall (tin) and Cyprus (copper) to Mediterranean markets.

- **Urbanization and Fortifications**. Growing wealth led to larger cities protected by massive earthen ramparts, stone walls, and fortified gates, alongside the rise of specialized craftspeople and merchant classes.

- **Warfare Advancements**. The period saw the widespread adoption of the horse-drawn spoke-wheeled chariot, composite bows, socketed spearheads, and bronze swords, transforming ancient military tactics.
