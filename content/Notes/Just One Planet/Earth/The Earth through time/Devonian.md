
The Devonian (spanning from 419.2 to 358.9 million years ago during the Paleozoic Era) is famously known among scientists as the "Age of [[Fishes]]" because marine ecosystems experienced an unprecedented explosion of fish diversity and jawed predators.

The Devonian oceans witnessed a massive diversification of jawed fish, which rapidly replaced the primitive, jawless filter-feeders of earlier eras:

Placoderms (Armored Fish): These were the undisputed rulers of the Devonian seas. They possessed heavy, bony armor plating over their heads and thoraxes.

- The Rise of Modern Lineages: This period saw the rise of the two groups that dominate oceans today: 
	- **Chondrichthyes** (cartilaginous fish, the ancestors of sharks and rays)
	- **Osteichthyes** (bony fish).

- **Lobe-Finned Fish**: A specialized group of bony fish developed muscular, lobed fins with internal bone structures. These fins eventually evolved into limbs, leading directly to the first land-walking vertebrates (tetrapods) like _**Tiktaalik**_ and _Ichthyostega_.

While the oceans were teeming with life, the dry land underwent a massive green transformation known as the Devonian Plant Explosion:

- From Moss to Trees: At the start of the Devonian, land plants were tiny, rootless structures (like _Cooksonia_) restricted to water edges. By the Late Devonian, plants evolved deep root systems, complex leaves, and wood, forming the **world's first true forests**.

- The *Archaeopteris* Forests: The dominant tree of these ancient forests was _Archaeopteris_, a primitive spore-bearing tree that grew up to 30 meters (100 feet) tall, resembling a modern conifer but reproducing like a fern.

- Soil and Climate Engineering: These massive root systems broke down rocks, creating the planet's first deep organic soils. This drastically altered Earth's carbon cycle and weather patterns.

The ancient continents were steadily moving toward one another during the Devonian, paving the way for the eventual creation of the supercontinent Pangea.

- **Two Super-Landmasses**: Most land was split into Euramerica (comprising modern North America and Europe) and Gondwana (comprising South America, Africa, Antarctica, India, and Australia).
- **Greenhouse Climate**: The climate was generally warm, tropical, and stable worldwide, completely lacking glaciers or polar ice caps for the majority of the period.

The period ended with the Late Devonian extinction event, one of the "Big Five" mass extinctions in planetary history. It wiped out roughly 70% to 80% of all marine species, severely decimating coral reef systems and permanently erasing the armored placoderm fish from the fossil record.

Unlike other extinctions triggered by sudden asteroid impacts or volcanic eruptions, the Devonian collapse was likely a slow, prolonged ecological crisis caused by the plants themselves:

1. **Runaway Weathering**: The rapid spread of deep tree roots caused massive amounts of minerals and nutrients to wash out of the soil and into the oceans (eutrophication).
2. **Algal Blooms & Anoxia**: This nutrient overload triggered global, catastrophic algal blooms that consumed all the dissolved oxygen in the water, creating massive marine "dead zones" (anoxia).
3. **Global Cooling**: The immense forests absorbed vast amounts of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, reversing the greenhouse effect and plunging the planet into a sudden, severe glacial cooling phase that the tropical marine life could not survive.
