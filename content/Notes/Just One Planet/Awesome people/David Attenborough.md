Sir David Frederick Attenborough (born May 8, 1926) is an iconic English broadcaster, biologist, natural historian, and environmental advocate whose career has spanned more than seven decades. 

Celebrating his 100th birthday in May 2026, he is widely regarded as the pioneer of modern wildlife filmmaking and the world's most influential voice for nature conservation.

After earning a degree in Natural Sciences from the University of Cambridge and serving in the Royal Navy, Attenborough joined the BBC in 1952:

In 1954, he launched _Zoo Quest_, combining live studio segments with on-location animal expeditions—a format that laid the groundwork for the BBC Natural History Unit.

Serving as Controller of BBC Two (1965–1968) and later Director of Programmes (1969–1972), he oversaw the introduction of color television to Britain and commissioned landmark cultural series such as _Civilisation_ and _Monty Python's Flying Circus_ before resigning to return to full-time documentary filmmaking.

Attenborough revolutionized science communication by pairing poetic narration with cutting-edge camera technology:

- The _Life_ Collection (1979–2008). Beginning with _Life on Earth_ (1979)—famous for his intimate on-camera encounter with wild mountain gorillas in Rwanda—he created a nine-part encyclopedic survey of life covering plants, birds, mammals, insects, reptiles, and microscopic organisms.

- Ultra-HD global blockbusters. He narrated and presented genre-defining series including _The Blue Planet_ (2001), _Planet Earth_ (2006, 2016, 2023), _Frozen Planet_ (2011, 2022), and Netflix's _Our Planet_ (2019), pioneering aerial, deep-sea, infrared, and time-lapse cinematography.

- Recent centenary releases. Continuing to work into his late nineties and centenary, he released the feature documentary _Ocean with David Attenborough_ (2025) and Netflix's _A Gorilla Story: Told by David Attenborough_ (2026).

In the latter decades of his career, Attenborough shifted from pure natural history observation to urgent environmental activism:

His 2020 film and book _A Life on Our Planet_ documented the dramatic loss of global wilderness during his lifetime while outlining practical steps—such as rewilding, renewable energy, and marine protection—to restore biodiversity.

His footage of plastic pollution in _Blue Planet II_ helped spark international policy shifts against single-use plastics, and he has addressed UN climate summits and world leaders on biodiversity loss.

Knighted in 1985 and appointed to the Order of Merit in 2005, he is the only person to win BAFTA awards in black-and-white, color, HD, 3D, and 4K television. Over 40 species of plants, insects, reptiles, and fossils—as well as the British polar research vessel RRS _Sir David Attenborough_—have been named in his honor.
