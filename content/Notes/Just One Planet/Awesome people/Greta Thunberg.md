Greta Thunberg (born January 3, 2003, in Stockholm, Sweden) is a Swedish environmental and social justice activist best known for sparking the global youth climate strike movement.

In August 2018, at age 15, she began a solo protest outside the Swedish Parliament holding a sign reading "Skolstrejk för klimatet" (School Strike for Climate). Her weekly strikes grew into Fridays for Future, mobilizing millions of students worldwide to demand urgent government action on global warming.

She has addressed major international forums—including the United Nations, the World Economic Forum, and UN climate summits—criticizing world leaders for prioritizing economic growth over emissions reductions. In November 2026, she returned to UN climate talks at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, for her first appearance at a COP summit since 2021.

Alongside climate and Indigenous land rights campaigns, her activism has broadened to international humanitarian and political causes, including pro-Palestinian protests and joining maritime aid missions such as the Global Sumud Flotilla toward Gaza in 2025 and 2026.

She is the author of No One Is Too Small to Make a Difference (2019) and editor of The Climate Book (2022). Named Time magazine's Person of the Year in 2019, she has also spoken openly about her diagnosis of Asperger syndrome, calling neurodiversity her "superpower".

She frequently emphasizes that the climate crisis cannot be solved without addressing capitalism, indigenous rights, and corporate exploitation.

In recent years, she has shifted from organizing student marches to participating in high-profile direct actions, blockade protests, and civil disobedience campaigns across Europe. These actions have occasionally resulted in brief detentions by police in countries such as the UK, Germany, the Netherlands, and Sweden.

She has been nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize multiple times by international lawmakers.

Her work has been documented in books, theatrical releases (like the documentary _I Am Greta_), and she has co-authored _The Climate Book_, a comprehensive anthology features essays from over 100 scientists and experts.
