Technology, Entertainment, Design is the original full name of TED, the global non-profit organization and media platform famous for its "Ideas Worth Spreading" (now "Ideas change everything") conferences and short, impactful talks.

Co-founded by Richard Saul Wurman and Harry Marks in 1984 in Monterey, California, TED began as a one-off conference exploring the convergence of Technology, Entertainment, and Design—three industries that were beginning to overlap through the rise of personal computing and digital media.

After becoming an annual event in 1990, TED was acquired in 2001 by Chris Anderson's non-profit Sapling Foundation. Under Anderson, it broadened its scope far beyond its original three pillars to cover science, global issues, business, psychology, art, and philosophy.

TED began posting its 18-minute-or-less talks online for free in 2006, turning speakers like Sir Ken Robinson, Brené Brown, and Hans Rosling into global phenomena and amassing billions of views worldwide.

Today, TED operates as a global ecosystem of educational and community-driven platforms:

- **TED Talks**. Short, curated presentations limited to a maximum of 18 minutes, designed to communicate a single powerful idea clearly and engagingly.

- **TEDx**. Independently organized local events licensed by TED that bring the conference format to communities, universities, and cities worldwide.

- **TED-Ed**. An educational platform pairing educators and animators to create short, animated lessons for students and lifelong learners.

- **The Audacious Project**. A collaborative philanthropy initiative housed at TED that funds large-scale, high-impact solutions to critical global challenges.
