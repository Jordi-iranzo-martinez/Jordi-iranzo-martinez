
[The Great Green Wall](https://thegreatgreenwall.org) for the Sahara and the Sahel Initiative) is an African-led ecological and economic restoration project launched by the African Union in 2007.

Spanning roughly 8,000 kilometers (5,000 miles) across the width of [[Africa]]—from Senegal in the west to Djibouti in the east—it aims to combat desertification, climate change, drought, and poverty across the Sahel region.

Originally envisioned as a continuous 15-kilometer-wide belt of trees to stop the Sahara Desert from expanding southward, the project quickly evolved into a mosaic of sustainable land-management practices tailored to local ecosystems.

Rather than simply planting tree seedlings—many of which struggled to survive in arid conditions—communities rely heavily on [Farmer-Managed Natural Regeneration](https://fmnrhub.com.au/) (FMNR) (protecting native tree roots already in the soil), zai pits (small planting holes that trap water and compost), and half-moon earthen bunds (_demi-lunes_) that harvest scarce rainwater and recharge groundwater

Backed by over 20 participating African countries and international partners coordinated through the [UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD)](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESYAHEIkXXpWeRN-4304pGcGG9hAqzNW7mktob72wxNfXrKIBsSvtsjdzPhv-imm8TDdLt4TmjhKtZAEe4u9JZKqRZO12xzeSTLtStJV86TfV1WqjMNefQDDbnhXDa51SGJA), the initiative sets three primary goals for 2030:

- **Land restoration**. Restore 100 million hectares (247 million acres) of degraded land.
- **Carbon sequestration**. Capture 250 million tons of carbon dioxide.
- **Green jobs**. Create 10 million green jobs in rural communities to boost food security and reduce forced migration.

While named a [UN World Restoration Flagship](https://www.decadeonrestoration.org/world-restoration-flagships) and supported by over $19 billion in international pledges (via the Great Green Wall Accelerator), implementation remains uneven: 

- **On-the-ground achievements**. Countries like Ethiopia, Niger, Nigeria, and Senegal have restored millions of hectares of land, revived local water tables, and supported women-led agroforestry cooperatives (producing shea, moringa, and baobab).

- **Ongoing hurdles**. Regional security conflicts in the Sahel, funding delays, extreme drought, and gaps in standardized monitoring mean that overall progress remains well short of the 2030 targets, prompting the African Union to adopt an expanded Ten-Year Implementation Framework (2024–2034) alongside a sister initiative across Southern Africa (SADC).
