
The Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew—commonly known as [Kew Gardens]([Kew Gardens](https://www.kew.org))—is a world-renowned botanical garden and scientific research institution located in Richmond upon Thames, southwest London.

Founded in 1759, it spans 132 hectares (326 acres) and houses the world's largest and most diverse living botanical and mycological collections.

Kew is famous for blending historic royal architecture with groundbreaking Victorian glass-and-iron engineering:

- **Palm House**. Built between 1844 and 1848 using wrought-iron techniques borrowed from shipbuilding, this iconic conservatory recreates a humid tropical rainforest filled with palms, cycads, and endangered tropical flora.

- **Temperate House**. Opened in 1863, it is the world's largest surviving Victorian glasshouse, housing rare and threatened temperate plants from Africa, Australia, New Zealand, the Americas, and Asia.

- **Princess of Wales Conservatory**. A modern glasshouse divided into ten distinct computer-controlled climatic zones ranging from arid desert cacti to cloud forest orchids and giant water lilies.

- **Great Pagoda & Kew Palace**. The 50-meter-tall, ten-story Great Pagoda (built in 1762) offers panoramic views over London, while [Kew Palace](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAEScQHEIkXXSIAwFdb-0KSholElpNVcCnNBRsXUq0I720HFrKR2n5tA_0qFO6gJIouWE30FZdU_ifzjb2zA65rl-NeE6xqFLueDlV4upJJGPGIztIjTaa708oAJbH1yRutAlB_rxZHLALayxoWmD9JDs-AS) is the smallest of the British royal palaces and former summer residence of King George III.

- **Treetop Walkway & The Hive**. Visitors can walk 18 meters above ground through the forest canopy on the Treetop Walkway or step inside _The Hive_, a 17-meter-tall aluminum installation that lights up and hums in response to the real-time activity of a living bee colony.

Beyond being a major London attraction, Kew is a powerhouse for international botanical research staffed by over 350 scientists:]

- **Herbarium and Library**. Its herbarium holds more than 8.5 million preserved plant and fungal specimens—including type specimens collected by Charles Darwin and Captain James Cook—supported by a botanical library of over 750,000 volumes.

- **[Millennium Seed Bank](https://www.kew.org/science/collections-and-resources/research-facilities/millennium-seed-bank)**. Managed by Kew at its sister site, Wakehurst (in West Sussex), it is the largest wild plant seed conservation project on Earth, banking over 2.4 billion seeds from tens of thousands of species to safeguard globl biodiversity against extinction and climate change.

 Across the grounds and arboretum, visitors can explore over 50,000 living plants and 14,000 trees.

It was designated a [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Humankind/People/UNESCO]] World Heritage Site in 2003
