Allan Savory (born in 1935 in Zimbabwe) is an ecologist, biologist, and livestock farmer best known for developing Holistic Management, a land management framework designed to reverse desertification through planned livestock grazing.

He studied zoology and botany at the University of Natal (South Africa) and worked as a research biologist and game ranger in the British Colonial Service in Rhodesia (now Zimbabwe), studying ecosystem degradation and wildlife conservation.

He developed this decision-making framework after concluding that overgrazing is driven not by the number of animals, but by the length of time plants are exposed to grazing. His method involves bunching and moving livestock on a planned schedule to mimic the natural behavior of wild herds evading predators, aiming to restore soil health and biodiversity.

He co-founded the Africa Centre for Holistic Management in Zimbabwe (1992) and the Savory Institute in the United States (2009) to promote the methodology globally.

He served as a member of parliament in Rhodesia before going into exile in the United States in 1979 due to his opposition to the government's racial policies. He is the author of Holistic Management: A Commonsense Revolution to Restore Our Environment and his memoir UnSavory (2025).

While he has received international honors such as the Buckminster Fuller Challenge (2010), many mainstream scientists question his claims regarding the capacity of holistic planned grazing to sequester carbon at a global scale and reverse climate change.
