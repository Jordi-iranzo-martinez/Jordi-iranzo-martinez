
福岡 正信 (Masanobu Fukuoka) (1913–2008) was a Japanese farmer, microbiologist, and philosopher celebrated as the pioneer of natural farming (shizen nōhō), often called "do-nothing" farming. 

Through decades of experimentation on his family farm on [Shikoku Island](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Shikoku%2C+Japan) ([[Web/content/Notes/Just One Humankind/Just One World/Japan|Japan]]), he demonstrated that crops could achieve yields equal to modern industrial agriculture without plowing, chemical fertilizers, pesticides, or weeding.

![[Web/content/Visual/masanobu-fukuoka.jpg|424x407]]


Born in Iyo, Ehime Prefecture, Fukuoka studied microbiology and plant pathology, working as an agricultural customs inspector in Yokohama during the 1930s.

After recovering from a near-fatal bout of pneumonia (1937), he experienced a profound realization that nature is self-regulating and that most human agricultural interventions create the very problems they try to solve.

He resigned from his government post and returned to his father's farm to test farming by subtraction—asking _"What if we don't do this?"_ rather than _"What else can we add?"_

His masterpiece, the book [The One-Straw Revolution](https://uk.bookshop.org/p/books/the-one-straw-revolution-an-introduction-to-natural-farming-masanobu-fukuoka/93fe78d9d7fef158?ean=9788185569314&bkshp-astro=t) (1978), became an inspiring, foundational text for worldwide movements like permaculture, organic farming, and regenerative design.

Fukuoka's method centers on working alongside natural ecosystem cycles rather than forcing the land:

- **No tillage** (no plowing). The soil cultivates itself naturally through plant roots, earthworms, and microorganisms.

- **No chemical fertilizers or prepared compost**. Soil fertility is maintained by returning uncut straw to the surface and growing living ground covers like white clover.

- **No weeding by tillage or herbicides**. Weeds are managed—not eradicated—through straw mulch and timing of sowing so they enrich the soil without choking crops.

- **No pesticides**. Pest populations are kept in check naturally by preserving biodiversity and predator-prey balance.

- **No pruning**. Fruit trees are allowed to grow in their natural form rather than being heavily pruned.

To sow seeds without tilling the soil—and to protect them from birds, rodents, and drought—Fukuoka revived and popularized clay seed balls (*nendo dango*), encasing mixtures of crop, clover, and tree seeds inside small pellets of clay and compost.

His 1975 book, [The One-Straw Revolution](https://uk.bookshop.org/p/books/the-one-straw-revolution-an-introduction-to-natural-farming-masanobu-fukuoka/93fe78d9d7fef158?ean=9788185569314&bkshp-astro=t), became a foundational text for the global permaculture and regenerative agriculture movements, followed by _The Natural Way of Farming_ and _Sowing Seeds in the Desert_.

Later in life, he traveled across Africa, India, Southeast Asia, Europe, and North America to demonstrate revegetating arid and degraded lands using seed balls, earning the Ramon Magsaysay Award (1988) and the Earth Council Award (1997).
