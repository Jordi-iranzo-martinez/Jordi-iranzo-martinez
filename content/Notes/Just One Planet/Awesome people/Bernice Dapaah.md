Bernice Dapaah is a social entrepreneur, environmentalist, and the founder and CEO of the [Ghana Bamboo Bikes Initiative](https://ghanabamboobikes.org/) (and Envirotech Bamboo Limited).

Based in [Kumasi](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Kumasi,+Ghana/@6.6900855,-1.6161061,25024m/data=!3m2!1e3!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0xfdb93e59a4e4c49:0x829c711d7b65e682!8m2!3d6.6666004!4d-1.6162709!16zL20vMDI1eTY3?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MTExNy4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D) (Ghana) she is internationally recognized for transforming locally abundant bamboo into durable, eco-friendly bicycles to tackle youth unemployment, rural transportation barriers, and climate change.

Growing up in rural Ghana, Dapaah had to walk several hours each day to attend school until her grandfather gave her a bicycle. Seeing firsthand how reliable transport could protect a child's education—and noticing how imported metal bikes frequently broke down on rugged rural roads—inspired her to look for a stronger, locally sourced alternative.

Shortly before graduating with a degree in business administration from Christian Service University College (she later earned a Master's in Development Management from KNUST and completed executive fellowships at Harvard and Oxford), she co-founded Ghana Bamboo Bikes in the 2009 with Winnifred Selby to create green jobs for young people.

Dapaah's social enterprise combines circular-economy engineering with community development:

- **Sustainable craftsmanship**. Workers harvest, treat, and dry native Ghanaian bamboo to build lightweight, shock-absorbing frames bound together with natural sisal fibers and epoxy resin. The bikes are sold both locally and exported internationally to Europe and North America (including electric models branded as _EcoRide_).

- **Reforestation and climate action**. Because bamboo grows rapidly and sequesters high amounts of carbon without needing replanting after harvest, the initiative fights deforestation and soil erosion—planting 10 new trees for every bamboo culm harvested.

- **Empowering women and students**. Over half of the trained frame-builders are women, gaining technical skills and financial independence. Additionally, through a "buy-one, give-one" and philanthropic model, the organization donates bicycles to rural schoolchildren to reduce dropout rates caused by long daily walks.

She is globally celebrated for transforming an abundant natural resource—bamboo—into eco-friendly bicycles, turning this invention into a powerful tool for sustainable mobility, job creation, and social inclusion in West Africa.

Dapaah serves as a World Bamboo Ambassador and an advisory board member for WIPO GREEN. Her honors include:
- UNFCCC Momentum for Change Light Award (2013)
- World Economic Forum Young Global Leader (2014)
- UNEP / Dubai International Award for Best Practices to Improve the Living Environment (2014)
- WAYA Women Agripreneurs Award (2023)]

She is a member of [World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/people/bernice-dapaah/), was also [interviewed in WIPO Green](https://unfccc.int/climate-action/momentum-for-change/women-for-results/ghana-bamboo-bikes-initiative), the Market place for Sustainable Technology.  She was also cited as an example of [Climate Action in the UNFCCC](https://unfccc.int/climate-action/momentum-for-change/women-for-results/ghana-bamboo-bikes-initiative).
