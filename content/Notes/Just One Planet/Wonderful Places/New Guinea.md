
New Guinea is the world's second-largest island (after Greenland), covering an area of 785,753 square kilometers (303,476 square miles).

*Located in Melanesia in the southwestern [[Pacific Ocean]] just north of [[Australia]]—separated by the 150-kilometer-wide Torres Strait—it is renowned for its extreme linguistic diversity and immense tropical biodiversity.*

New Guinea's biodiversity is among the richest and most unique on Earth. Despite covering less than 0.5% of the planet's land area, the island harbors an estimated 5% to 10% of all known species globally, a vast majority of which are endemic (found nowhere else on Earth).

Comprehensive botanical studies confirm that New Guinea has the highest plant diversity of any island in the world, with over 13,600 documented species of vascular plants (surpassing both Madagascar and Borneo), nearly 70% of which are endemic.
