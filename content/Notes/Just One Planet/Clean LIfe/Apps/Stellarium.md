
[Stellarium](https://stellarium-web.org/) is a free, open-source planetarium software that renders a realistic 3D simulation of the night sky in real time—showing exactly what you would see with the naked eye, binoculars, or a telescope from any location on Earth and at any point in time.

The default installation includes over 600,000 stars (expandable to more than 177 million via downloadable catalogs), alongside over 80,000 deep-sky objects such as nebulae, star clusters, and galaxies from the Messier and NGC catalogs.

It displays the [[Sun]], [[Moon]], planets, major moons, comets, asteroids, and artificial satellites (including the International Space Station), complete with realistic phases, eclipses, and atmospheric light scattering.

You can set your exact geographic coordinates—or simulate viewing from another planet or moon—and fast-forward or rewind time thousands of years into the past or future to observe historical eclipses, planetary alignments, or star drift.

Beyond standard Western constellations, Stellarium includes star lore and constellation artwork from over 40 different world cultures, including Mayan, Chinese, Egyptian, Inuit, Norse, and Arabic traditions.

Amateur astronomers use it in the field thanks to its red-light Night Mode (which preserves dark-adapted vision), customizable ocular/eyepiece views, and direct motorized telescope control.
