
*Thermodesulfobacterium* (written with an "s" after _Thermo-_) is a genus of thermophilic, strictly anaerobic, sulfate-reducing bacteria belonging to the phylum _Thermodesulfobacteriota_ (family _Thermodesulfobacteriaceae_). Originally described in 1983, its name translates to "heat-loving, sulfate-reducing bacterium". 

Key Characteristics

- **Morphology**. They are Gram-negative, non-spore-forming, rod-shaped cells that occur singly, in pairs, or in short chains. Depending on the species, they may be nonmotile (such as _T. commune_) or motile via a single polar flagellum (such as _T. thermophilum_ and _T. hydrogeniphilum_).

- **Unique membrane lipids**. Unlike nearly all other bacteria—which have ester-linked fatty acids in their cell membranes—_Thermodesulfobacterium_ species possess ether-linked membrane lipids (a biochemical feature otherwise characteristic of Archaea) combined with unusual C17 hydrocarbon chains.

- **Thermophilic lifestyle**. They thrive at high temperatures, typically growing between 50 °C and 85 °C (122–185 °F), with optimal growth around 70–75 °C (158–167 °F).

They carry out dissimilatory sulfate reduction, using sulfate (SO₄²⁻), sulfite, or thiosulfate as terminal electron acceptors and reducing them to hydrogen sulfide (H₂S).

Depending on the species, they oxidize molecular hydrogen (H₂) chemolithoautotrophically (fixing CO₂) or utilize simple organic substrates such as lactate, pyruvate, and ethanol—though they cannot oxidize acetate.

They inhabit anoxic, high-temperature ecosystems worldwide, including terrestrial hot springs (such as in Yellowstone, Iceland, Japan, and Iran), deep-sea hydrothermal vents (like the Guaymas Basin), and deep subsurface petroleum reservoirs.
