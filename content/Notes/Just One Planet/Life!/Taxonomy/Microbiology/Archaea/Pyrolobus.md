
Pyrolobus (from Greek *πῦρ*: "fire" and *λοβός* "lobe") is a genus of lobed, hyperthermophilic archaea famous for including some of the most heat-resistant organisms on the planet.

There is only one specie knon: *Pyrolobus fumarii*, hich live in deep-sea black smoker specialists.

_Pyrolobus fumarii_ thrives optimally at a temperature of 113°C (235°F) and can continue to multiply up to 118°C. In fact, if the temperature drops below 90°C (194°F), the organism stops growing entirely, as it requires extreme heat for its metabolism to function.

This microorganism is so resilient that it can survive a full hour inside a laboratory autoclave at 121°C (250°F)—the standard medical sterilization process designed to destroy virtually all other forms of life.

It was originally discovered on the walls of deep-sea hydrothermal vents ("black smokers") along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, over 3,000 meters below the surface. This environment is completely dark, under crushing pressure, and saturated with toxic chemical compounds.
