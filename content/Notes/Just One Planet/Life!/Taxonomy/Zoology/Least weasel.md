
The least weasel (*Mustela nivalis*) is the world's smallest living carnivoran and a fierce, hyper-active predator native to [[North America]], [[Europe]], and [[Asia]]. 

[[Carnivores]]

Despite weighing less than a deck of cards, it is a fearless hunter capable of taking down prey much larger than itself.

Adults measure just 13 to 26 cm (5 to 10 inches) in body length (excluding a short tail of 1.5 to 8 cm) and typically weigh between 25 and 250 grams (0.9 to 8.8 ounces), with males being noticeably larger than females. Its long, slender body and short legs allow it to slip effortlessly into underground rodent tunnels.

It has a chestnut-brown back and a pure white belly separated by a jagged or straight line. In northern populations with heavy winter snow, the least weasel molts completely into a pure white winter coat for camouflage (unlike the closely related stoat/ermine, it lacks a black tip on its tail).

Because its long, thin shape loses body heat quickly, the least weasel has an exceptionally fast metabolism and must eat roughly one-third to one-half of its own body weight every day just to survive.

It hunts day and night, pursuing voles, mice, lemmings, and shrews directly inside their subterranean burrows. It kills prey swiftly with a precise bite to the base of the skull.

Despite its miniature size, it regularly hunts rabbits and rats several times heavier than itself, and occasionally eats birds, eggs, lizards, and insects.

The least weasel has a circumpolar range across Eurasia and [[North America]] (and was introduced to New Zealand), thriving in open fields, meadows, hedgerows, farmland, tundra, and woodland edges where small rodents are abundant.

*[[Europe]], [[Asia]]*

Solitary and fiercely territorial, least weasels breed rapidly when rodent populations boom, sometimes raising two litters of 4 to 6 kits in a single year. In the wild, their lifespan is brief, typically lasting 1 to 2 years due to predation by owls, hawks, foxes, and cats.
