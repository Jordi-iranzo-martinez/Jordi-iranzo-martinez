The Ceratosaurus (*Ceratosaurus nasicornis*)

Ceratosaurus (from Greek keras "horn" and sauros "lizard," meaning "horned lizard") is a genus of medium-to-large carnivorous theropod dinosaur that lived during the Late [[Web/content/Notes/Jurassic]] Period (approximately 153 to 148 million years ago).

It is instantly recognizable by its prominent nasal horn, deep skull, and row of bony armor plates running down its back.



[[Reptiles]]

[[Dinosaurs]]

• Head & Horns. Possessed a disproportionately large, deep skull sporting three distinct horns: a prominent, blade-like horn on the nasal bones and a pair of smaller horn-like ridges over the eyes (formed by the lacrimal bones). Because these bony cores were relatively thin and fragile, they were likely used for species recognition and visual display rather than combat.
• Blade-Like Teeth. Equipped with exceptionally long, thin, serrated teeth—some measuring up to 9 cm (3.5 in) in crown length—that projected well below the lower jaw when the mouth was closed.
• Body Armor (Osteoderms). Uniquely among Jurassic theropods, Ceratosaurus featured a single row of small dermal armor plates (osteoderms) running down the midline of its neck, back, and tail.
• Size & Proportions. Adults typically measured 5.5 to 7 meters (18–23 feet) in length and weighed between 500 and 1,000 kg, with short, four-fingered forelimbs and a deep, flexible tail suited for propulsion.

Ceratosaurus shared its ecosystem with larger predators like Allosaurus and Torvosaurus. Studies suggest it avoided direct competition by favoring riverine and wetland habitats, preying heavily on aquatic and semi-aquatic animals (such as lungfish, turtles, and crocodilians) alongside smaller dinosaurs.

Fossil remains (assigned to Ceratosaurus dentisulcatus or closely related species) have also been identified in the Lourinhã Formation of Portugal, demonstrating faunal connections across the proto-Atlantic Ocean during the Late Jurassic.

Ceratosauria is an early-diverging clade of theropods that later gave rise to the specialized, short-armed abelisaurids of the Cretaceous (such as Carnotaurus).
