
The Philippine Eagle (*Pithecophaga jefferyi*), also known as the monkey-eating eagle, is one of the largest, most powerful, and rarest birds of prey in the world. It is the national bird of the Philippines, to which it is strictly endemic, meaning it is found nowhere else on Earth in the wild.

[[Philippines]]

[[Birds]]

Females are significantly larger than males, measuring between 86 to 102 centimeters (34 to 40 inches) in length and boasting a wingspan of up to 2 meters (6.5 feet).

Unlike eagles of open plains, its wings are relatively short and broad, paired with a long tail. This aerodynamic design grants the eagle incredible maneuverability to weave through the dense canopy of tropical rainforests at high speeds.

Depending on the island, their main food sources are Philippine flying lemurs (colugos), Asian palm civets, large snakes, monitor lizards, and giant fruit bats.

They have evolved a clever cooperative hunting technique: one member of a breeding pair flies conspicuously near the treetops to distract and terrify a troop of monkeys, while the other launches a surprise ambush from behind to secure the prey.

The Philippine Eagle is classified as Critically Endangered by the [[IUCN]]. It is estimated that fewer than 400 breeding pairs remain in the wild, highly fragmented across just four Philippine islands: Luzon, Samar, Leyte, and Mindanao (which holds the largest population).

Its two greatest threats are:

- **Massive Deforestation**: Illegal logging and agricultural expansion destroy the old-growth primary rainforests they require. A single breeding pair needs a massive territory of roughly 7,000 hectares (17,300 acres) of healthy forest to find enough food to sustain one chick.

- **Poaching and Trapping**: Despite strict laws by the Philippine government—punishing those who harm these eagles with up to 12 years in prison and massive financial fines—they continue to fall victim to illegal traps set for other wild animals or are shot by local farmers.
