
Platyhelminthes (commonly known as flatworms) is a phylum of relatively simple, soft-bodied invertebrates characterized by their flattened, ribbon-like or leaf-shaped bodies. 

With roughly 20,000 known species, they range from microscopic free-living marine worms to massive internal parasites like tapeworms.

Flatworms are among the simplest animals to exhibit bilateral symmetry (a distinct head and tail end) and three embryonic tissue layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm).

They lack a fluid-filled body cavity (coelom); instead, the space between their outer body wall and digestive tract is filled with solid spongy tissue called parenchyma.

Because their bodies are extremely thin and flat, every cell sits close to the exterior. Oxygen and nutrients diffuse directly across their skin, eliminating the need for lungs, gills, or blood vessels.

Free-living flatworms have a single opening (a mouth) that serves for both ingestion and waste expulsion, leading to a branched gastrovascular cavity. Parasitic tapeworms have lost their digestive system entirely and absorb pre-digested nutrients directly through their skin.

Reproduction

- **Hermaphroditism**. Almost all flatworms are simultaneous hermaphrodites, possessing both male and female reproductive organs.

- **Asexual Fission**. Many free-living species can also reproduce asexually by splitting their bodies in half (transverse fission) and regenerating the missing parts.

Major Groups of Flatworms:

- **Turbellaria** (Planarians). Mostly free-living, aquatic flatworms found in oceans, freshwater streams, and moist soil. Planarians are famous in biology for their extraordinary regenerative abilities—cutting a planarian into pieces can result in each fragment growing into a complete new worm.

- **Trematoda** (Flukes). Internal parasites with leaf-shaped bodies and specialized suckers used to attach to host tissues. They often have complex life cycles involving multiple hosts, such as snails and vertebrates (e.g., _Schistosoma_, which causes schistosomiasis).

- **Cestoda** (Tapeworms). Long, ribbon-like intestinal parasites consisting of a head equipped with hooks or suckers (_scolex_) followed by a chain of reproductive segments (_proglottids_). They infect vertebrates, including humans, through undercooked meat or fish.

- **Monogenea**. Small external parasites that primarily attach to the skin, fins, or gills of fish and amphibians using specialized hooked organs.
