Flapjack octopuses (genus *Opisthoteuthis*) are small, gelatinous deep-sea cephalopods belonging to the umbrella octopus family (Opisthoteuthidae).

Named for their ability to flatten their webbed bodies onto the ocean floor like a pancake, these reddish-orange octopuses inhabit cold, dark waters worldwide at depths typically ranging from 130 to over 2,300 meters (430 to 7,700 feet).

[[Mollusks]]

Unlike shallow-water octopuses, their eight arms are connected by an extensive web of skin. They typically reach about 20 cm (8 inches) in mantle length, though some species grow slightly larger.

They belong to the suborder Cirrata, meaning they possess a pair of stubby, ear-like fins on top of their head for steering and tiny, finger-like sensory filaments called cirri lining their single row of suckers.

Adapted to the pitch-black deep sea, flapjack octopuses lack both an ink sac and chromatophores (color-changing skin cells). Instead, their solid red or orange coloration appears virtually invisible in deep water where red light does not penetrate.

*[[Deep Ocean]]*

They drift and swim by pulsing their webbed umbrella-like arms like a jellyfish, flapping their head fins, or using gentle jet propulsion through their funnel. When resting on silty seafloors, they spread out flat to stay inconspicuous.

They feed on small worms, amphipods, copepods, and other tiny invertebrates. Using their cirri and suckers to probe and stir up seafloor mud, they trap prey beneath their webbed arms.
