
Angiosperms (from Greek *angeion* "vessel" and *sperma* "seed") are [[Plants]] with flowers which seeds are enclosed within a protective fruit plants and the most diverse, widespread group of land plants on Earth, comprising around 300,000 known species (roughly 90% of the entire plant kingdom).

They have floer a reproductive structure
- **Flowers:** The definitive reproductive structure. Flowers attract pollinators (such as insects, birds, and bats), which drastically increases the efficiency of genetic transfer compared to wind-reliant gymnosperms.
- **Fruits:** After fertilization, the flower's ovary matures into a fruit. Fruits protect developing seeds and aid in their dispersal via animal consumption, wind, or water.
- **Double Fertilization:** A unique reproductive process where one sperm cell fertilizes the egg to form a diploid embryo, while a second sperm cell fuses with two nuclei to form the **endosperm**—a highly nutritious tissue that feeds the developing embryo.
- **Advanced Vascular System:** They possess highly efficient water-conducting cells called **vessels** (in addition to tracheids), which allow them to transport water and nutrients faster than more primitive plant lineages.
