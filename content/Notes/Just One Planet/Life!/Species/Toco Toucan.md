The toco toucan (*Ramphastos toco*) is the largest and best-known species in the toucan family (Ramphastidae), native to [[South America]].

*[[Birds]]*

Instantly recognizable by its glossy black plumage, bright white throat, and enormous yellow-orange bill tipped with a black spot, it is an iconic symbol of tropical wildlife.

Adults measure roughly 55 to 65 cm (22 to 26 inches) in total length and weigh around 500 to 860 grams (1.1 to 1.9 lbs). Their body is jet-black with a vivid white throat bib, blue skin ringing the eyes, white uppertail coverts, and bright red feathers beneath the tail.

The bill alone measures about 19 to 23 cm (7.5 to 9 inches)—nearly a third of the bird's total length. Despite its massive appearance, it is remarkably light because it is made of a thin outer shell of keratin supported by a spongy, honeycomb-like network of hollow bone fibers.

Beyond reaching fruit on thin outer branches and intimidating nest predators, the toucan's oversized bill acts as a thermal radiator. By increasing or restricting blood flow to the bill's surface, the bird can shed excess body heat in warm weather or conserve heat during cool nights.

Like woodpeckers and parrots, toucans have two toes facing forward and two facing backward, giving them a secure grip on branches.

Unlike many other toucan species that stick strictly to deep, closed-canopy rainforests, the toco toucan prefers more open habitats across central and eastern [[South America]]—including [[Brazil]], Bolivia, Paraguay, northern [[Argentina]], and [[Peru]]. It thrives in savannas (_Cerrado_), gallery forests, woodland edges, palm groves, and orchards.

They feed primarily on fruits (such as figs, passionfruit, and peppers), using their long, serrated bills to pluck berries and toss them back into their throats with a flick of the head. They also supplement their diet with insects, spiders, tree frogs, lizards, and the eggs or nestlings of smaller birds.

Toco toucans travel in small, noisy flocks of up to a dozen birds, flying with an undulating pattern of rapid wingbeats followed by glides. At night, they sleep inside tree hollows, folding their long bills backward over their shoulders and flipping their tails up over their backs to save space. They also nest in natural or woodpecker-made tree cavities, laying 2 to 4 white eggs per clutch.
