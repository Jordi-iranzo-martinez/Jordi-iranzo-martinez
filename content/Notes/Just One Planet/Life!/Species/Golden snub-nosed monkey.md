
The golden snub-nosed monkey (*Rhinopithecus roxellana*) is an Old World monkey endemic to the cold, temperate mountain forests of central and southwestern [[China]] (across Sichuan, Gansu, Shaanxi, and Hubei provinces).

[[Primates]]

Instantly recognizable by its vivid golden-orange fur and bright pale-blue face, it is one of the few primates adapted to survive harsh, snowy winters at elevations up to 3,400 meters (11,200 feet).

Its short, upturned nose has forward-facing nostrils without a protruding nasal bridge—an adaptation believed to protect exposed nasal tissue from frostbite in sub-zero alpine weather.

Both sexes have bare, ice-blue skin around their eyes and nose, framed by thick, long golden-red fur across the shoulders, chest, and back. Adult males also develop fleshy, reddish-pink swellings at the corners of their upper lips.

When winter temperatures plunge well below freezing, troops huddle tightly together in the lower forest canopy or on branches to conserve body heat.

Belonging to the leaf-eating subfamily Colobinae, they have specialized multi-chambered stomachs to digest tough plant fibers. In spring and summer, they eat young leaves, buds, fruits, and flowers; during barren winters, they survive largely on tree bark, pine needles, and lichens.

Golden snub-nosed monkeys live in modular social bands that can merge into massive super-troops of 200 to 600 individuals. Each band consists of multiple "one-male units" (one dominant adult male with a harem of several females and their offspring), alongside separate all-male bachelor groups.

Unlike almost all other Old World monkeys, females regularly nurse and care for one another's infants during the first few months of life, boosting infant survival in harsh mountain climates.

They are listed as Endangered on the [[International Union for the Conservation of Nature]] under CITES Appendix I on the [[CITES Convention]].

The wild population is estimated at roughly 15,000 to 20,000 individuals. Historically hunted for their dense pelts (which were prized for winter coats) and threatened by deforestation and habitat fragmentation, they now receive strict first-class legal protection in China alongside giant pandas in reserves such as Wolong, Foping, and Shennongjia.
