
Slime molds are an informal grouping of diverse, single-celled eukaryotic organisms (formerly classified as fungi, now placed primarily within the supergroup Amoebozoa and related protist clades) that spend part of their life cycle as free-living amoebae and aggregate or grow into macroscopic, multinucleate, spore-producing structures.

[[Amoebae]]

Found globally in damp, shaded terrestrial habitats—such as decaying logs, forest leaf litter, tree bark, and garden mulch—where they play a vital role in nutrient cycling and regulating soil microbial populations.

When environmental conditions become dry or food is depleted, the mobile feeding mass transforms into stationary, often vividly colored fruiting bodies (sporangia) that release wind-dispersed spores.
