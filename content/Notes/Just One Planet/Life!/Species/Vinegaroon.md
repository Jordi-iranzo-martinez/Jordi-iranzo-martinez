A vinegaroon (order Thelyphonida, also known as a whip scorpion) is a harmless, nocturnal arachnid famous for its scorpion-like pincers and its unique chemical defense mechanism of spraying concentrated acetic acid—the main component of vinegar.

*[[Arachnids]]*

Although they belong to the class Arachnida alongside spiders and scorpions, vinegaroons are neither. They have no venom glands and cannot sting.

Their front appendages are modified into thick, spiny pincers used to crush and tear prey, as well as to dig underground burrows.

Like all arachnids, they have eight legs, but their extraordinarily thin first pair of legs is not used for walking. Instead, these legs act as delicate sensory feelers to navigate in the dark.

Their abdomen ends in a long, slender, segmented tail called *Flagellum*. When threatened, they raise this whip and spray a defensive mist from glands at its base.

When startled or attacked by predators like birds, lizards, or small mammals, a vinegaroon aims its abdomen and shoots a fine liquid spray containing roughly 85% acetic acid mixed with caprylic acid (which helps the acid penetrate an insect's waxy exoskeleton).

While the spray causes severe irritation to a predator's eyes and mucous membranes—and gives off a strong, unmistakable smell of vinegar—it is harmless to humans unless it gets directly into the eyes or an open cut.

Around 120 species exist worldwide, thriving primarily in tropical and subtropical regions of [[Asia]], [[North America]], [[South America]], and parts of [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Life!/Wonderful Places/Africa]].

*The giant vinegaroon (Mastigoproctus giganteus), native to the southern [[United States of America]] and [[Mexico]], can reach up to 8.5 cm (3.3 inches) in body length (excluding the tail).*

They spend the day hidden inside burrows they excavate under logs or rocks, emerging at night to hunt crickets, cockroaches, millipedes, slugs, and other small invertebrates.

Female vinegaroons are attentive mothers. After laying 20 to 40 eggs inside a sealed burrow, the mother guards them without eating until they hatch, carrying the pale white hatchlings on her back until their first molt.
