Ankylosaurus (*Ankylosaurus magniventris*) is an extinct genus of heavily armored thyreophoran dinosaur that lived at the very end of the Late [[Cretaceous]] period, roughly 68 to 66 million years ago, in western [[North America]]. 

As the largest and best-known member of the ankylosaurid family, it is famous for its tank-like bony armor and massive tail club.

*[[Reptiles]], [[Dinosaurs]]*

_Ankylosaurus_ was a low-slung, wide-bodied quadrupedal herbivore. It measured 6 to 8 meters (20 to 26 feet) long, stood roughly **1.7 meters (5.6 feet) tall** at the hips, and weighed between 4.5 and 8 metric tons.

Its back, neck, and sides were covered in thick, oval plates of bone embedded directly in the skin (osteoderms), surrounded by smaller scales and triangular horns jutting from the back of its skull. Even its eyelids were protected by small bony plates.

The tip of its tail ended in two large lobes of bone fused to the final tail vertebrae, while the rear half of the tail was stiffened by ossified tendons to act like the handle of a sledgehammer. Swung laterally with powerful tail-base muscles, this club could deliver enough impact force to shatter the leg bones of large predators like _Tyrannosaurus rex_.

Its wide, triangular skull housed a relatively small brain and ended in a toothless keratinous beak for cropping plants. CT scans revealed intricate, looping nasal passages inside its snout that likely acted as heat exchangers to warm and humidify inhaled air, enhanced its sense of smell, and produced deep resonant vocalizations.

_Ankylosaurus_ inhabited the lush coastal floodplains and forests of Late Cretaceous North America (known from the Hell Creek, Lance, and Scollard formations), living alongside _Tyrannosaurus_, _Triceratops_, and _Edmontosaurus_ right up until the Cretaceous–Paleogene mass extinction.

Its small, leaf-shaped teeth were suited for snipping low-growing vegetation—such as ferns, shrubs, and early flowering plants—rather than heavy chewing. Instead, it swallowed large volumes of plant matter that fermented slowly inside its massive, barrel-shaped gut (giving rise to its species name _magniventris_, meaning "great belly").

With a very low center of gravity, _Ankylosaurus_ was nearly impossible for predators to flip onto its unarmored belly. When threatened, it likely crouched low to protect its legs and swung its heavy tail club to keep carnivores at bay.
