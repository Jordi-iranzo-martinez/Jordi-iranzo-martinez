The Barn Owl (*Tyto alba*) is one of the most widely distributed birds in the world, found on every continent except Antarctica. Easily recognized by its stark white, heart-shaped facial disc, dark eyes, and ghostly silent flight, it is a specialized nocturnal rodent hunter that frequently nests near farms and rural buildings.

*[[Birds]], [[Europe]], [[Asia]], [[North America]], [[South America]], [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Life!/Wonderful Places/Africa|Africa]], [[Oceania]]

The barn owl has a distinct heart-shaped white face framed by a golden-brown border and deep black eyes.

Adults measure 33 to 39 cm (13 to 15 inches) in length with a wingspan of 80 to 95 cm (2.6 to 3.1 feet), weighing only 300 to 500 grams (10 to 18 oz). Their upperparts are a delicate mosaic of golden-buff and ash-grey speckled with fine black-and-white spots, while their underparts are pure white (or warm buff in some regional subspecies).

They have unusually long, featherless lower legs and sharp talons adapted for reaching through tall grass to snatch hidden rodents.

Barn owls have the sharpest low-light vision of any terrestrial animal, yet they can hunt in 100% pitch darkness using hearing alone.

Their heart-shaped facial disc acts like a satellite dish, funneling faint sounds directly into their ears. Because their ear openings are placed asymmetrically—one higher on the skull than the other—the owl's brain calculates the tiny time difference between sounds reaching each ear to pinpoint a mouse's exact horizontal and vertical position beneath grass or leaves.

Soft, velvety feather surfaces and comb-like serrated edges on their leading wing feathers break up air turbulence, allowing them to glide completely silently without alerting prey or drowning out faint sounds with their own wingbeats.
