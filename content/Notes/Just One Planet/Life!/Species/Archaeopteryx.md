*Archaeopteryx* (meaning "ancient wing" or "ancient feather") is an extinct genus of raven-sized, feathered theropod dinosaurs that lived during the Late [[Web/content/Notes/Jurassic]] period, about 150 million years ago, and is famous as the iconic evolutionary link between non-avian dinosaurs and modern birds.

*[[Birds]], [[Dinosaurs]]*

Roughly the size of a modern crow or raven—measuring about 50 cm (1.7 feet) long and weighing 0.8 to 1 kg (1.8 to 2.2 pounds)—_Archaeopteryx_ combined reptilian features (jaws lined with sharp teeth, three clawed fingers on each wing, and a long bony tail) with avian traits (a wishbone/furcula and asymmetrical flight feathers).

Unlike modern birds, it lacked a deeply keeled breastbone (sternum) to anchor massive flight muscles. Biomechanical studies, including [2025–2026 research on the nearly complete "Chicago _Archaeopteryx_"](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESgQEBxCJF1zPl0mnjyfF_XvU3iXCN3LuRI2h-SzuE1EzMCe5UPfL9keA6V7Lt9CfjqyHosXoK2OQXFGYxvjdvHoAwci3P4QSpiIhJ7t9whDqM6MdpGIOQ6Xr-AUHnrDYPFAnuArAnJEWUI0j4qd9-TwkwILylz0H-uH7S5SqoCMEa9hM), show it had specialized inner-wing feathers (_tertials_) and strong legs—suggesting it was an adaptable generalist that climbed, foraged on the ground, leaped into the air via sequential hops, and engaged in short bursts of flapping or gliding flight rather than sustained soaring.

All known body fossils (about a dozen confirmed specimens, plus an isolated feather) have been found in the fine-grained [Solnhofen limestone deposits](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESdwHEIkXXjYYlelC5Ds2EiuTcfehFJQubxcE65pFUBViyGKKY5zoEOR_lRkv6-FqWrJnCKmI2shwJ2mYgJumaAhJpKiGHLW-hU7BfB5MQsvxqajiOQySeq7FW9uTB30XBX3dqFXMXGCg_lDb5OirjMQ8X9GZeuDVo) of Bavaria, southern Germany. During the Late Jurassic, this region was a tropical archipelago of hypersaline, oxygen-poor lagoons that preserved delicate feather impressions and soft tissues in extraordinary detail.

The first skeletal specimen (the "London specimen") was unearthed in 1861—just two years after Charles Darwin published _On the Origin of Species_. Its blend of reptile and bird anatomy provided the earliest and most compelling fossil confirmation of evolutionary transitions, championed by biologist Thomas Henry Huxley.

*Archaeopteryx* inhabited dry, scrubby coastal islands dotted with low conifers and cycads, sharing the skies with pterosaurs and the waters with marine reptiles and horseshoe crabs.

Its small, conical teeth and agile limbs were well suited for snapping up insects, spiders, beetles, and small lizards on the ground or among low branches.
