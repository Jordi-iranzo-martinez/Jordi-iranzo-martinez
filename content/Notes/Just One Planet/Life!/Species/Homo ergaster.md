*Homo ergaster* ("working man") is an extinct hominin species that lived in eastern and southern Africa roughly 1.9 to 1.4 million years ago. It is widely regarded as the first representative of our genus (Homo) to exhibit essentially modern human body proportions and an adaptive lifestyle suited for open landscapes.


- **Modern Proportions.** Unlike earlier hominins such as _Homo habilis_, _Homo ergaster_ had long legs, shorter arms, and narrow hips. This anatomy was specialized for efficient bipedal walking and long-distance running across the open savanna.

- **Height and Build.** Adults stood between 1.60 and 1.80 meters (5 ft 3 in to 5 ft 11 in) tall and weighed roughly 50 to 65 kg (110 to 143 lbs).

- **Skull and Brain.** Its cranial capacity ranged from 800 to 900 cm³, notably larger than that of earlier species. It featured a prominent brow ridge, a projecting nose similar to modern humans, and smaller teeth and jaws.

- **Turkana Boy.** The most famous specimen of this species is the nearly complete skeleton of an adolescent boy aged around 11 ([KNM-WT 15000](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESZgHEIkXX1AS9jX4bS4UhJGGd7uLuWut5SqkpaIqVnZhJVZA_QEIZo4IjZam-ZCPfX8VDm1QUITxtC_G7BjeklhapIJtu-foQCKlDmlNG2DYL57l1ZGk6XFWLBurPhCA1lGcvrMKaeA)), discovered in 1984 near Lake Turkana in Kenya, who already stood 1.60 meters (5 ft 3 in) tall.

Homo ergaster pioneered Mode 2 (Acheulean) stone technology, best known for handaxes (bifaces) symmetrically flaked on both sides. Crafting these tools required advanced spatial planning and manual dexterity.

It was an omnivore that incorporated significantly more meat and bone marrow into its diet, providing the caloric energy needed to fuel a larger brain. Some archaeological sites suggest early opportunistic use or control of fire, though scientists still debate how widespread this was.

Populations descended from or closely related to Homo ergaster were likely the first hominins to migrate out of Africa into Eurasia around 1.8 million years ago, giving rise to Asian *[[Homo erectus]]* and the earliest human inhabitants of Europe.
