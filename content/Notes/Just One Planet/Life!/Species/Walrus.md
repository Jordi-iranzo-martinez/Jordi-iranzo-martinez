The walrus (*Odobenus rosmarus*) is a large flippered marine mammal (pinniped) native to the [[Arctic Ocean]] and subarctic seas of the Northern Hemisphere. 

The sole living species in the family Odobenidae, it is instantly recognizable by its massive bulk, prominent ivory tusks, and bristled mustache. Odobenus means "tooth-walker"

Subspecies and Range

- **Atlantic walrus** (*Odobenus rosmarus rosmarus*). Inhabits coastal areas from the Canadian [[Arctic]] and [[Greenland]] to Svalbard and the western Russian Arctic.
	*[[Canada]], [[Russia]]*

- **Pacific walrus** (*Odobenus rosmarus divergens*). Larger and more numerous than the Atlantic subspecies, migrating seasonally between the Bering and Chukchi seas off [[Alaska]] and eastern [[Siberia]]. _(A third population in the Laptev Sea is generally classified as a westernmost branch of the Pacific walrus)._

Adult Pacific males regularly reach 2.7 to 3.6 meters (9–12 ft) in length and weigh 800 to 1,700 kg (1,760–3,750 lbs) (with exceptional bulls exceeding 2,000 kg), while females weigh roughly 400–1,250 kg. A thick layer of blubber up to 15 centimeters (6 in) deep insulates them against freezing water.

Their thick, wrinkled skin shifts color with blood flow: in icy water, blood vessels constrict to conserve heat, turning the walrus pale grey-white; when sunbathing on ice or beaches, blood rushes to the skin to release excess heat, turning them reddish-pink.

Both males and females grow elongated upper canine teeth that can reach 1 meter (3.3 ft) in length. Rather than digging for food, tusks are used to haul their heavy bodies out of the water onto sea ice, maintain breathing holes in ice, establish social dominance, and defend against predators.

Arranged in dense rows across a broad snout, their 400 to 700 sensitive whiskers (*vibrissae*) are connected to rich nerve endings, allowing walruses to feel and identify prey buried in dark, muddy seafloors.

Walruses dive to shallow continental shelves (typically 10–80 meters deep) and feed primarily on benthic bivalve mollusks (such as clams, cockles, and mussels), alongside marine worms, snails, and sea cucumbers. Using their powerful tongue like a piston, they create a vacuum to suck the soft meat straight out of clam shells without crushing them.

Highly social animals, walruses haul out in dense, noisy herds of hundreds or thousands of individuals on sea ice floes or rocky beaches. Air sacs in their throats act like natural flotation pillows so they can sleep vertically in open water, and also amplify bell-like mating calls underwater.

Listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List, walruses rely heavily on drifting sea ice as a resting platform close to offshore feeding grounds. As Arctic sea ice retreats rapidly in summer, tens of thousands of walruses are forced onto crowded coastal beaches, increasing energy expenditure, competition for nearshore food, and fatal stampedes.
