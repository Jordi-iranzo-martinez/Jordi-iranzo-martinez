Pterosaurs (Pterosauria) were an extinct clade of flying reptiles that lived during the [[Mesozoic]] Era, from the Late [[Triassic]] to the end of the [[Cretaceous]] period (roughly 228 to 66 million years ago).

*The word "pterodactyl" is an informal term often used for any flying reptile. Scientifically, it refers specifically to Pterodactylus, just one of more than 130 known pterosaur genera.*

*[[Reptiles]]*

They were the earliest vertebrates known to evolve powered flight, predating both birds and bats.

Pterosaurs were not dinosaurs. They belong to a closely related evolutionary branch of reptiles called archosaurs.

Pterosaur wings were formed by a membrane of skin, muscle, and tough fibers stretching from their ankles to a single, dramatically elongated fourth finger.

To stay light enough for flight, pterosaurs evolved thin-walled, hollow bones reinforced with internal struts and filled with air sacs.

Fossil evidence shows that pterosaurs were warm-blooded and covered in hair-like filaments called pycnofibers, which helped regulate their body temperature and streamline them for flight.

When on the ground, pterosaurs folded their wings upward and walked efficiently on all fours, using their folded wing-hands as front limbs.
