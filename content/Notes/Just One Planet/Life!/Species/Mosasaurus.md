*Mosasaurus* is an extinct genus of massive, carnivorous marine lizards that ruled the oceans during the Late [[Cretaceous]] period, roughly 82 to 66 million years ago. 

*[[Reptiles]]*

Despite often being mistaken for a dinosaur, it was actually a giant squamate—a marine reptile closely related to modern monitor lizards and snakes.

The largest species, *Mosasaurus hoffmannii*, grew up to 13 to 17 meters (43 to 56 feet) in length and weighed roughly 10 to 15 metric tons, making it one of the largest marine predators ever to live.

Unlike earlier mosasaurs that swam like eels, Mosasaurus had a streamlined, barrel-shaped torso, four paddle-like flippers for steering, and a long muscular tail ending in a crescent-shaped fluke (similar to a shark's tail) for powerful bursts of speed.

Its massive skull featured a flexible lower jaw that could widen to swallow large prey, along with a second row of pterygoid teeth on the roof of its mouth to grip slippery victims and prevent them from escaping.

Mosasaurus inhabited the shallow, warm epicontinental seas of [[North America]] (the Western Interior Seaway), [[Europe]], [[Africa]], and [[Antarctica]]. Because it breathed air like a whale, it hunted primarily near the ocean's surface.

Fossilized stomach contents and bite marks show that _Mosasaurus_ ate almost anything it could catch, including large fish, sharks, sea turtles, ammonites, plesiosaurs, seabirds, and even smaller mosasaurs.

Like modern sea snakes and marine mammals, mosasaurs were viviparous, giving birth to live young in the open ocean rather than coming ashore to lay eggs.
