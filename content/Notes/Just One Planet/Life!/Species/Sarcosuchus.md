Sarcosuchus ("flesh crocodile") is an extinct genus of giant crocodylomorph that lived during the Early [[Cretaceous]] period (around 112 million years ago) in what is now [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Life!/Wonderful Places/Africa]] and [[South America]]. Popularly nicknamed "SuperCroc," it was one of the largest semi-aquatic reptiles ever to exist.

The best-known species, *Sarcosuchus* imperator, grew to roughly 9 to 9.5 meters (30 to 31 feet) in length (with older estimates reaching up to 11–12 meters) and weighed between 2.5 and 4 metric tons, nearly twice as long as the largest modern saltwater crocodiles.

Its skull alone measured about 1.6 meters (5.2 feet) long. It featured a long, narrow snout lined with over 100 thick, conical teeth, ending in a bulbous tip called a _bulla_ that likely enhanced its sense of smell or helped amplify vocalizations.

Its back and neck were covered in overlapping bony plates (osteoderms) up to 30 cm (1 foot) long, forming a heavy protective shield from head to tail.

Despite looking very similar to modern crocodiles, _Sarcosuchus_ belonged to a distinct evolutionary branch (the family _Pholidosauridae_). This makes it a distant relative rather than a direct ancestor of today's crocodilians.

Its most famous fossils were discovered in the Ténéré Desert of Niger, which during the Cretaceous was a lush, tropical network of massive rivers, lakes, and floodplains. Remains have also been found in [[Brazil]].

Unlike modern gharials (whose thin jaws are strictly adapted for catching fish), Sarcosuchus had jaws that widened at the tip and delivered an estimated bite force of around 8 metric tons. This allowed it to ambush both giant prehistoric fish and dinosaurs that came to the water's edge to drink.

Analysis of growth rings inside its bony armor shows that Sarcosuchus kept growing steadily throughout almost its entire lifespan, taking 35 to 40 years to reach its full adult size.
