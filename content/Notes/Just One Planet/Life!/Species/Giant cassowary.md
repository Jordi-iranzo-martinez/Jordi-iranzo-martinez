The Giant cassowary (*Casuarius casuarius*) is a massive, flightless ratite bird native to the tropical rainforests of northern [[Australia]], [[New Guinea]], and surrounding islands. 

*While still widespread across parts of New Guinea, the Australian population in Far North Queensland (estimated at around 4,400 adults in the Wet Tropics) is listed as Endangered, threatened primarily by habitat fragmentation, vehicle strikes, and attacks by domestic or feral dogs.*

Standing up to 1.8–2 meters (6 feet) tall and weighing up to 76 kg (167 lbs), it is the heaviest bird in Australia and the third-tallest and second-heaviest living bird in the world, behind only the ostrich and emu.

Both sexes sport a tall, spongy crest made of keratin over a honeycomb core on top of their heads. Scientists have found it acts as a "thermal window"—releasing excess body heat in humid jungles—while also amplifying low-frequency booming calls and helping part dense undergrowth.]

Their bodies are covered in coarse, glossy black, hair-like feathers that shed rain and protect against thorny vines. In sharp contrast, their featherless heads and necks are vivid cobalt blue and red, with two bright red wattles hanging from the throat.

Each three-toed foot features a razor-sharp inner claw up to 12 cm (5 inches) long. Combined with powerful legs capable of running up to 50 km/h (31 mph), jumping 2 meters high, and swimming across rivers, this has earned the cassowary a reputation as the "world's most dangerous bird" when cornered or provoked.

They are primarily frugivores, swallowing fallen rainforest fruits whole—including large or toxic fruits that no other animal can eat. Because their gentle digestive tracts pass seeds intact alongside a pile of fertilizer, they are essential for dispersing the seeds of over 230 rainforest plant species.

Outside of mating season, cassowaries are shy, solitary, and territorial. Females are larger and more dominant than males; after laying 3 to 5 large green eggs, the female leaves, and the male alone incubates the eggs for about 50 days and raises the striped chicks for up to nine months.
