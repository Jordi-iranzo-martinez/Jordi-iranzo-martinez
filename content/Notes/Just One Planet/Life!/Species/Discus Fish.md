The discus fish (genus *Symphysodon*) are a group of brightly colored, laterally compressed freshwater cichlids native to the [[Amazon]] River basin in [[South America]], belonging to the cichlid family (Cichlidae).

*[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Life!/Taxonomy/Zoology/Fishes|Fishes]]*

- **Wild species.** Taxonomists generally recognize three primary wild species (though exact classification has been debated):
    
    - **Heckel discus** (*Symphysodon discus*). Native to the Rio Negro and nearby blackwater rivers, easily recognized by a prominent, extra-dark **fifth vertical stress bar** running down the middle of its body.

    - **Blue / Brown discus** (*Symphysodon aequifasciatus*). Found in the eastern and central Amazon basin, featuring nine equally dark vertical bars and base tones ranging from warm brown to metallic blue-turquoise.

    - **Green discus** (*Symphysodon tarzoo*). Inhabiting the western Amazon (such as the Tefé and Nanay rivers), distinguished by bright red spots across its flanks and anal fin.

- **Domestic color strains.** Decades of selective breeding have produced dozens of vivid aquarium varieties—including Blue Diamond, Pigeon Blood, Red Turquoise, Leopard, Marlboro Red, and White Butterfly.

Adults grow to 15–20 centimeters (6–8 in) in diameter and length, with tall dorsal and anal fins that merge into a round profile ideal for slipping effortlessly among submerged tree roots and aquatic vegetation.

Like many cichlids, monogamous pairs clean a vertical surface (such as a broad leaf or driftwood) to lay and guard their eggs. Once the fry hatch and become free-swimming, both parents secrete a nutrient-rich epidermal mucus from their sides, and the baby fish gather around the parents' flanks to feed on it for their first few weeks of life.

In the wild, discus inhabit quiet, warm, slow-moving tributaries, floodplain lakes (_igarapés_), and flooded forests (_igapós_) with soft, acidic water stained dark by tannins from decaying leaves.

Because of their tropical origins and sensitivity to water quality, keeping discus successfully requires strict conditions:

- **Warm water**. Temperatures between 28 °C and 30 °C (82–86 °F)—warmer than most community tropical tanks.

- **Pristine water parameters**. Soft, slightly acidic to neutral water (pH 6.0–7.0), powerful biological filtration, and frequent water changes.

- **Social needs**. Peaceful schooling fish that thrive best in groups of five or more in spacious aquariums (at least 200–300 liters / 55–75+ gallons) with calm tank mates like cardinal tetras, rummy-nose tetras, and _Corydoras_ catfish.
