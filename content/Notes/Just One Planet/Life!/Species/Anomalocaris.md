*Anomalocaris* (meaning "unusual shrimp") is an extinct genus of giant marine predators that lived during the [[Cambrian]] period, roughly 520 to 499 million years ago, and is widely recognized as one of Earth's first apex predators.

While most animals during the Cambrian Explosion were only a few centimeters long, *Anomalocaris* grew up to 60 to 100 cm (2 to 3.3 feet) in length (including its frontal appendages and tail fan), making it the largest known animal of its era.

It possessed a pair of flexible, 14-segmented frontal limbs lined with sharp spines for seizing prey, positioned in front of a distinctive, disk-shaped mouth (_oral cone_) made of overlapping, serrated plates arranged like a pineapple ring.

It had large, stalked compound eyes containing over 16,000 to 24,000 individual lenses—giving it remarkably sharp vision for tracking fast-moving targets—along with a flattened, segmented body lined with overlapping lateral flaps and a three-paired tail fan for agile, undulating swimming.

*When isolated fossil parts were first discovered in Canada's famous [Burgess Shale](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESfQHEIkXX7VJtFPxL2dU1A7nZHHSzm9kfu86CqYTVjLsiGEwvkHqVBlh2pcUZWWZzNeJEKcfzJ0YqaQlq4ZXLOfOIeGZqHl_F7H8O36oVoOBW5hjoFaGK76r1gQpT1TZHD7u4fcHgXtlDOMua9wIGgycAJH3IUhG9Tgunm2hD) in the late 19th century, paleontologists mistook them for three completely different creatures: a frontal appendage was named Anomalocaris (thought to be the abdomen of a headless shrimp), the circular mouth was classified as a jellyfish (Peytoia), and the soft body was identified as a sea sponge (Laggania).*

*In the 1980s, paleontologists Harry Whittington and Derek Briggs analyzed newly prepared complete specimens and realized all three fossils were actually anatomical parts of a single, extraordinary stem-group arthropod. Fossils have since been found in Canada, China, Australia, and the United States.*

For decades, _Anomalocaris_ was thought to hunt hard-shelled trilobites by cracking open their armor. However, biomechanical modeling and finite element analysis (including [2023 research from the American Museum of Natural History](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESfAHEIkXXP7G_G2W55lf8Bm0WkeSZoh9KJ0Wjg-WhsmJwe-Am_ZSVMLw3SzrMNBke7f8ccvuWr8x_Ge_LepQUbOZJvwiL9dpHZeibVdrSH-WqpikL2dRGhX_D2mrGtvP9pzp-XNZ8VpR6dTQnYbHRsrCk198o2KZk50dNHcE)) revealed that its spiky appendages were relatively delicate and would have been damaged by hard shells.

Scientists now believe _Anomalocaris_ was a fast, mid-water predator that used its exceptional eyesight and speed to dart after soft-bodied prey swimming in the water column rather than crushing armored seafloor creatures.
