The white-spotted pufferfish (*Torquigener albomaculosus*) is a small marine fish native to the waters off [[Japan]]'s Ryukyu Islands, famous for creating intricate geometric sand circles on the seafloor.

*[[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Life!/Taxonomy/Zoology/Fishes|Fishes]]*

Adults reach only about 10 to 12 cm (4 to 4.7 inches) in length. They have a brownish back covered in tiny white spots—earning them the Japanese name Amami hoshizorafugu, meaning "starry sky pufferfish"—and a silvery-white belly

They inhabit sandy seafloors at depths of 10 to 30 meters (33 to 98 feet).

To attract a mate, a male spends seven to nine days swimming along the seafloor, flapping his fins and dragging his belly to carve a circular nest up to 2 meters (6.6 feet) wide.

The nest features radiating outer ridges and valleys surrounding a smooth, maze-like central zone. Males also clear away debris and decorate the peaks with small shells and coral fragments.

The outer ridges slow down ocean currents and channel fine sand particles toward the center. This keeps the water calm and well-oxygenated for the eggs.

Females inspect the completed sand circles and choose a partner based on the nest's symmetry and the quality of the fine sand gathered in the center.

Once a female selects a nest, the pair spawns in the center. The male then stays behind to guard and fan the fertilized eggs for about five days until they hatch.

Males never reuse an old nest. Ocean currents quickly flatten the sand, so the male starts building a brand-new circle from scratch for his next mate.
