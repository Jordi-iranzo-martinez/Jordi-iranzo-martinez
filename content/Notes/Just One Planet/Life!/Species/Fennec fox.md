The fennec fox (*Vulpes zerda*) is a small crepuscular and nocturnal fox native to the [[Sahara]] Desert and parts of the Sinai and Arabian peninsulas.

It is the smallest of all wild canids, instantly recognizable by its disproportionately large ears, which serve both to dissipate excess body heat and to detect subterranean prey.

[[Mammals|Mammals]]

Measuring 10 to 15 cm (4–6 inches)—nearly half the length of its body—its ears radiate body heat through an extensive network of blood vessels close to the skin and provide acute hearing capable of detecting insects and small rodents moving beneath the sand.

Adults measure 24 to 41 cm (9.5–16 in) in head-and-body length (plus an 18–31 cm black-tipped tail) and weigh just 0.8 to 1.6 kg (1.8–3.5 lb).

Its thick, cream-colored fur reflects intense solar radiation during the day while insulating against freezing desert nights. Dense fur also covers the soles of its paws, protecting them from scorching sand and providing traction on loose dunes.

Its kidneys are highly specialized to restrict water loss, allowing the fennec fox to survive indefinitely without drinking free water by extracting moisture from food and dew inside its burrow.

Inhabits sandy deserts and arid scrublands across North Africa. To escape extreme daytime heat, it digs extensive underground burrows—often beneath desert shrubs for root stability—that can reach over 10 meters in length.

Active primarily at dusk and night, it feeds on insects (such as locusts and beetles), small rodents, lizards, birds, eggs, desert roots, and fruits.

Unlike most solitary fox species, fennec foxes live in small family groups of up to 10 individuals consisting of a mated pair and their offspring, communicating through a wide repertoire of barks, purrs, chatters, and whimpers.

It is protected under Appendix II on the [[CITES Convention]]
