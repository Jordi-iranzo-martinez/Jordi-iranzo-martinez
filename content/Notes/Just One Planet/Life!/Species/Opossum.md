The Opossum (*Didelphis virginiana*) is the only marsupial native to [[North America]] (north of [[México]])
[[Mammals]]

Females give birth to tiny, underdeveloped babies (the size of a jellybean) after a gestation period of just 11 to 13 days.

The blind newborns must crawl from the birth canal into the mother’s abdominal pouch, where they latch onto a teat and continue growing for about two months. Once older, they climb out and ride piggyback on their mother's back.

Their most famous trait is their defensive mechanism of faking death when facing a predator (like a [[Coyote]] or [[Dog]]). It is an involuntary, physiological reaction triggered by extreme fear.

The opossum drops to the ground, its body goes completely stiff, its eyes lock open into a glassy stare, and its tongue hangs out. To make the ruse perfect, its heart rate drops, it foams at the mouth, and it secretes a foul-smelling green fluid from its anal glands that mimics the smell of a decaying corpse.

Predators, preferring fresh meat, will usually sniff the "dead" animal and walk away. The opossum can remain in this catatonic state for up to four hours before waking up safely.
