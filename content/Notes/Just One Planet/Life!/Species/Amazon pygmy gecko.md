
Amazon pygmy gecko (*Chatogekko amazonicus*) is one of the smallest lizards in the world, growing to a maximum total length of just 24 to 30 millimeters (about 1 inch).

The Amazon pygmy gecko (commonly referring to *Chatogekko amazonicus*, also known as the Brazilian pygmy gecko, or the closely related Pseudogonatodes guianensis) is one of the smallest reptiles in the world.

[[Reptiles]]

Native to the leaf litter of the [[Amazon]] rainforest, it is famous for its microscopic size and water-repellent skin that allows it to float and walk on water.

 Adults of Chatogekko amazonicus reach a maximum total length of just 24 millimeters (0.94 inches), including the tail—small enough to sit comfortably on a human fingertip. (Pseudogonatodes guianensis is slightly larger, reaching up to 5.6 centimeters).

 Its body is covered in millions of microscopic, hair-like spines that trap tiny air pockets. This makes its skin completely waterproof, preventing raindrops from crushing or drowning it and allowing surface tension to keep it afloat on puddles and flooded forest floors

 It has a brownish, speckled coloration that blends seamlessly into dead leaves and forest debris.

Found across northern South America (including Brazil, Colombia, Venezuela, Peru, Ecuador, and the Guianas), it lives among damp leaf litter on the rainforest floor.

Unlike many larger nocturnal geckos, it is active during the day, hunting minuscule invertebrates such as springtails, mites, and tiny insects.

Females lay a single, tiny, hard-shelled egg at a time in hidden crevices within the leaf litter.
