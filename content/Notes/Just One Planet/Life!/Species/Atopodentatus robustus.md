*Atopodentatus unicus* is an extinct genus of bizarre, hammerhead-snouted marine reptile that lived during the Middle [[Triassic]] period, roughly 247 to 240 million years ago, in what is now Yunnan Province, southwestern [[China]].

*The genus name Atopodentatus unicus translates from Latin as "unique strange-toothed". When paleontologists first described the animal in 2014 from a crushed, incomplete skull, they mistakenly reconstructed it with a hooked upper beak split vertically down the middle like a zipper.*
 
Growing nearly 3 meters (9 feet) long, it is recognized as the earliest known herbivorous marine reptile.

The front of its upper and lower jaws flared outward laterally into a wide, flat, hammer-like bar lined with tightly packed, chisel-shaped teeth.

Behind the front chisel teeth, the sides of its jaws were lined with dense rows of fine, needle-like teeth forming a sieve. Paleontologists concluded that Atopodentatus used its wide front jaw like a scraper or vacuum to shear algae and marine plants off rocky seafloors, then closed its mouth and pushed seawater out through its needle-like teeth to trap the plant matter—similar to how baleen whales filter-feed.

An early relative of plesiosaurs and nothosaurs (a basal sauropterygian), it possessed an elongated body, a relatively short neck, a long tail, and robust, clawed limbs capable of both swimming in shallow coastal waters and hauling itself onto shore.
