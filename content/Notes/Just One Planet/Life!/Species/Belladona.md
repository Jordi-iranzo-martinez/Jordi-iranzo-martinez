Belladona or Deadly Nightshade (*Atropa belladonna*) is one of the most famously toxic perennial herbaceous [[Plants]] in the world.

Native to [[Europe]], North [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Audiovisual/Documentaries/Africa]], and Western [[Asia]], it belongs to the Solanaceae (nightshade) family, sharing a botanical lineage with tomatoes, potatoes, and eggplants

Every single part of the belladonna plant is saturated with highly toxic chemical compounds known as tropane alkaloids. The three primary active compounds are:

- **Atropine**: a potent compound that blocks the parasympathetic nervous system.
- **Scopolamine** (Hyoscine): a sedative substance that directly impacts central nervous system signaling.
- **Hyoscyamine**: the primary underlying alkaloid synthesized by the living plant.

These chemicals are anticholinergic agents. They work by aggressively blocking acetylcholine receptors in the body, which completely shuts down involuntary bodily functions like sweating, digestion, pupil constriction, and heart-rate regulation.

Ingesting even a minute amount of belladonna can be fatal. For a healthy adult, consuming just two to five berries or a single leaf can result in death. Children are exceptionally vulnerable.
