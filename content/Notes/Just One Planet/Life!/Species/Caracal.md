
The caracal (*Caracal caracal*) (from the Turkish *karakulak*, meaning "black ear") is a medium-sized wild cat native to Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, and the Indian subcontinent, renowned for its distinctive black-tufted ears and extraordinary ability to leap into the air to catch birds in flight.

Its long, pointed ears are backed with black fur and topped with black hair tufts up to 5 cm (2 in) long. Each ear is controlled by roughly 20 independent muscles, allowing the cat to swivel them like radar dishes to pinpoint hidden prey.

Adults measure 60–90 cm (24–35 in) in head-and-body length with a 20–30 cm tail, weighing between 8 and 20 kg (18–44 lb), with males larger than females. It has a short, dense, uniform reddish-tan or sandy coat with a white underbelly, and powerful hind legs that are noticeably longer than its forelegs.

Evolutionarily, it is most closely related to the serval (*Leptailurus serval*) and the African golden cat (*Caracal aurata*).

[[Desert]]

Caracals inhabit savannas, semi-deserts, arid steppes, and scrublands. Highly adapted to dry environments, they can survive for long periods without drinking standing water, obtaining most of their moisture directly from their prey.

Primarily solitary and nocturnal or crepuscular, the caracal uses its muscular hindquarters to execute vertical leaps of up to 3 meters (10 feet) from a standstill, swatting multiple birds out of the air with its paws before landing. Its diet also includes rodents, hares, hyraxes, and small antelopes.

Asian populations are listed under CITES Appendix I (prohibiting commercial international trade), while African populations are listed under CITES Appendix II. Keeping wild felids like caracals as pets is strictly prohibited across Spain and much of the European Union.
