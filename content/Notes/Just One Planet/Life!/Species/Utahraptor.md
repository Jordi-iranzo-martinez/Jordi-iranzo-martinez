*Utahraptor* (Utahraptor ostrommaysi) is an extinct genus of giant theropod dinosaur belonging to the dromaeosaurid ("raptor") family. 

Living during the Early [[Cretaceous]] period—roughly 135 to 130 million years ago in what is now Utah—it is the largest known member of the raptor family, dwarfing famous relatives like Velociraptor and Deinonychus.

While a real _Velociraptor_ was roughly the size of a turkey, an adult _Utahraptor_ measured 5.5 to 7 meters (18 to 23 feet) in length, stood around 1.8 to 2 meters (6 feet) tall at the hips, and weighed roughly 300 to 500 kg (660 to 1,100 lbs)—comparable to a polar bear.

Like all dromaeosaurids, it carried a massive, curved, retractable sickle-shaped claw on the second toe of each hind foot. In _Utahraptor_, this claw measured up to 24 cm (9.5 inches) along its outer curve, used to pin down and slash prey.

Unlike its smaller, more slender relatives built purely for high-speed sprinting, _Utahraptor_ had shorter, thicker leg bones and a heavily muscled torso built for immense strength and ambush power.

Although direct feather impressions have not yet been found with _Utahraptor_ fossils, phylogenetic bracketing with closely related dromaeosaurids (such as _Velociraptor_ and _Microraptor_) strongly indicates it was covered in feathers, including long wing-like feathers on its arms used for balance and display.

Equipped with serrated, blade-like teeth and powerful grasping hands, Utahraptor was the dominant predator of the Cedar Mountain Formation ecosystem. It likely preyed on armored ankylosaurs (Gastonia), iguanodonts (Hippodraco), and young sauropods.

*In 2001, paleontologists discovered a massive 9-ton sandstone block in Utah containing the articulated skeletons of at least a dozen Utahraptor individuals of varying ages—ranging from juveniles to a large adult—alongside an herbivorous iguanodont. This "predator trap" suggests they either hunted cooperatively in social groups or gathered to scavenge a trapped herbivore before getting stuck in quicksand themselves.*
