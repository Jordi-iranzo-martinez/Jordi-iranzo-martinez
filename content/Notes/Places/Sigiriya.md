Sigiriya, widely known as the Lion Rock, is a 5th-century ancient palace complex and rock fortress located in the Matale District of Sri Lanka's Central Province. Rising nearly 200 meters (660 feet) high from the surrounding jungle, this massive granite monolith is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and is frequently dubbed the "Eighth Wonder of the World" due to its brilliant urban planning, architecture, and engineering.

It was built by King Kasyapa (477–495 CE), who seized the throne from his father and exiled his brother. Fearing a retaliatory attack, he moved the capital from Anuradhapura to this secure location. He transformed the sheer rock into a celestial fortress complete with ramparts, extensive pleasure gardens, and a palace on the absolute summit. After Kasyapa's defeat in battle, it served as a Buddhist monastery before being abandoned to the jungle and eventually rediscovered by British explorer Jonathan Forbes in 1831.
