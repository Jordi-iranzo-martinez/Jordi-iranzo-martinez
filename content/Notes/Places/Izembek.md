
Izembek National Wildlife Refuge is a 311,000-acre protected wilderness area located at the dynamic intersection of the Bering Sea and the Pacific Ocean on the western tip of the Alaska Peninsula.

Established as a federal refuge in 1980, this remote landscape features rugged volcanic mountains, vast tundra plains, and one of the most critical marine habitats in North America.

The absolute heart of the refuge is Izembek Lagoon, a 150-square-mile coastal body of water that hosts one of the largest continuous eelgrass (Zostera marina) beds in the world.

This dense underwater forest creates an irreplaceable ecological bottleneck:

- **The Pacific Flyway Hub:** Virtually the entire global population of Pacific Black Brant (~150,000 birds) arrives at Izembek every autumn to feast on the nutrient-rich eelgrass, building up vital fat reserves before their non-stop trans-oceanic flight to wintering grounds in Mexico.

- **Massive Waterfowl Conflux:** The lagoon also supports nearly all of the world's Emperor Geese and Steller’s Eiders, alongside hundreds of thousands of migratory ducks, shorebirds, and sea otters.

- **Apex Mammals:** The surrounding upland tundra provides a critical habitat for large land mammals, including massive Alaskan brown bears, caribou from the Northern Peninsula herd, and wolves.
