### Soil Taxonomy

by the [[Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations]] and the [International Union of Soil Sciences](https://www.iuss.org/) (IUSS) 

Here is the complete and official list of the **32 Reference Soil Groups (RSGs)** of the **FAO** World Reference Base for Soil Resources (WRB), written in English.

They are organized into the **10 logical sets** defined by the official manual based on the dominant factors managing their formation:

1. Soils with thick organic layers
	1. **Histosols** (HS): Organic soils (peatlands). They form in water-saturated environments where the decomposition of organic matter is extremely slow.
2. Soils with strong human influence
	2. **Anthrosols** (AT): Soils deeply modified or created by long-term agricultural practices, such as continuous manuring or ancient irrigation.
	3. **Technosols** (TC): Soils containing a significant amount of human-made artifacts (rubble, concrete, mine spoils) or sealed by artificial linings.
3. Soils with drastic limitations for root growth
	2. **Cryosols** (CR): Polar and high-altitude soils affected by permafrost (permanently frozen subsoil).
	3. **Leptosols** (LP): Very shallow soils over continuous hard rock or extremely gravelly soils (typical of steep mountain slopes).
	4. **Solonetz** (SN): Soils with a dense subsurface accumulation of clay and an exceptionally high percentage of exchangeable sodium.
	5. **Vertisoles** (VR): Cracking clay soils. They shrink and crack deeply during the dry season and swell when wet, causing a self-ploughing effect
	6. . **Solonchaks** (SC): High-salinity soils showing a massive concentration of soluble salts (salt flats), typical of arid and coastal zones.
4. Soils conditioned by groundwater or surface water
	9. **Gleysols** (GL): Waterlogged soils permanently saturated by groundwater, developing gray, olive, or bluish colors due to lack of oxygen.
	10. **Andosols** (AN): Soils developed from volcanic ash and glass. They are dark, lightweight, and highly fertile.
	11. **Podzols** (PZ): Acid forest soils (typical of coniferous taiga) where organic matter and iron have been chemically leached from the surface and deposited in the subsoil.
5. Tropical and subtropical humid soils (highly leached)
	12. **Plinthosols** (PT): Soils containing a iron-rich clay mixture (plinthite) that hardens irreversibly into a brick-like material upon drying.
	13. **Nitisols** (NT): Deep, clayey, and shiny red tropical soils with highly stable blocky structures.
	14. **Ferralsols** (FR): The ultimate soils of the humid tropics. They are extremely weathered, very old, deep, and rich in iron and aluminum oxides.
6. Soils with clay accumulation in the subsoil
	15. **Planosols** (PL): Soils with a drastic textural contrast: a light-colored sandy or silty surface layer sitting on top of an impermeable clay pan.
	16. **Stagnosols** (ST): Soils where rainwater is temporarily perched or stagnated in the upper layers due to a compact, poorly permeable subsurface layer.
	17. **Alisoles** (AL): Very acid soils with a high-activity clay subsoil heavily saturated with exchangeable aluminum.
	18. **Acrisols** (AC): Strongly weathered, acidic tropical clay soils with very low natural nutrient fertility.
	19. **Luvisols** (LV): Fertile soils with clay accumulation in the subsoil but well-supplied with nutrients. They are highly common in temperate agricultural regions.
	20. **Lixisols** (LX): Similar to Luvisols but found in dry subtropical or savanna climates; they have clay accumulation but low nutrient retention capacity.
7. Soils of the steppes and prairies (Humus-rich)
	21. **Chernozems** (CH): The famous "black earths" of Ukraine and the American Great Plains. Deep, ultra-fertile soils packed with organic humus.
	22. **Kastanozems** (KS): Chestnut-brown prairie soils found in drier grassland steppes, showing secondary calcium accumulation.
	23. **Phaeozems** (PH): Dark, organic-rich soils of humid temperate grasslands (like the Argentine Pampas), lacking calcium carbonate in the upper subsoil.
8. Arid soils rich in cemented minerals
	24. **Gypsisoles** (GY): Arid soils showing a massive accumulation of secondary gypsum.
	25. **Durisoles** (DU): Soils containing a subsurface layer cemented by silica (known as a duripan) that roots cannot break through.
	26. **Calcisoles** (CL): Soils typical of dry environments with a highly visible accumulation of secondary calcium carbonate (caliche or lime crusts).
9. Acid soils of temperate forests
	27. **Retisoles** (RT): Soils where clay accumulates in the subsoil forming a network or interlocking tongues of bleached sand.
	28. **Umbrisoles** (UM): Soils with a thick, dark surface layer that is very rich in organic matter but highly acidic and nutrient-poor.
10. Young soils or those conditioned by topography
	29. **Cambisols** (CM): Moderately developed young soils showing changes in color and structure from the parent bedrock, but without strong material accumulation.
	30. **Fluvisols** (FL): Young soils formed by recent sediments deposited by rivers, seas, or lakes. They are flat and highly fertile for farming.
	31. **Arenosols** (AR): Purely sandy soils (sand dunes, beaches, or desert sands) displaying minimal or no soil horizon evolution.
	32. **Regosols** (RG): Very young soils formed on unconsolidated or soft materials that do not fit into any other group, lacking clear layer differentiation.
