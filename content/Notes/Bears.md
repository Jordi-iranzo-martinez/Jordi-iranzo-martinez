
Bears are large, heavy-bodied carnivoran mammals belonging to the family Ursidae. There are eight living species of bears found across [[North America]], [[South America]], [[Europe]], and [[Asia]], ranging from the tropical bamboo forests of [[China]] to the sea ice of the [[Arctic]].

*[[Carnivores]]*

The 8 Living Bear Species

- [[Brown Bear]] (*Ursus arctos*). The most widely distributed bear species, native to North America (including **grizzly** and **Kodiak** bears), Europe (such as the Cantabrian brown bear in Spain), and northern Asia.

- [[Polar Bear]] (*Ursus maritimus*). The largest living bear species and the only marine mammal among bears, native to the Arctic Circle and specialized for hunting seals on sea ice.

- [[American Black Bear]] (*Ursus americanus*). The most abundant bear species in the world, native to North American forests and capable of climbing trees easily; despite its name, its fur can also be brown, cinnamon, or blond.

- [[Asian Black Bear]] (*Ursus thibetanus*). Also known as the **moon bear** for the white crescent-shaped patch on its chest, native to forested mountains across Asia.

- [[Giant Panda]] (*Ailuropoda melanoleuca*). Native to south-central China, famous for its black-and-white coat and specialized diet consisting almost entirely of bamboo.

- **Spectacled Bear** (*Tremarctos ornatus*). Also called the Andean bear, it is the only bear native to South America, named for the pale, ring-like markings around its eyes.

- **Sloth Bear** (*Melursus ursinus*). Native to the Indian subcontinent, equipped with a long snout, missing upper front teeth, and long curved claws adapted for raiding termite and ant mounds.

- **Sun Bear** (*Helarctos malayanus*). The smallest bear species in the world, native to Southeast Asian tropical rainforests and recognizable by its short black coat, long tongue, and golden chest patch.

Unlike dogs or cats that walk on their toes, bears walk flat-footed on the soles of their feet (_plantigrade_), allowing them to stand upright briefly on their hind legs.

Bears have one of the sharpest senses of smell in the animal kingdom—significantly stronger than a bloodhound's—allowing them to detect food or carcasses from kilometers away.

While polar bears are strict carnivores and giant pandas are nearly strict herbivores, most bear species are adaptable omnivores whose diets shift seasonally among berries, roots, nuts, insects, fish, and mammals.
