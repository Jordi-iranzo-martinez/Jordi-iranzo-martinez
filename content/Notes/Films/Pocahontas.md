Her birth names were Amonute and Matoaka (which meant _"flower between two streams"_), while "Pocahontas" was simply a childhood nickname meaning _"playful one"_ or _"mischievous girl"_

When Captain John Smith arrived in Virginia in 1607, he was a 27-year-old soldier. Matoaka had around 10 to 12 years old.

It is highly believed that Smith misinterpreted a tribal adoption ritual—a staged mock-execution designed to "rebirth" Smith as an honorary member or ally of the tribe.

In 1613, at roughly 17 years old, Pocahontas was lured onto an English ship and kidnapped by Captain Samuel Argall. The colonists held her hostage for over a year to extort her father for food, English prisoners, and stolen weapons. Oral tribal history indicates she suffered severe mistreatment and assault during this captivity.

While being held captive, she was heavily pressured to convert to Christianity, baptized under the name Rebecca, and married to an English tobacco planter named John Rolfe in 1614. They had a son named Thomas.

 In 1616, the Virginia Company brought her to London as a propaganda symbol—essentially "living proof" to show English society that Native Americans could be "civilized". After a few months of attending high-society events, she boarded a ship to return home to Virginia in March 1617. However, she became gravely ill on the ship and died of respiratory failure (likely tuberculosis, smallpox, or pneumonia) at just 21 years old. She was buried far from home in Gravesend, England.
