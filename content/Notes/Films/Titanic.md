On 1912 sinking of the RMS Titanic


Built in Belfast (Northern Ireland) the Titanic was designed to be the largest, most luxurious, and safest ship of its time.

It featured an advanced system of 16 watertbight compartments. Because the ship could stay afloat even if four of these compartments flooded, the press and the public widely declared the Titanic to be "unsinkable."

On April 14 th 1912 the Titanic received multiple wireless radio warnings from other ships about large ice fields and icebergs ahead in the North Atlantic.

Traveling at a high speed of about 22 knots, the lookouts suddenly spotted an iceberg directly ahead. First Officer William Murdoch ordered the ship to turn, but it was too close. The iceberg scraped along the starboard (right) side of the ship, tearing open holes below the waterline across five watertight compartments—one too many to survive.

The Impact (11:40 PM): 


The ship carried only enough lifeboats to accommodate about 1,178 people—roughly half of the total passengers and crew on board (around 2,200). This was legally allowed at the time due to outdated safety regulations.

Captain Edward Smith gave the order to uncover the lifeboats and broadcast distress signals (CQD and SOS). Under the "women and children first" protocol, many lifeboats were lowered into the freezing water only half-filled due to confusion and panic.

As the bow (front) filled with water, the stern (back) rose high into the air. The extreme weight caused the ship to snap in two before plunging into the icy Atlantic Ocean. Most victims did not drown; they died of hypothermia within minutes in the 28°F (-2°C) water.

The ocean liner RMS Carpathia arrived around 4:00 AM after hearing the distress calls. They rescued 705 survivors from the lifeboats and brought them safely to New York.

The disaster led to the creation of the International [Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%201226/volume-1226-I-18961-English.pdf) (SOLAS), ensuring that all ships have enough lifeboats for every person on board and forcing ships to maintain 24-hour radio watches.
