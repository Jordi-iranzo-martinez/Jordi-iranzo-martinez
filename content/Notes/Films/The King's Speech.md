Prince Albert, Duke of York suffers from a severe, lifelong stammer.

When his brother, King Edward VIII, abdicates the throne in 1936 to marry a divorced American socialite, Albert is suddenly thrust into the monarchy as King George VI.

Terrified of public speaking and faced with the dawn of radio broadcasting, his wife, Queen Elizabeth, hires an eccentric, unqualified Australian speech therapist named Lionel Logue. Through highly unconventional physical and psychological techniques, Logue helps the King find his voice.

The emotional peak of the film centers on September 3, 1939, when King George VI must deliver a live, high-stakes radio broadcast to the entire British Empire, uniting his citizens as the nation officially declares war on Nazi Germany, marking the beginning of World War II.
