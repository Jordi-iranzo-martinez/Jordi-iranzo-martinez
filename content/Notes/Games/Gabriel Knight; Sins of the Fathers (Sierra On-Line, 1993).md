New Orleans is a unique blend of French, Spanish, African, and Caribbean cultures has created a captivating atmosphere that influences everything from its architecture to its world-famous music and cuisine.

The Faulkner House Books, located in Pirate’s Alley (right next to St. Louis Cathedral) is where author William Faulkner lived in the 1920s.

Louisiana Voodoo (New Orleans Voodoo) is a legitimate religion based on [[Voodoo]].

Drums were the spiritual heart of Voodoo gatherings, famously held at Congo Square in New Orleans. They were used to call upon the spirits and, during times of slavery, they were indeed used as a sophisticated way to send messages across distances that white overseers could not understand.

Voodoo practitioners believe in a single, supreme god (_Bondye_) and worship powerful spirits called Loa who act as intermediaries.

Damballah is one of the most important and benevolent Loa. He is the sky father, the primordial creator of life, and is represented as a great white serpent or a rainbow.

Marie Laveau was the Voodoo Queen of New Orleans during the 19th century. She was an incredibly powerful, brilliant, and charitable woman. She was a devout Catholic who went to Mass every day, yet she led massive Voodoo ceremonies. To this day, people visit her tomb in St. Louis Cemetery No. 1, drawing three "X" marks on her crypt to ask for her spiritual favor.

A _gris-gris_ is a small cloth amulet bag containing a mix of herbs, oils, stones, or personal items, worn for good luck or protection from evil.
