### Primates taonomy

- Strepsirrhini (Wet-nosed Primates)
	- **Lemuriformes** (Lemurs): Widespread and highly diverse primates endemic exclusively to the island of Madagascar. _Examples: Ring-tailed lemurs, aye-ayes, and mouse lemurs._
	- **Lorisiformes** (Lorises & Galagos): Slow-moving or leaping nocturnal primates native to Africa and Asia. _Examples: Slow lorises and bushbabies._
- Suborder Haplorhini (Dry-nosed Primates)
	- **Tarsiiformes** (Tarsiers): Tiny, nocturnal, leaping primates native to Southeast Asian islands, characterized by massive eyes that cannot rotate in their sockets.
	- **Simiiformes** (Anthropoids / Higher Primates): The dominant primate group, split into two massive geographical lineages:
	    - **Platyrrhini** (New World Monkeys): Native to Central and South America, characterized by flat noses with side-facing nostrils and frequently possessing a prehensile tail used as a fifth limb. _Examples: Capuchins, spider monkeys, and marmosets._
	    - **Catarrhini** (Old World Monkeys & Apes): Native to Africa and Asia, featuring narrow noses with downward-facing nostrils and completely lacking prehensile tails. This group is split into two families:
	        - **Cercopithecidae** (Old World Monkeys): Monkeys with tails that are not prehensile. _Examples: Baboons, macaques, and mandrills._
	        - **Hominoidea** (Apes): Advanced primates completely lacking external tails, possessing wide chests and highly complex brains. This includes the _Hylobatidae_ (lesser apes/gibbons) and the _Hominidae_ (great apes: orangutans, gorillas, chimpanzees, bonobos, and **humans**).
