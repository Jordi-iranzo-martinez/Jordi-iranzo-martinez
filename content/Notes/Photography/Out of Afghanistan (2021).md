### Kabul

Kabul is the capital and largest city of Afghanistan, spanning an area of approximately 106.2 square miles (275 km²). It is located in the eastern part of the country, situated at a high altitude of about 5,900 feet (1,800 meters) in a valley surrounded by the Hindu Kush mountains.

#### Fall of Kabul

In February 2020, the Trump administration signed the Doha Agreement with the Taliban. It set a timeline for the complete exit of Western forces in exchange for counter-terrorism promises. When President Biden confirmed the final, unconditional withdrawal deadline for 2021, it fundamentally shifted the balance of power.

The Afghan National Defense and Security Forces (ANDSF) were heavily trained to operate with U.S. air support and high-tech logistics. When American military contractors were pulled out alongside the troops, the Afghan military lost its ability to maintain its own aircraft, manage supply lines, and repair vehicles. Starved of ammunition, fuel, and air surveillance, many outposts were left completely stranded.

For years, the U.S.-backed central government in Kabul struggled with endemic corruption. Billions of dollars in foreign aid often failed to reach the populace or the front lines. Furthermore, many local commanders listed "ghost soldiers" (fake names on rosters) to pocket their salaries. Because many real soldiers went months without pay, food, or ammunition, their willingness to die for a distant, unaccountable political elite evaporated.

The Taliban capitalized on the psychological blow of the Western exit. They used tribal elders to negotiate with local, isolated Afghan military units, offering them safe passage or cash if they surrendered their weapons instead of fighting. As provincial capitals began falling one after another like dominoes in the summer of 2021, a massive crisis of morale swept through the army, leading to widespread desertions without a single shot being fired.

The Afghan government under President Ashraf Ghani was excluded from the initial U.S.-Taliban Doha talks, which severely weakened its political authority. Up until the final weeks, Ghani's inner circle misjudged the situation, strongly believing that the United States would never actually complete a total withdrawal or leave the capital completely exposed.

On August 15, 2021, as Taliban fighters reached the outskirts of the city, President Ashraf Ghani unexpectedly fled the country via helicopter. His sudden departure completely dissolved any remaining political command, shattered plans for a peaceful, negotiated transition of power, and left the gates of the capital open for the Taliban to march into the presidential palace unresisted.
