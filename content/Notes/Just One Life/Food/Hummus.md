
Hummus (from the Arabic word *ḥummuṣ*, meaning "chickpeas") is a creamy, savory Levantine dip and spread made primarily from cooked, mashed chickpeas blended with tahini (toasted sesame seed paste), lemon juice, and garlic.

Originating in the [[Middle East]]—with documented recipes dating back to 13th-century Cairo cookbooks—it is a staple across Levantine, Mediterranean, and global cuisines.

Traditional hummus bi tahina combines tender chickpeas, rich sesame tahini, fresh lemon juice, raw garlic, salt, and cold water or ice cubes (whipped together to create a smooth, airy emulsion).

Served swirled on a shallow plate, hummus is traditionally finished with a generous drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil, whole chickpeas, chopped flat-leaf parsley, and a dusting of paprika, sumac, or ground cumin.

Across the Levant, warm hummus is frequently served as a main dish topped with spiced minced lamb or beef (*hummus kawarma*), sautéed pine nuts, mushrooms, or fava beans (ful).

It is traditionally scooped up with warm pita bread or flatbreads as part of a mezze platter alongside falafel, tabbouleh, and olives, and is also widely eaten with raw vegetables (crudités) or used as a sandwich spread.

Naturally vegan, gluten-free, and dairy-free, hummus is rich in plant-based protein, dietary fiber, healthy monounsaturated fats (from tahini and olive oil), iron, folate, and magnesium.
