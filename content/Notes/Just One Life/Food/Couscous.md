
Couscous (from the Berber _seksu_ or Arabic _kuskus_) is a traditional staple food of North African cuisine (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, and Libya). 

Although it looks like a grain, it is actually a type of pasta made from crushed durum wheat semolina rolled into tiny granules.

It is rich in complex carbohydrates, provides a modest amount of plant-based protein, and is very low in fat.

Semolina is sprinkled with water and flour, hand-rolled into minuscule pellets, sifted for uniform size, and sun-dried.

Grain comes in three main varieties: 

- **Fine** (often used for desserts or cold salads)
- **Medium** (the standard choice for savory stews)
- **Coarse** (such as Maghrebi _berkoukes_ or Israeli _ptitim_, which resemble small pearl pasta).

Beyond classic durum wheat, couscous is also made from whole wheat, spelt, corn, or legumes for gluten-free diets.

In North [[Web/content/Notes/Just One Planet/Biodiversity/Wonderful places/Africa]], raw couscous is steamed multiple times in a couscoussier (a two-tiered steamer pot) directly over a simmering stew of meat and vegetables, rubbed with olive oil or fermented butter (_smen_) between steamings to keep the grains light and fluffy.

It is traditionally served as a bed for spiced lamb, chicken, or beef stews with vegetables (carrots, zucchini, turnips, squash) and chickpeas, seasoned with _ras el hanout_, cumin, and cilantro. It is also served sweet with cinnamon, raisins, and almonds (_seffa_).

*Because it is derived from durum wheat, standard couscous is not suitable for people with celiac disease or gluten intolerance.*
