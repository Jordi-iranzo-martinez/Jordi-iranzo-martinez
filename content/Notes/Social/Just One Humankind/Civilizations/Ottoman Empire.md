
The Ottoman Empire was one of the most powerful, longest-lasting, and largest empires in world history, spanning over 600 years from its founding in 1299 until its official dissolution in 1922.

At its peak in the 16th and 17th centuries, it controlled vast territories across Southeastern [[Europe]], Western [[Asia]], and North [[Web/content/Notes/Natural/Just One Planet/Films/Africa]].

It originated as a small, independent Turkish principality in Anatolia (modern-day Turkey), founded by a tribal leader named Osman I, from whom the name "Ottoman" is derived.

Led by the 21-year-old Sultan Mehmed II (The Conqueror), the Ottomans breached the legendary walls of Constantinople, bringing a definitive end to the Byzantine Empire. They renamed the city Istanbul and made it their magnificent imperial capital.

Under the rule of Suleiman the Magnificent, the empire reached the zenith of its military power, wealth, and cultural achievement. Ottoman borders stretched from the gates of Vienna to the Persian Gulf, and from Algeria to Yemen.
