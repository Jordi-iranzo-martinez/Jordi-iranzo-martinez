Birmingham is the third-most populous city in Alabama ([[United States of America]]), and serves as the state's industrial, economic, and cultural heart.

Founded in 1871 during the Reconstruction era, the city experienced explosive industrial growth due to its rich deposits of iron, coal, and limestone, earning it the nickname "The Magic City."

By 1963, Birmingham was considered one of the most rigidly segregated and racially violent cities in America.

Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC) launched Project C (for "confrontation"), a campaign of nonviolent direct action involving sit-ins, economic boycotts, and peaceful marches to dismantle the city's Jim Crow laws.

At the height of the anti-segregation protests led by Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., the city's Public Safety Commissioner, Bull Connor, ordered the deployment of police dogs and high-pressure fire hoses against non-violent demonstrators and student marchers
