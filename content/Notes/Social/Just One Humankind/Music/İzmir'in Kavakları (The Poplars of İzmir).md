
Çakırcalı Mehmet Efe (1871–1911) ("Çakıcı,") is arguably the most famous and complex outlaw figure (Zeybek/Efe) in Turkish history. Operating out of the Aegean mountains near İzmir, Aydın, and Ödemiş, he led an armed rebellion against the Ottoman Empire for more than 15 years. 

Born in Ödemiş in 1871, Mehmet’s destiny was shaped by tragedy. His father, Koca Ahmet, was a retired mountain outlaw who had accepted an amnesty from the Ottoman government to live peacefully as a farmer. However, an Ottoman police sergeant named Hasan Çavuş systematically ambushed and murdered the father to prevent any future rebellions.

When Mehmet was just an adolescent, his father's close ally was also murdered. Left with no protection from corrupt authorities, at the age of 22, Mehmet took his rifle and fled to the mountains to hunt down his father's killers, formally starting his life as a Zeybek.

He heavily targeted wealthy landowners, corrupt tax collectors, and usurers who exploited the local peasantry. Then, he distributed the stolen wealth directly to poor villagers, paid the dowries of impoverished young women so they could marry, and physically forced wealthy elites to finance public infrastructure like bridges, water fountains, and mosques

In return for his protection, the local peasants adored him. Whenever zaptiyes (Ottoman military units) marched into the mountains to hunt him, the villagers refused to speak and secretly channeled food, ammunition, and intelligence to Çakırcalı's men.

He is rumored to have killed or ordered the deaths of over 1,000 people during his 15-year reign. He showed no mercy to traitors, corporate spies, or any wealthy targets who refused his financial demands, occasionally utilizing summary executions to keep total authority over the region. 

Unable to defeat him via guerrilla warfare, the Ottoman government was eventually forced to grant him official amnesties and even offered him formal security positions. However, unable to tolerate the passivity of town life, he repeatedly broke his vows and went straight back to the mountains.

In November 1911 during a massive firefight on Karıncalıdağ (Karınca Mountain) near Nazilli, his small squad was surrounded by an elite volunteer zaptiye unit.

Some records state he was hit by a stray bullet, while folklore suggests he shot himself to prevent capture.

Çakırcalı had given his loyal men (kızans) a strict, terrifying death pact: 

>"if I am killed, you must destroy my body so the state cannot identify me and use my corpse to humiliate my family."

Following his death, his men immediately cut off his head and his hands. Because he was known to have a heavily haired chest, they entirely skinned his torso to mask his identity before scattering his parts into the brush and fleeing.

Ottoman forces found the heavily mutilated body. They had to bring his first wife, Iraz, to the morgue in Nazilli. She was only able to officially verify it was Çakırcalı by recognizing a highly specific birthmark located on his back.

Initially dumped in an unmarked trench outside the municipal limits (because he was a criminal) his children successfully petitioned to have his remains exhumed in 1947. He is now buried under an honorable monumental headstone in his ancestral home of Kayaköy, Ödemiş
