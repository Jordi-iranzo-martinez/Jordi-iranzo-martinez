Lisbon (*Lisboa*) is the coastal capital and largest city of Portugal, famous for its steep cobbled hills, pastel-colored buildings, vintage yellow trams, and sweeping views over the Tagus River estuary.

As continental Europe's westernmost capital, it blends centuries of maritime history with a vibrant modern arts and food scene.

Key Landmarks & Neighborhoods

- **Alfama**. Lisbon's oldest district—a labyrinth of narrow medieval alleys beneath São Jorge Castle (Castelo de São Jorge), known for its scenic viewpoints (miradouros) and traditional Fado music houses.

- **Belém**. A waterfront district west of the center showcasing Portugal's Age of Discovery, home to the UNESCO-listed Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower, as well as the original bakery for Pastéis de Belém custard tarts.

- **Baixa & Chiado**. The elegant downtown grid rebuilt after the devastating 1755 earthquake, anchored by the riverfront Praça do Comércio, Rossio Square, and the historic cafés and bookshops of Chiado.

- **Bairro Alto & Príncipe Real**. Historic hilltop quarters featuring boutique shopping, botanical gardens, and Lisbon's liveliest evening bars and restaurants.

- **LX Factory & Alcântara**. A former 19th-century industrial complex beneath the 25 de Abril Bridge transformed into creative studios, street art, independent shops, and restaurants.

Roughly 600,000–658,000 residents live within the city limits, with around 2.8 to 3.3 million in the Greater Lisbon metropolitan area.

Known for azulejos (hand-painted ceramic tiles), melancholic Fado folk singing, and pastéis de nata (warm egg-custard tarts dusted with cinnamon).

Often called the "City of Seven Hills," Lisbon enjoys a mild Mediterranean climate with over 290 sunny days a year and easy train access to nearby Sintra and the beaches of Cascais.
