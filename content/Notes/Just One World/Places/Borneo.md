
[Borneo](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Borneo/@1.4293648,114.0550177,1612044m/data=!3m2!1e3!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x3200a51b0ce8057b:0xf81f7b0a97d0b88a!8m2!3d0.9618834!4d114.5548495!16zL20vMDFmenA?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MTAyNy4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D) is the third-largest island in the world and the largest in [[Asia]], located in the Malay Archipelago of Southeast Asia. Known for its ancient tropical rainforests and extraordinary biodiversity, the island is uniquely divided among three sovereign nations.

Borneo is split between Indonesia (which controls roughly 73% of the island, a region known as Kalimantan), Malaysia (about 26%, comprising the states of Sabah and Sarawak in the north), and the tiny, oil-rich sultanate of Brunei (about 1% on the northern coast).

The island's interior is dominated by rugged mountain ranges, vast river systems (such as the Kapuas and Mahakam), and dense equatorial jungle. Its highest peak is Mount Kinabalu in Sabah, Malaysia, rising to 4,095 meters (13,435 feet).

Borneo's tropical rainforest is estimated to be around **140 million years old**, making it one of the oldest on Earth—even older than the Amazon.

The island is one of only two places in the world (alongside Sumatra) where wild orangutans live. It is also home to the Bornean pygmy elephant, proboscis monkey, clouded leopard, sun bear, and Rafflesia arnoldii, the world's largest individual flower.

Over recent decades, large areas of lowland rainforest have been cleared for palm oil plantations, logging, and coal mining, putting severe pressure on wildlife habitats and indigenous communities.

Borneo is home to over 21 million people, including hundreds of distinct indigenous ethnic groups collectively known as the Dayak, traditionally famous for their communal longhouses, intricate woodcarving, and rich oral traditions.

Alongside indigenous groups, coastal cities feature vibrant Malay, Chinese, and Indonesian communities, creating a diverse mix of languages, religions (primarily Islam, Christianity, and traditional animism), and cuisines.

Indonesia is actively building its new national capital city, Nusantara, in East Kalimantan on Borneo's eastern coast to relieve overcrowding and land subsidence in Jakarta.
