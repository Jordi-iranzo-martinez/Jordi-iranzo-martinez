Isla de la Juventud ("Isle of Youth") is the second-largest island in Cuba, located roughly 50 km (31 miles) south of the main Cuban mainland across the Gulf of Batabanó.

Covering about 2,200 km² (or 2,419 km² including surrounding cays) with a population of around 73,000, it is administered directly by the central government as a Special Municipality rather than belonging to any province.

Nueva Gerona is the capital, a quiet riverfront port town on the northern coast founded in 1830.

The northern half features low marble hills (_Sierra de Casas_ and _Sierra de Caballos_), pine forests, and citrus orchards, separated from the wild, protected southern swamps (_Ciénaga de Lanier_) and coral reefs.

The municipality also includes the Canarreos Archipelago—most notably Cayo Largo del Sur, a popular resort island to the east.

After Christopher Columbus sighted the island in 1494 and named it _La Evangelista_, its secluded coves became a major base for pirates and privateers—including Francis Drake, Henry Morgan, and John Rackham. Local lore and accounts of the island helped inspire Robert Louis Stevenson's _Treasure Island_ and J. M. Barrie's _Peter Pan_.

Following the Spanish–American War in 1898, the island's status was left ambiguous, prompting hundreds of American settlers to buy land there until the U.S. formally recognized Cuban sovereignty in 1925.

Presidio Modelo, built in the late 1920s on the panopticon design, this massive circular prison held political prisoners—most famously Fidel and Raúl Castro from 1953 to 1955 following the Moncada Barracks attack.

Formerly called Isla de Pinos (Isle of Pines), it was renamed _Isla de la Juventud_ in 1978 after tens of thousands of Cuban and international students came to the island to study and work in agriculture.]

Notable Landmarks

- **Presidio Modelo**. Now a national monument and museum showcasing the haunting circular cellblocks.

- **Punta Francés Marine National Park**. A protected southwestern peninsula renowned for pristine white-sand beaches and world-class scuba diving walls.

- **Cuevas de Punta del Este**. A coastal cave complex containing over 200 ancient pictographs painted by the island's indigenous Guanahatabey/Siboney inhabitants.

- **Finca El Abra**. The 19th-century estate where Cuban independence hero José Martí spent several weeks recovering in 1870 before his exile to Spain.

- **Playa Bibijagua**. A unique beach on the northern coast famous for its naturally black sand formed from eroded marble.
