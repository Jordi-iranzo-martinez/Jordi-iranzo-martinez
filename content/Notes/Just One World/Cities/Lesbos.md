Lesbos (also spelled Lesvos) is Greece's third-largest island, located in the northeastern Aegean Sea just off the coast of Turkey. 

Covering about 1,633 km² (631 sq mi) with a population of around 84,000, it is renowned for its lush olive groves, thermal hot springs, medieval hilltop towns, deep literary heritage, and status as the ouzo capital of Greece.

Key Towns & Landmarks

- **Mytilene** (Mytilini). The island's lively port capital on the southeastern coast, crowned by a massive Byzantine and Genoese fortress and home to neoclassical mansions, the Roman Aqueduct at Moria, and the University of the Aegean.

- **Molyvos** (Mithymna). A picturesque northern coastal town of cobbled alleys and stone houses cascading down from a well-preserved medieval castle to a colorful fishing harbor.

- **Petrified Forest of Lesvos**. A UNESCO Global Geopark near Sigri on the western peninsula containing 20-million-year-old fossilized sequoia trees buried by ancient volcanic eruptions.

- **Plomari**. A traditional southern town celebrated as the birthplace and production hub of Greece's finest ouzo, featuring historic distilleries and ouzo museums.

- **Skala Eresou**. A laid-back southwestern beach village and the ancient birthplace of the lyric poet Sappho, famous today for its long sandy bay and annual international women's festival.

- **Gulf of Kalloni & Thermal Springs**. The island's central saltwater bay is a premier European birdwatching wetland (famous for flamingos and herons), while volcanic activity feeds natural thermal spas at Eftalou, Polichnitos, and Gera.

In antiquity, Lesbos was a major cultural center that produced the lyric poets Sappho and Alcaeus, as well as the philosopher and botanist Theophrastus (Aristotle's successor). The English word _lesbian_ derives directly from the island's name in reference to Sappho's poetry.

Blanketed by over 11 million olive trees, Lesbos produces prized extra virgin olive oil alongside Ladotyri Mytilinis (a hard sheep's-milk cheese aged in olive oil) and cured Kalloni sardines.
