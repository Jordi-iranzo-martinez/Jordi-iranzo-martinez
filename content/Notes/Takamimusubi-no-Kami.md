
Takamimusubi-no-Kami (高御産巣日神, meaning "Exalted Generative Force") is one of the primordial creator deities (Zōka Sanshin) of Japanese Shinto mythology, representing the active, heavenly principle of creation, growth, and agriculture.

*[[Ancient Japan]], [[Shintoism]]*

According to the *Kojiki*, Takamimusubi was the second _kami_ to spontaneously come into existence in the High Plain of Heaven (*Takamagahara*), immediately after Ame-no-Minakanushi and just before Kamimusubi. Like the other primordial gods (*Kotoamatsukami*), he came into being alone (*hitorigami*) without gender and initially hid himself away after sparking creation.

Unlike the other primordial deities who remained distant, Takamimusubi actively reappears later in mythology—often under the alternate name Takagi-no-Kami ("*High Tree Deity*")—acting alongside the sun goddess Amaterasu to convene the heavenly assembly of gods and oversee the pacification of Earth.

He is the father of [[Omoikane]] (the god of wisdom) and Takuhadachiji-hime. When his daughter married Amaterasu's son, they gave birth to Ninigi-no-Mikoto—making Takamimusubi the maternal grandfather of the heavenly grandson sent to rule Japan and a key tutelary deity of the Japanese Imperial Family.

When the envoy god Ame-no-Wakahiko betrayed Heaven and shot a sacred pheasant messenger, Takamimusubi caught the bloodied arrow in Heaven and hurled it straight back down through the portal, striking the traitor dead.
