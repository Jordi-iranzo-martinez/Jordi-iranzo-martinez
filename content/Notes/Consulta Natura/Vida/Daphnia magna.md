
*Daphnia magna* is a species of freshwater planktonic crustacean, commonly known as the giant water flea, widely used as a model organism in scientific research, ecotoxicology, and aquariums.

*[[Crustacea]]*

Despite its common name, it is not an insect, but a small branchiopod crustacean of the order Cladocera:

- **Size and appearance**. It is one of the largest species in its genus; adult females measure between 2 and 5 millimeters, while males are smaller (around 2 mm). It has a translucent chitinous carapace that allows its beating heart and digestive tract to be observed under a microscope.

- **Movement**. It uses a pair of large, branched second antennae to propel itself through the water in jerky, hopping movements, which gives rise to the name "water flea."

- **Habitat and diet**. It inhabits freshwater lakes, ponds, and pools across much of the Northern Hemisphere. It is a filter feeder that primarily consumes microalgae, bacteria, and suspended organic matter.

Its reproductive strategy shifts depending on environmental conditions:

- **Parthenogenesis** (asexual reproduction). Under favorable conditions (warm temperatures and abundant food), females produce diploid eggs that develop without fertilization in their dorsal brood chamber, rapidly generating female genetic clones.

- **Sexual reproduction**. When faced with environmental stressors such as cold, drought, food scarcity, or overcrowding, males are born and fertilized haploid eggs are produced. These are protected inside a resistant casing called an **ephippium**, which can survive in a dormant state for years until optimal conditions return.

It plays a vital role in both natural ecosystems and laboratory settings:

- **Trophic link**. It connects primary producers (phytoplankton) with secondary consumers (fish and insect larvae), while also helping keep water clear by controlling algal growth.

- **Bioindicator in ecotoxicology**. Due to its high sensitivity to chemical pollutants, heavy metals, and changes in water quality, it is used in standardized international tests (such as OECD guidelines) to assess the toxicity of substances and effluents.

- **Aquaculture and aquariums**. It is widely cultured as a highly nutritious live food for ornamental fish and fry.
