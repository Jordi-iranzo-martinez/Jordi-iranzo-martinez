
The landscape is the visible feature of an area of land, including its physical elements, living elements, human creations, and transitory elements like lighting and weather. It represents the interface between nature and culture.

Geographically and environmentally, landscapes are broadly categorized into three main types:

- Natural Landscapes
- Cultural / Human Landscapes
- Transitory & Perceptual Elements


1. Natural Landscapes

These are areas untouched or minimally impacted by human activity, where natural forces shape the environment.

- **Mountainous Landscapes:** Characterized by high elevations, steep slopes, rocky terrain, and distinct climate zones (e.g., the Andes or the Alps).
- **Coastal Landscapes:** Where the land meets the sea, shaped by wave action, tides, and currents (e.g., cliffs, beaches, and estuaries).
- **Arid Landscapes:** Deserts and drylands shaped by wind erosion and minimal rainfall, closely tied to the processes of desertification.

2. Cultural / Human Landscapes

These are landscapes that have been intentionally modified, built, or shaped by human civilization over time.

- **Agricultural Landscapes:** Rural areas organized for farming, crop cultivation, and livestock herding, which often change the natural vegetation patterns.
- **Urban Landscapes:** Highly dense, built environments consisting of cities, roads, infrastructure, and modified green spaces (like public parks).
- **Industrial Landscapes:** Areas dedicated to heavy industry, mining, or energy production, which can heavily alter the physical topography of the land.

3. Transitory & Perceptual Elements

A landscape is also defined by how it is experienced. **Landscape aesthetics** involve how humans perceive an area through sight, sound, and smell, heavily influenced by changing weather, seasons, and times of day.
