
Great American Prairie

[[North America]]

The Great American Prairie refers to the massive expanse of temperate grasslands that once covered the entire central portion of North America, stretching from the Mississippi River in the east to the Rocky Mountains in the west, and from southern Canada down into Texas.

The American prairie changes drastically from east to west based on the amount of rainfall it receives, splitting into three distinct ecological zones:

- **Tallgrass Prairie** (East): Receiving the most rainfall, this area was dominated by grasses like Big Bluestem, which could grow up to 3 meters (10 feet) tall. Today, less than 4% of the original tallgrass prairie survives, making it one of the rarest ecosystems in the world.

- **Mixed-grass Prairie** (Central): A transitional zone containing a blend of both tall and short grasses, highly resilient to shifting weather patterns and droughts.

- **Shortgrass Prairie** (West): Sitting in the rain shadow of the Rocky Mountains, this arid zone is dominated by low-growing, drought-resistant grasses like Buffalo grass, which rarely grow higher than a few inches.
