The Coconut crab (*Birgus latro*) is the largest land-living arthropod in the world, famous for its staggering size, bone-crushing claw strength, and ability to climb tall [[Coconut Palm Tree]].

They can weigh up to 4.1 kg (9 lbs) and grow to a massive leg-span of up to 1 meter (3.3 feet) from tip to tip. They can live for more than 40 to 60 years.

Unlike their fragile hermit crab cousins, adult coconut crabs possess a heavily calcified, rock-hard exoskeleton. Because their abdomen develops this tough armor shell as they mature, they permanently discard foreign snail shells once they grow past juvenile stages.

Their left claw is massively enlarged and can exert a crushing force of up to 3,300 newtons. This grip strength is physically more powerful than the bite force of most land predators, including lions, leopards, and bears, allowing the crab to easily rip the tough, fibrous husks off coconuts.

[[Crustacea]]

They feed on fallen tropical fruits, nuts, seeds, and carrion. However, they are also active, efficient hunters, stalking and killing large birds (like nesting boobies), rats, other crabs, and small mammals while they sleep.
