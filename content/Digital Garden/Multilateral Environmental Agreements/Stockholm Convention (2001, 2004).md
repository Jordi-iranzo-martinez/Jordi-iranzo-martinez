title: Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants
---
The Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs) is a legally binding global treaty designed to protect human health and the environment from highly dangerous, long-lasting chemical pollutants.

Adopted in 2001 and entering into force in 2004, it targets chemicals that remain intact in the environment for exceptionally long periods, distribute widely across geographic regions, accumulate in the fatty tissue of humans and wildlife (bioaccumulation), and are toxic to both humans and ecosystems.

To regulate these substances, the treaty categorizes them into three strict legal lists (Annexes) managed by the Persistent Organic Pollutants Review Committee (POPRC):

- **Annex A (Elimination):** Member states must completely ban the production and use of these chemicals (e.g., Aldrin, Endrin, and newer additions like certain flame retardants).

- **Annex B (Restriction):** Production and use are heavily restricted, allowed only for acceptable critical purposes. The most famous example is DDT, which is banned globally except for targeted public health initiatives to control malaria-carrying mosquitoes.

- **Annex C (Unintentional Production):** Targets toxic chemicals that are not manufactured on purpose but are released as accidental byproducts of industrial processes or waste burning (such as dioxins and furans). Governments must implement strict environmental controls to minimize these releases.

Because the chemical industry continuously develops new compounds, the Stockholm Convention acts as a dynamic framework, regularly adding new substances to its restriction lists:

- **The Global Elimination of PFAS:** In recent high-level implementations, the convention's bodies have dramatically tightened restrictions on **PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances)**, widely known as "forever chemicals." Key compounds like PFHxS, PFOA, and PFOS are transitioning into total phase-out phases globally, forcing manufacturing industries to completely redesign waterproof and non-stick consumer goods.

- **Targeting Plastic Additives:** Under recent chemical review mandates, the POPRC has heavily focused on toxic chemical additives embedded within global plastics. Hazardous UV stabilizers and chlorinated paraffins have been fast-tracked for international restrictions, plugging loopholes where toxic chemicals were being shipped internationally disguised as recyclable plastic polymers.

- **Synergy with the BBNJ and Basel Treaties:** The Stockholm Secretariat has unified its field data tracking alongside the Basel Convention (Hazardous Waste) and the newly active UN High Seas Treaty. This ensures that when electronic or industrial waste is dumped, toxic chemical bioaccumulation inside marine food chains is tracked and prosecuted under a unified international framework.
