The Paris Agreement is a legally binding international treaty on climate change adopted by 196 Parties on December 12, 2015, entering into force on November 4, 2016. Its main goal is keeping the global average temperature increase to well below 2°C while pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.

The agreement uses a universal framework binding all nations through:

- **Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs):** Plans created by countries detailing their greenhouse gas reduction and adaptation efforts.

- **The Five-Year Ambition Cycle:** A requirement for nations to submit progressively ambitious updated NDCs every five years.

- **Climate Finance:** Developed nations providing financial aid to help vulnerable countries transition to low-carbon systems and address climate impacts.

**Current Global Status & Key Developments**

- **The 1.5°C Realignment:** Stricter adherence to a permanent 1.5°C peak is viewed as increasingly difficult under current trends, shifting focus toward minimizing temperature overshoot.

- **Geopolitical Shifts:** Fluctuations in participation, including U.S. withdrawals, impact major emitter dynamics within the accord.

- **Implementation Challenges:** Numerous signatories face compliance scrutiny and deadlines for upgraded "NDC 3.0" roadmaps ahead of upcoming climate summits.
