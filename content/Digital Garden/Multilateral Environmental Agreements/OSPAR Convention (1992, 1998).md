title: Convention for the Protection of the Marine Environment of the North-East Atlantic
---
The OSPAR Convention (formally known as the Convention for the Protection of the Marine Environment of the North-East Atlantic) is the primary comprehensive international legal instrument governing environmental cooperation to safeguard the North-East Atlantic Ocean.

Adopted in 1992 and entering into force in 1998, it merged and updated two pioneering maritime treaties: the 1972 Oslo Convention (on dumping from ships) and the 1974 Paris Convention (on land-based marine pollution). The name "OSPAR" is an acronym combined from Oslo and Paris.

The convention brings together 15 national governments (Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Iceland, Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom) alongside the [[European Union]].

The convention's legal framework operates through specialized working strategies designed to target specific ecological pressures:

- **Annex I:** Prevention and elimination of pollution from land-based sources (such as industrial runoff, municipal wastewater, and agricultural chemicals).

- **Annex II:** Prevention and elimination of pollution by dumping or incineration of waste at sea from ships and aircraft.

- **Annex III:** Prevention and elimination of pollution from offshore sources (primarily regulating oil and gas platforms and drilling infrastructure).

- **Annex IV:** Continuous assessment of the quality of the marine environment through coordinated scientific monitoring and reporting.

- **Annex V:** Protection and conservation of ecosystems and biological diversity against the adverse impacts of human activities (added to the convention in 1998).
