The famous **"Nine Kings" photograph** was captured on **May 20, 1910**, in the White Drawing Room at [Windsor Castle](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESpQEB6zswFR8agwnjBdyWcW6e1rPL8ApnMinl4zdWtYJxAbidbJSKa2_spQqrnvx-_HKFO8NkuMW-i2rOOed7oyaybMkxLNYcWjd_g9msphyts6z0SnQq-VjC4-oGCRYzAh_J3bG_tRYuv12cQX0iT-RPt36OnUGLGH3Nc1nsGVVGlG1JR-9pr5ODdMS15SnEybdMRgRxczFsDkBddNWMVg3XiMSPSCA). It represents the largest and most iconic gathering of reigning European monarchs ever recorded in a single photograph, assembled for the state funeral of **King Edward VII** of the United Kingdom.

Standing (Left to Right)

- **King Haakon VII** (Norway): nephew by marriage and son-in-law of Edward VII (married to Edward's daughter, Princess Maud).

- **Tsar Ferdinand I** of Bulgaria

- **King Alfonso XIII** (Spain) – nephew-in-law of Edward VII (married to Edward's niece, Victoria Eugenie of Battenberg).

- **King Manuel II** (Portugal)

- **King George V** (United Kingdom) (seated) (son of Edward VII)

- **Kaiser Wilhelm II** (Germany)

- **King George I** (Greece)

- **King Frederick VIII** (Denmark)

- **King Albert I** (Belgium)

Within four years, the outbreak of World War I shattered these familial alliances, pitting first cousins (like King George V and Kaiser Wilhelm II) directly against one another. By the end of the conflict, several of the royal houses represented in the photograph were permanently abolished or dismantled.
