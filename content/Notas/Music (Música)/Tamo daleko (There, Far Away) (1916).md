The Great Retreat (1915–1916)

In the winter of 1915, during World War I, the Kingdom of Serbia was invaded by the combined forces of Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Bulgaria. Facing total annihilation, the Serbian Army, along with the King and thousands of civilian refugees, chose not to surrender. Instead, they undertook a brutal, heroic march across the frozen mountains of Albania toward the Adriatic coast.

Known as the Great Serbian Retreat, this exodus cost the lives of over 240,000 people due to starvation, freezing temperatures, and constant ambushes. The survivors were eventually evacuated by Allied French and British ships to the Greek island of Corfu. It was in this makeshift island refuge, surrounded by sick and dying soldiers longing for their occupied homeland, that the song was born in 1916.

Following World War II, when the communist government of Yugoslavia was established under Josip Broz Tito, "Tamo daleko" was implicitly banned or heavily discouraged from being performed publicly. The regime feared that the song could spark ethnic Serbian nationalism and disrupt the fragile balance of unity among the different Yugoslav republics.

The ban only elevated its status to a cult song of resistance. After the collapse of Yugoslavia, it returned to the public sphere as a revered historical anthem of remembrance, honoring the immense sacrifices of the generation that fought in the Great War.
