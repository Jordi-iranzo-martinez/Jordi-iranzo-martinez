Ja, vi elsker dette landet (Yes, we love this country) is the national anthem of Norway. It was

*The poem was written by the famous Norwegian author and Nobel Prize laureate Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson between 1859 and 1868. The melody was composed by Bjørnson’s cousin, Rikard Nordraak, in 1864.*

For over 400 years, Norway was completely ruled by Denmark. In 1814, Denmark was forced to cede Norway to Sweden after the Napoleonic Wars.

Norway tried to declare independence and quickly wrote its own democratic constitution on May 17, 1814, but Sweden was militarily too strong and forced Norway into a political union under the Swedish King.

When Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson wrote "Ja, vi elsker dette landet" in the 1860s, Norway was still stuck in this forced union with Sweden. The song was written as a peaceful cultural protest. Norwegians couldn't fight Sweden with a massive army, so they fought with cultural identity, poetry, and music. The anthem was designed to remind Norwegians that they were a distinct, proud nation with their own ancient history, entirely separate from Sweden and Denmark.

This cultural movement eventually succeeded: in 1905, Norway peacefully dissolved the union and became a completely independent kingdom.
