- In the Gullah dialect, **"Kum Ba Yah"** literally translates to **"Come by Here"**.

The song is an emotional plea to God. Enslaved people who were enduring unimaginable hardships, violence, and forced labor sang to the heavens, asking God to physically "come by here" to witness their suffering and help them. The verses reflect this raw plea: "Someone's crying, Lord, come by here," followed by "Someone's praying, Lord..." and "Someone's singing, Lord..."

The earliest written record of the song dates to the 1920s, and the first audio recording was captured on a wax cylinder in 1926 by a folklorist traveling through the Georgia coast.

The song’s easy-to-learn, repetitive melody and message of unity made it a massive hit across the world. It became the definitive song of the Civil Rights Movement during protest marches, and it was adopted globally by the Boy Scouts, Christian youth groups, and summer camps as a wholesome anthem sung around campfires while holding hands.
