Betty Grable** (1916–1973) was an iconic American actress, dancer, and singer who reigned as the **definitive queen of the Hollywood musical during the 1940s**.

With her vibrant platinum-blonde persona, effortless comedic timing, and spectacular athletic dancing skills, she became [20th Century Studios](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESWwHrOzAVZMbhPEf-UQjsQETmsH_hHKF4DRjOO5IQTajjWzavkNTu7jnIwaGlhUX9AxRcV5h-NPwftM3uZJntwW8ntytNbBiDv1sqSLzrKiqrtigu7m8vJLS9awA)' top box-office draw and, at her peak during World War II, the **highest-paid woman in the entire United States**.

While she starred in massively successful Technicolor musicals such as _Down Argentine Way_ (1940), _Moon Over Miami_ (1941), and later the comedy smash _How to Marry a Millionaire_ (1953) alongside Marilyn Monroe, Grable's cultural impact transcended cinema screens:

- **The Mythic Photograph (1943):** In 1943, studio photographer Frank Powolny took a back-view studio shot of Grable smiling playfully over her shoulder in a tight white one-piece swimsuit.

- **The Soldiers' Allied Charm:** This single image became the most famous **pin-up poster in 20th-century history**. Over five million copies were printed and distributed, finding its way into the lockers, barracks, and flight jackets of **millions of Allied servicemen fighting in Europe and the Pacific**. Her wholesome yet glamorous appeal was widely credited with boosting troop morale, cementing her as an immortal symbol of the wartime home front.

Grable's most celebrated physical trademark was her legs, shaped by years of rigorous professional dance training since childhood.

- In a legendary publicity masterstroke that set an entertainment industry precedent, 20th Century Fox took out a **$1 million insurance policy on her legs** with the famous Lloyd's of London underwriters. This staggering financial contract forever embedded her in pop culture history under her definitive moniker: **"The Million Dollar Legs."**

The distinctive "back-shot" pose was entirely accidental. At the time of the photo shoot, Grable was in the early stages of pregnancy. To discreetly conceal her changing figure, Powolny suggested she turn her back to the camera and smile over her shoulder. Dressed in a simple, one-piece white swimsuit and wearing high heels, the resulting silhouette perfectly highlighted her features and her famously insured legs.

As musicals fell out of fashion in the 1950s, her film career wound down, though she continued performing on stage, including in Las Vegas and in touring productions. She was married twice, most notably to bandleader Harry James, and had two daughters with him.

She died in 1973 at age 56 from lung cancer.
