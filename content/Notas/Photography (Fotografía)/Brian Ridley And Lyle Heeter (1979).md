It stands as one of the most famous, subversive, and definitive works from his exploration of the underground gay BDSM and leather subculture in New York City.

Brian Ridley is seated in a grand leather wingback armchair, completely bound by handcuffs and metal shackles attached to his ankles and wrists. Lyle Heeter stands defiantly beside him, his elbow resting on the armchair as he holds the metal chain connected to a leather collar buckled around Ridley's neck. It explicitly and proudly maps out a sadomasochistic master-slave relationship

In stark contrast to their full leather outfits, chains, and riding crops, the men are posing inside a highly conventional, upper-middle-class living room. The space is meticulously decorated with soft floral curtains, elegant antiques, and a glass-topped table balanced on decorative stag antlers.

By bringing the gay S&M underground out of hidden dungeons and framing it inside a classic, respectable parlor setting, Mapplethorpe sought to bestow formal beauty, classical symmetry, and human dignity upon a community that was heavily criminalized and demonized by mainstream society at the time. The direct, unblinking stares of both men challenge the viewer to accept their relationship as an organized, consensual, and normal reality.
