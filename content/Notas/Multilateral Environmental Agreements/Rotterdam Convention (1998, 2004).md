title: Rotterdam Convention on the Prior Informed Consent Procedure for Certain Hazardous Chemicals and Pesticides in International Trade
---
The Rotterdam Convention (formally known as the Rotterdam Convention on the Prior Informed Consent Procedure for Certain Hazardous Chemicals and Pesticides in International Trade) is a legally binding multilateral treaty designed to promote shared responsibility and cooperative efforts in the international trade of toxic chemicals.

Adopted in 1998 and entering into force in 2004, it acts as a global early-warning system to protect human health and the environment—particularly in developing nations—from the unwanted importation of highly dangerous pesticides and industrial chemicals.

The entire treaty operates around a strict legal tool known as the Prior Informed Consent (PIC) procedure.

- **The Listing System:** Chemicals that have been banned or severely restricted for health or environmental reasons by two or more signatory countries are evaluated by a technical expert committee and added to the treaty's **Annex III list**.

- **The Right to Refuse:** Once a chemical is listed in Annex III, exporting nations are legally forbidden from shipping it unless the importing country has been formally notified of the chemical's toxic risks and has explicitly given its written consent to accept the shipment.

- **The Labeling Mandate:** If an export is approved, the convention forces the exporting country to ensure that the hazardous cargo carries clear warning labels, safety data sheets, and information on potential human health risks.

The convention currently regulates dozens of highly toxic substances, divided into two main categories:

- **Pesticides:** Heavily toxic agricultural chemicals, including severely hazardous pesticide formulations that pose acute health risks to farmers and local water tables in developing nations.

- **Industrial Chemicals:** Extremely dangerous industrial compounds, such as various forms of **asbestos**, polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), and highly toxic flame retardants.
