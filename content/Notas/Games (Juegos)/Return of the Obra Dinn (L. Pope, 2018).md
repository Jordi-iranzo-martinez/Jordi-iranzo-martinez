The East India Company (EIC), founded in 1600 and active until 1874, was the most powerful monopoly corporation in global history. 

It was founded in 1600, based in London

While it began as a regular trading enterprise, it evolved into a mega-corporation that commanded its own private armies, conquered entire nations, and controlled half of the world's trade. It is the corporate entity you work for as an insurance inspector in Return of the Obra Dinn.

Queen Elizabeth I granted the company a royal charter, giving it an absolute monopoly on all English trade in the East Indies (the region covering India, Southeast Asia, and China).

Initially, the EIC traded peacefully for spices, textiles, and tea. However, by exploiting political divisions within the declining Mughal Empire, the Company began seizing territory. Following the Battle of Plassey (1757), the EIC effectively became the ruling government of India.

At its peak, the Company ruled over one-fifth of the world’s population and commanded a private military of over 260,000 soldiers (an army twice the size of the British state military at the time).

To transport vast wealth across oceans, the EIC utilized a unique, highly specialized class of merchant ships known as East Indiamen, built as warships to travel long distances alone and defend of pirates and rival empires (like the French). They were heavily armored and carried rows of defensive cannons. On the high seas, an East Indiaman could easily be mistaken for a British Royal Navy Ship of the Line.

To pay for Chinese tea, the EIC smuggled illegal opium into China, directly triggering devastating international military conflicts.

Decades of brutal economic exploitation, cultural disrespect, and heavy taxation led to The Great Rebellion (1857) across India. The conflict was so violent that the British government realized a private corporation could no longer be trusted to govern a subcontinent.

In 1858, the British Crown stripped the Company of its power and took direct control of India (initiating the British Raj), completely dissolving the EIC shortly after.
