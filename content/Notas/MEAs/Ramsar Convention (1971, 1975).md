The [Ramsar Convention](https://www.ramsar.org/) is 


Adopted on **February 2, 1971**, in the Iranian city of Ramsar, it entered into force in 1975. Unlike other global treaties that cover broad environmental issues, Ramsar is unique because it is the **only global treaty dedicated to a single, specific type of ecosystem: wetlands**. It currently unites **173 member states** protecting over **2,500 "Ramsar Sites"** worldwide, covering nearly 259 million hectares of land.

The convention adopts an exceptionally wide definition of wetlands. It encompasses not only freshwater marshes, bogs, and peatlands, but also rivers, lakes, floodplains, mangroves, coral reefs, estuaries, and even human-made structures like fish ponds, rice paddies, and reservoirs.

To effectively safeguard these highly productive environments, the [Ramsar Convention](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESUQHrOzAVG5Xc990tMrLGcp5kli9WBnCUItlmY7f2nhYVQUppbmiddNE-BsTtr1ve1OhpYODi1jop5LcAZXK0KreBU2aY2hE-Ok__rPJ27yNoQQ) forces contracting parties to operate across three mandatory pillars:

- **The Wise Use of All Wetlands:** Member nations must integrate wetland conservation considerations into their national land-use and urban planning systems. "Wise use" historically translates to sustainable use, ensuring human economic activities do not degrade the local water table or biological diversity.

- **The Ramsar List (Wetlands of International Importance):** Upon adhering to the treaty, every country must designate at least one wetland to the official [Ramsar List](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESbAHrOzAVfrjH7Mtu9E-B4yMcHjb4Q7Jeu-p_zMOAz_8D_V7WnHC78VYdAcu8y9bWDpIdZyXvuQwxk6IS7CHEHTOouS4YVIwar2loN6tH2KAIjvMKOsk3vefemmUQMXsKES8hRLGWhdNyg94v1Q). These protected zones acquire an international conservation status and are managed under strict criteria to preserve their ecological framework.

- **International Cooperation:** Wetlands and their wildlife often ignore geopolitical borders. Countries must cooperate regarding transboundary wetlands, shared water systems, and the preservation of global migratory waterbird flyways.
