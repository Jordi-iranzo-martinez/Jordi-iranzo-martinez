The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) (commonly known as PNUMA in Spanish) is the leading global authority that sets the international environmental agenda, promotes sustainable development within the UN system, and serves as an authoritative advocate for the global environment.

UNEP provides institutional guidance, tracks scientific data, and fosters partnerships to help nations tackle what it categorizes as the **"Triple Planetary Crisis"**:

- **Climate Change:** Accelerating global decarbonization and tracking climate indicators.
- **Nature and Biodiversity Loss:** Halting the destruction of ecosystems and the extinction of species.
- **Pollution and Waste:** Eliminating toxic chemicals, plastics, and industrial waste.

To fund this work, UNEP manages a core budget of **$1.113 billion** for its 2026–2027 cycle, relying heavily on universal membership and voluntary national contributions. 


One of UNEP’s most powerful roles is that it acts as the supreme institutional host, providing funding and administrative secretariats for the world’s most critical **Multilateral Environmental Agreements (MEAs)**. This includes:

- **The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD)**.
- **The Bonn Convention (CMS)** on migratory species.
- **CITES** (regulating the endangered wildlife trade).
- **The Montreal Protocol** (celebrated for achieving a 98% reduction in ozone-depleting substances).

Additionally, UNEP co-founded the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) alongside the World Meteorological Organization in 1988, which delivers the world's most influential, scientifically reviewed reports on global warming.
