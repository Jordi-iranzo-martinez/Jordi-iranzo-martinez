The [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change](https://www.ipcc.ch/) (IPCC) is is the premier international body responsible for assessing the science related to climate change.

Co-founded in 1988 by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) [UNEP] and the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), the panel is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland. It currently comprises 195 Member States.

The IPCC does not carry out original scientific research or monitor climate metrics. Instead, it crowdsources thousands of top-tier scientists globally to systematically peer-review published literature and technical data, delivering the gold-standard reports that outline the socioeconomic impacts, adaptation tracks, and mitigation models of human-induced global warming.

To organize its vast evaluation work, the IPCC divides its scientific panels into three distinct pillars and a specialized task force:

- **Working Group I (WGI) – The Physical Science Basis:** Evaluates the hard science behind climate systems and how human greenhouse gas emissions drive global warming anomalies.
- 
- **Working Group II (WGII) – Impacts, Adaptation, and Vulnerability:** Reviews how climate change damages natural biodiversity and human settlements, pinpointing solutions to build structural resilience and adaptation targets.

- **Working Group III (WGIII) – Mitigation of Climate Change:** Analyzes methods, technologies, and economic pathways to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and withdraw carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.

- **Task Force on National Greenhouse Gas Inventories:** Formulates standardized methodologies allowing world governments to transparently calculate and report their actual industrial emissions.

The IPCC publishes massive, comprehensive reviews called Assessment Reports (AR) roughly every six to seven years. These texts act as the absolute scientific baseline used by international climate negotiators under the UNFCCC to build international law, directly driving the execution of milestones like the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement.

To date, six full cycles have been completed. The Sixth Assessment Report (AR6), published in 2023, delivered a stark warning that immediate, drastic action is necessary to keep global warming below the 1.5°C threshold.

The panel is operating under its **Seventh Assessment Cycle (AR7)**, led by its chairperson, **Professor Sir Jim Skea** of the United Kingdom:

- **Three Key Reports Planned for 2027:** The IPCC has confirmed the upcoming release of three highly anticipated foundational frameworks: the _Special Report on Climate Change and Cities_, a methodology report on _Short-lived Climate Forcers_, and a technological guide on _Carbon Dioxide Removal (CDR)_.

- **The Standoff Over the Final Timeline:** The final deadline for the full AR7 suite—originally aimed for completion through a Synthesis Report in late 2029—has faced unprecedented administrative debate and structural deadlocks during recent plenaries (like IPCC-64 in Bangkok) due to friction between member states over review windows. Member states are converging in Addis Ababa to attempt to cement the definitive calendar.

- **Increased Focus on Adaptation and Diversity:** The AR7 cycle stands out for significantly stepping up metrics for climate adaptation, integrating diverse knowledge systems (including Indigenous and local environmental management), and achieving record author participation from developing nations.
