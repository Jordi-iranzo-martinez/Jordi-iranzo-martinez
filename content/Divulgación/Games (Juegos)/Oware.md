Oware is an ancient abstract strategy game belonging to the Mancala family of pit-and-pebble games. It is the national game of Ghana and is widely considered the oldest and most popular Mancala variant played across West Africa and the Caribbean.

The game is a pure mental calculation contest with no element of luck, where the objective is to capture more seeds (at least 25 out of 48) than your opponent.

- **The Board:** A wooden board consisting of **12 circular pits (houses)** arranged in two parallel rows of six, with one larger scoring pit at each end.
- **The Seeds:** A total of **48 identical seeds** (traditionally glossy grey _Caesalpinia bonduc_ seeds, nicknaming the game "warri").
- **Initial Setup:** At the start of the game, exactly **4 seeds are placed into each of the 12 pits**. Each player controls the row of six pits closest to them.


Play moves counter-clockwise around the board. A turn consists of two phases: **sowing** and **capturing**.

1. **Sowing (Lifting & Dropping):** On your turn, pick up all the seeds from any single pit on your side of the board. Moving counter-clockwise, drop exactly **one seed into each consecutive pit** until your hand is empty.
2. **The 12-Seed Exception:** If a pit contains 11 or more seeds, sowing will lap entirely around the board. When this happens, you must **skip the original starting pit**, leaving it empty.
3. **Capturing:** You can only capture seeds from your opponent's row. If the _very last seed_ in your hand lands in an opponent's pit and brings that pit's total to **exactly 2 or 3 seeds**, you capture all those seeds.
4. **Chain Capturing:** After capturing a pit, look at the preceding pit (clockwise). If it also belongs to your opponent and now holds exactly 2 or 3 seeds, you capture those as well. This chain continues until an unbroken condition is hit.
5. **Feeding the Opponent:** You are legally forbidden from wiping out all of your opponent's seeds unless you have no other choice. If your opponent's row is completely empty, you **must make a move that passes seeds into their territory** to keep the game alive.

The match ends immediately when:

- A player captures **25 or more seeds** (guaranteeing a majority).
- The board locks into a loop, or a player's row is empty and it is impossible to feed them. The remaining seeds are split, and the player with the highest total wins.

- **Ancient Origins:** While tracking the exact archaeological timeline is difficult because historical boards were carved out of wood or dug directly into the dirt, related pit games trace back thousands of years to ancient Egypt and Aksum.

- **The Asante Legend:** According to oral history from the Ashanti (Asante) people of Ghana, the name _Oware_ translates literally to **"he/she marries"**. Legend says that a man and a woman played the game for so long without stopping that they eventually had to get married just to be able to finish their match in the same house.

- **International Mind Sport:** Today, Oware has safely transitioned into the modern era as an international mind sport. Organized by federations like the International Oware Federation, formal tournaments are held annually across Europe and West Africa, celebrating it alongside chess and checkers as a premier intellectual game.
