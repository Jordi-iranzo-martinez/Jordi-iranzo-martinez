
Malala Yousafzai (1997) is a Pakistani education activist and the youngest Nobel Prize laureate in history.

While primarily known for her advocacy for girls' education, her work intersects with environmental and social justice issues, making her a powerful voice for global change.

## The girl who spoke out

Born on July 12, 1997, in Mingora, Pakistan, in the Swat Valley, Malala grew up in a region where the Taliban had banned girls from attending school. Her father, Ziauddin Yousafzai, was an educator who ran a school and instilled in her the importance of education and speaking out for what's right.

## Rising voice for education

At just 11 years old, Malala began writing a blog for the BBC Urdu service under a pseudonym, documenting life under Taliban rule and advocating for girls' right to education. Her courage in speaking publicly about education rights made her a target, but also amplified her message globally.

## The attack that changed everything

On October 9, 2012, when Malala was 15, a Taliban gunman boarded her school bus and shot her in the head. The assassination attempt shocked the world and, rather than silencing her, transformed her into a global symbol of resistance against oppression and the fight for universal education.

## Global activism

After recovering in the UK, Malala continued her advocacy on an international scale:

- Co-founded the Malala Fund to support girls' education worldwide
- Became the youngest-ever Nobel Peace Prize winner in 2014 at age 17

He was awarded with the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/2014/yousafzai/biographical/) (2014), “for their struggle against the suppression of children and young people and for the right of all children to education”, being the youngest girl to achieve it. 

He was 17 when he received it, being the youngest girl to achieve it, what included her in the [Guinness World Record](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/records/hall-of-fame/malala-yousafzai-youngest-nobel-prize-winner).

She graduated from Oxford University in 2020 and married in 2021. 




- Continues to champion education access in developing countries

## Connection to environmental justice

Malala's work intersects with environmental issues because:

- Climate change disproportionately affects girls' education (disasters often keep girls out of school permanently)
- Environmental degradation in developing countries often forces families to prioritize boys' education
- She advocates for sustainable development that includes educational access

## Lasting impact

Malala's advocacy has contributed to:

- Increased global awareness of girls' education barriers
- Policy changes in various countries
- Millions of dollars raised for educational programs
- Inspiring a new generation of young activists

Her story demonstrates how one young person's courage can spark global movements and create lasting change, proving that age is no barrier to making a significant impact on the world.
