
The Domestic Pig (_Sus scrofa domesticus_) is a highly intelligent mammal that was introduced to Pacific islands by Polynesian settlers. These omnivores quickly adapted to island life and became important sources of protein for island communities.

The Domestic Chicken (_Gallus gallus domesticus_) was another crucial species brought by Polynesian voyagers during their ocean migrations. These birds provided both eggs and meat, making them essential for survival on remote islands.

The Green Sea Turtle (_Chelonia mydas_) is a large marine reptile that plays crucial roles in ocean ecosystems by maintaining seagrass beds and coral reef health. These ancient navigators can travel thousands of miles during their migrations.

The Coconut Crab (_Birgus latro_) is the world's largest terrestrial arthropod, capable of climbing palm trees to harvest coconuts. These impressive crustaceans are found throughout the Indo-Pacific and are important seed dispersers.

The Manta Ray (_Mobula birostris_) is the largest ray species, with wingspans reaching up to 23 feet. These gentle giants are filter feeders that consume plankton and play important roles in marine nutrient cycling.

The Hawaiian Monk Seal (_Neomonachus schauinslandi_) is one of the world's most endangered marine mammals, found only in Hawaiian waters. These seals are important indicators of ocean ecosystem health.

The Moorish Idol (_Zanclus cornutus_) is a distinctive reef fish with its characteristic long dorsal fin and striking black and white stripes. These fish are important grazers that help maintain the balance of algae on coral reefs.

The Yellowfin Tuna (_Thunnus albacares_) is a large pelagic fish that travels vast distances across Pacific waters. These powerful swimmers are both important commercial species and apex predators in marine food webs.

The Giant Pacific Octopus (_Enteroctopus dofleini_) is the largest octopus species, known for its remarkable intelligence and problem-solving abilities. These cephalopods are master camouflage artists and important predators in marine ecosystems.

The Coconut Palm (_Cocos nucifera_) is perhaps the most important plant for Pacific island cultures, providing food, water, shelter materials, and tools. These palms are perfectly adapted to coastal environments and salt spray.

The Breadfruit (_Artocarpus altilis_) is a starchy tropical fruit that served as a staple food for Polynesian cultures. These trees can produce hundreds of fruits annually and were carefully cultivated and transported between islands.

The Pandanus (_Pandanus tectorius_) is a distinctive tree with prop roots and sword-like leaves used for weaving mats, baskets, and sails. These trees are highly adapted to coastal conditions and salt tolerance.
