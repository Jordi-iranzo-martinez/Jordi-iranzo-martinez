Taken in the heart of New York City’s notorious Mulberry Bend neighborhood.

At the turn of the 20th century, the Lower East Side of Manhattan was home to the most densely populated, crime-ridden, and squalid tenement slums in the Western world. "Bandit's Roost" was an infamously dangerous, narrow alleyway where local gangs and impoverished immigrants congregated.

The image features several men staring intently toward the camera lens. Their defensive body language, combined with the laundry hanging precariously overhead, created a tense, gritty visual reality for wealthy Americans who had never ventured into these neighborhoods.

[[Gangs of New York (M. Scorsese, 2002)]]

It forced city officials to enact strict building safety codes and sanitary regulations for tenements

In 1897, the entire crime-ridden block of Mulberry Bend, including this exact alleyway, was completely demolished

Today, the site where the dangerous "Bandit’s Roost" once stood has been transformed into Columbus Park in modern-day Chinatown, serving as a bright, open public space.
