Betty Grable was an American actress, singer, and dancer who became one of the biggest movie stars of the 1940s — and one of the most famous pin-up girls in American history.

Born in 1916 in St. Louis, Missouri, she was pushed into show business early by an ambitious stage mother. She started in Hollywood as a teenager in the early 1930s, working for years in small roles and as a chorus girl before finally breaking through as a leading lady around 1940.

Grable became the studio's top star through a string of colorful, upbeat musicals — films like _Down Argentine Way_ (1940), _Moon Over Miami_ (1941), _Coney Island_ (1943), and _Mother Wore Tights_ (1947). She wasn't necessarily the most trained dancer or singer in Hollywood, but she had an enormously appealing screen presence — cheerful, energetic, and glamorous — that made her the studio's biggest box-office draw for much of the decade.

Her most iconic image is the 1943 pin-up photograph of her in a white swimsuit, looking back over her shoulder — famously insured for a large sum because of her "million dollar legs." That single photo became one of the most reproduced images of World War II, plastered in barracks and lockers by American servicemen overseas. For a time, she was reportedly the highest-paid entertainer in the United States.

As musicals fell out of fashion in the 1950s, her film career wound down, though she continued performing on stage, including in Las Vegas and in touring productions. She was married twice, most notably to bandleader Harry James, and had two daughters with him.

She died in 1973 at age 56 from lung cancer.
