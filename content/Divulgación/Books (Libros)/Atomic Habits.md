"Atomic Habits" is a self-help book written by **James Clear**, published in 2018, which has become one of the best-selling and most influential books on habits and productivity in recent years.

James Clear is an American writer specializing in habits, decision-making, and continuous improvement. Before writing the book, he had already built a large following through his newsletter and blog, where he explored these ideas.

The book's core idea is that small, seemingly insignificant changes (the "atomic habits") compound consistently over time to produce extraordinary results — for better or worse. Clear argues that the focus shouldn't be so much on setting big goals, but on building sustainable systems and habits that, over time, generate the desired change almost automatically.

- **The Four Laws of Behavior Change**: Clear proposes a practical framework for building good habits (make them obvious, attractive, easy, and satisfying) and for breaking bad ones (invert each of those laws).


- **Identity over outcomes**: he argues that the most lasting habit changes come not from focusing on what you want to achieve, but on the type of person you want to become — a habit as a way of "voting" for a particular identity.

- **The 1% improvement**: the idea that improving by just 1% each day, though seemingly trivial in the moment, compounds into an enormous difference over time.

- **Systems vs. goals**: the notion that goals are good for setting direction, but it's the systems (the daily processes) that actually determine progress.

The book has sold over 20 million copies worldwide, has been translated into dozens of languages, and has remained on bestseller lists like the New York Times' for years. It's widely cited in contexts ranging from personal productivity to business, sports, and coaching.
