The [Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals](https://www.cms.int/) (Bonn, 1983)


The Bonn Convention, officially known as the Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals (CMS), is an international treaty aimed at conserving terrestrial, marine, and avian migratory species throughout their range. Signed in Bonn, Germany, in 1979 under the aegis of the [[United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP)]] (UNEP), it entered into force in 1983.






Migratory species are those which move to different habitats in their life cycle. 

The [United Nations Environmental Programme](https://www.unep.org/) auspiced The Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals (CMS) in 1979 ([Convention text](https://www.cms.int/en/convention-text)).

It aims to protect those animals, and also their habitats and migration routes. By promoting conservation measures and [ecological connectivity](https://www.cms.int/en/topics/ecological-connectivity).

It focuses on species that travel across different countries, the “Range States”. So international cooperation can be more effective.

The Convention gives a platform for the countries to negotiate and implement appropriate conservation measures. And promotes ecological connectivity.

The conservation status is all the influences that affect its long-term distribution and abundance. The status is positive if the population and the range is stable on the long-term. Also if it has enough habitat (where it can live) area and ecosystem distribution and abundance. It is unfavourable if any of this characteristics are not met.

[See the Manual for the national focal Points for CMS and its instruments](https://www.cms.int/sites/default/files/basic_page_documents/Internet_english_09012014.pdf). There is also an [e-community](https://www.migratoryspecies.org/).




It defines a migratory specie as those who use to cross several countries. Cyclically and predictably. They can be all the individuals or a separate population. And range is the area that a migratory specie inhabits and uses in their route. Countries with territory in their range are “Range States”.

CMS defines "migratory species" as:

“the entire population or any geographically separate part of the population of any species or lower taxon of wild animals, a significant proportion of whose members cyclically and predictably cross one or more national jurisdictional boundaries”.

Article I.1 a) of the Convention

For the purposes of the Convention, a migratory species is one that cyclically and predictably crosses one or more national jurisdictional boundaries.

Birds are the most common. They migrate from north to south of the Earth. 


There is a [Bird Migration Atlas](https://migrationatlas.org/) and a [World Migratory Bird Day](https://www.worldmigratorybirdday.org/)



They sometimes represent to cross several countries. They are more vulnerable because they need more habitats. Breeding, wintering, stagings… food, shelter… It can occur at different times of the year or at different stages. Some species include the whales,

They are vulnerable as they need a wider range of habitats. Also in different places, sometimes very far between themselves. They also face several threats, most of them by human activities. Many migratory species have been declining. Some became endangered.

- **Taking**: hunting ([dolphin drive hunts…](https://www.cms.int/en/page/statement-hunting-dolphins-taiji-and-faroe-islands)), fishing, [bycatch…](https://www.cms.int/en/page/bycatch) sometimes for wildlife crime
- **Habitat loss**: deforestation, mining, urbanization, poor [ecological connectivity](https://www.cms.int/en/topics/ecological-connectivity)
- **Pollution**: [marine noise](https://www.cms.int/en/topics/marine-noise), oil spills, light pollution, marine debris…
- **Collision** and electrocution (birds, for example with wind turbines...)
- **Climate change**
- **Disease** (avian influenza)
- **Invasive alien species**
- **Wildlife watching**

It aims to protect those animals, and also their habitats and migration routes. By promoting conservation measures and [ecological connectivity](https://www.cms.int/en/topics/ecological-connectivity).

  
It focuses on species that travel travel across different countries, the “Range States”. So international cooperation can be more effective. The Convention gives a platform for the countries to negotiate and implement appropriate conservation measures. And promotes ecological connectivity.

  
It defines a migratory specie as those who use to cross several countries. Cyclically and predictably. They can be all the individuals or a separate population. And range is the area that a migratory specie inhabits and uses in their route. Countries with territory in their range are “Range States”.

Parties have to implements conservation measures to protected them ([see all Parties and Range States](https://www.cms.int/en/parties-range-states)). By national legislation and procedures, the [National Biodiversity Strategies and Action Plans](https://www.cms.int/en/activities/capacity-building/nbsaps) (NBSAPs). ([See the Guidedlines for that](https://www.cms.int/sites/default/files/document/doc_27_guidelines_nbsap_e_0.pdf)). Also by economic incentive, education, and different strategies. They also have to make research on that.


The National Reporting is essential. By them, Parties report on their implementation the priorities of the instruments ([See all national reports](https://www.cms.int/en/documents/national-reports)).

  
The CMS Secretariat analyses the responses and prepares a report with an overall assessment on how the Convention is implemented.

The [species](https://www.cms.int/en/species) are listed. They include [mammals](https://www.cms.int/en/species?field_species_class_tid=443), [birds](https://www.cms.int/en/species?field_species_class_tid=421), [reptiles](https://www.cms.int/en/species?field_species_class_tid=454), [boned fishes](https://www.cms.int/en/species?field_species_class_tid=467), [cartilaginous fishes](https://www.cms.int/en/species?field_species_class_tid=1857), and even an [insect](https://www.cms.int/en/species?field_species_class_tid=1311)! ([meet several species](https://www.cms.int/sites/default/files/publication/Species.pdf)). They are in two [Appendices: I and II](https://www.cms.int/en/species/appendix-i-ii-cms) (A specie can also be also in both of them).

  
Appendix I includes all endangered of extinction. **Taking is** **in general** **not allowed.** **Except for scientific, conservational, and traditional subsistence, if it doesn’t affect the conservation.** **Parties have to informe the Secretariat of that. Examples include the indian elephant, the gorilla, the chimpanzee, and the white shark.**

Parties that are Range States have to conserve and restore their habitats. Also to prevent adverse effects of activities, obstacles to their migration, or other factors. **The Conference of the Parties can recommend further protection measures.** **The Secretariat** **can** **offer** **technical support to make national legislation** **to protect them.**

Appendix II includes the species with an unfavourable conservation status and also those which could benefit from improval conservation status. Parties have to make international Agreements for its conservation and management. They should cover all the range of the species. In particular for those with an unfavourable conservation status. A non-Party of the Convention but on the range can participate.

Each agreement shall want to restore to a favourable conservation status. Of one or more species. Also to deal with their conservation and management aspects. It should cover all the range. Any Range State can participate, even if is not a Party of the Convention. It is legally binding. Parties on it have to inform the Secretariat about them. There are 7 of them, which protect albatrosses and petrels, cetaceans, waterbirds, bats, gorillas and seals:

- [Agreement on the Conservation of Albatrosses and Petrels (ACAP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_the_Conservation_of_Albatrosses_and_Petrels)
- [Agreement on the Conservation of Cetaceans of the Black Sea, Mediterranean Sea and Contiguous Atlantic Area (ACCOBAMS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACCOBAMS)
- [Agreement on the Conservation of African-Eurasian Migratory Waterbirds (AEWA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_the_Conservation_of_African-Eurasian_Migratory_Waterbirds)
- [Agreement on the Conservation of Small Cetaceans of the Baltic, North East Atlantic, Irish and North Seas (ASCOBANS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCOBANS)
- [Agreement on the Conservation of Populations of European Bats (EUROBATS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_the_Conservation_of_Populations_of_European_Bats)
- [Agreement on the Conservation of Gorillas and their Habitats (Gorilla Agreement)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_the_Conservation_of_Gorillas_and_Their_Habitats)
- [Agreement on the Conservation of Seals in the Wadden Sea (Wadden Sea Seals)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadden_Sea_Agreement)

Any Party can propose to the Conference of the Parties to change a specie, according to scientific data. If it affirmative if in the next Conference of the Parties, two thirds of them presenting and voting supports the decision. The change takes effect 90 days after.

Besides, people started many projects in different regions. To conservate a specie or a group of them, or to adress threats, Some of them came from [Agreements](https://www.cms.int/en/cms-instruments/agreements) or MOUs. The term “[CMS Family](https://www.migratoryspecies.org/)” referes to [the Convention](https://www.cms.int/en/convention-text), and to all Agreements and MOUs. There are also alternatives to them:

- Special Species Initiatives
    
- Concerted actions
    
- Action Plans
    

  

[Special Species Initiatives](https://www.cms.int/en/cms-intruments/special-initiatives) are a more flexible way to involve government and non-governmental stakeholders. There are four of them:

- [African Carnivores Initiative](http://www.cms.int/en/legalinstrument/african-carnivores-initiative)
    
- [Central Asian Flyway](https://www.cms.int/en/legalinstrument/central-asian-flyway)
    
- [Central Asian Mammals Initiative](http://www.cms.int/cami)
    
- [Sahelo-Saharan Megafauna](https://www.cms.int/en/legalinstrument/sahelo-saharan-megafauna)
    

**[Concerted Actions](https://www.cms.int/en/documents/concerted-actions)** try to cooperate to improve the conservation status of a listed species or group. An Action Plan sets specific measures to secure the species.

  

- An **Action Plan** is a set of very specific measures to implement an instrument or secure the conservation status of a species or group of species.
    

[See the Manual for the national focal Points for CMS and its instruments](https://www.cms.int/sites/default/files/basic_page_documents/Internet_english_09012014.pdf). There is also an [e-community](https://www.migratoryspecies.org/).

The [Tasks Forces](https://www.cms.int/en/taskforces) are teams to adress specific serious threates to them. It can have experts and collaborate with other sectors, beyond the Convention. There are 5:

  

- [Intergovernmental Task Force on Illegal Killing, Taking and Trade of Migratory Birds in the Mediterranean](https://www.cms.int/en/taskforce/mikt) (MIKT)
    
- [Energy Task Force](https://www.cms.int/en/taskforce/energy-task-force)
    
- [Scientific Task Force on Avian Influenza and Wild Birds](https://www.cms.int/en/workinggroup/scientific-task-force-avian-influenza-and-wild-birds)
    
- [Saker Falcon Task Force](https://www.cms.int/raptors/en/workinggroup/saker-falcon-task-force)
    
- [Intergovernmental Task Force to Address Illegal Hunting, Taking and Trade of Migratory Birds in the East Asian-Australasian Flyway](https://www.cms.int/en/taskforce/ittea) (ITTEA)
