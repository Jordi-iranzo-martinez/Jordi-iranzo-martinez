
The [United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change](https://unfccc.int) (UNFCCC) is the UN process for negotiating an agreement to limit dangerous climate change. It is an international treaty among countries to combat "dangerous human interference with the climate system". The main way to do this is limiting the increase in greenhouse gases in the atmosphere.

The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) is the supreme legally binding international treaty created to address human interference with the global climate system. Signed at the historic 1992 Rio Earth Summit alongside its sister treaties, the CBD and the UNCCD, it forms the foundational baseline for all global climate governance and law.

The primary objective of the UNFCCC is to stabilize greenhouse gas (GHG) concentrations in the atmosphere at a level that prevents dangerous anthropogenic interference with the climate. It established the revolutionary legal principle of "common but differentiated responsibilities," meaning that while all nations must act, developed countries carry a heavier historical burden to lead and finance the transition.

Because the original 1992 framework did not contain strict emission limits, the UNFCCC serves as an umbrella under which more targeted, legally binding treaties have been negotiated over the decades:

- **The Kyoto Protocol (1997):** The first major addition to the convention. It committed industrialized nations to mandatory, legally binding emission reduction targets. Its operational periods ran from 2008 through 2020. 

- **The Paris Agreement (2015):** A massive geopolitical turning point. It replaced the Kyoto framework and united nearly every country on Earth under a single banner: **to limit global warming to well below 2°C, and preferably to 1.5°C**, compared to pre-industrial levels. It forces all nations to submit public, progressive climate strategies called **Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)** every five years.


The supreme decision-making body of the treaty is the Conference of the Parties (COP), which meets annually to negotiate global climate policy. Climate diplomacy has accelerated rapidly through the following milestones:

- **COP29 (Baku, Azerbaijan - 2024):** Widely dubbed the "Finance COP," it concluded with developed nations pledging to triple international climate finance, securing a baseline of **$300 billion annually by 2035** to support developing nations.

- **COP30 (Belém, Brazil - 2025):** Hosted right at the gateway of the Amazon rainforest, this landmark summit required all world governments to submit their highly anticipated **NDCs 3.0** with drastically increased emission cutting targets. It concluded with Brazil launching two strict global roadmaps to guide a just transition away from fossil fuels and to completely reverse deforestation.

- **COP31 (Antalya, Türkiye - November 9–20, 2026):** This upcoming summit features a highly unique partnership. **Türkiye** will act as the physical host country and handle the administrative presidency, while **Australia** will exert exclusive authority over the negotiation tables to steer post-2020 finance goals and global progress reviews.
