
**iNaturalist** is one of the world's most popular **citizen science initiatives and social networks for nature enthusiasts.

It was launched in 2008

It is operated as a joint initiative by the [California Academy of Sciences](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESUwHrOzAVlULOoEHYUA6gIjSwPobhUxeuVjSLlMCconwMziqLy7MCeviuycwL0RFBZsrxDucGNQjQv__SROI_TT_-__aUoHGsOWmPc8aSP_v4kfNr) and the [National Geographic Society](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESWwHrOzAV2GzDsPFlAvdXL_ZLTDKWyCX7OSFpyvPIyq_xrliFnP09aevAourZmyHNsVzdufADm0596njPljrND8QPsuoDUMCL32IPkan4Rg_mvCGKCA-Z9-J1URQ).

The platform allows anyone—from professional biologists to amateur hikers—to map, share, and log biodiversity observations across the globe.

The app acts like an "Instagram for nature," transforming a simple photograph into valuable scientific data through a collaborative process:

- **Capture an Observation:** You take a picture or record an audio file of a wild organism (a plant, animal, fungus, or insect) using your smartphone or camera.

- **AI-Powered Identification:** Before uploading, iNaturalist's advanced image-recognition software analyzes the photo and immediately suggests the most likely species or genus based on visual traits and your location.

- **Community Crowdsourcing:** Once posted, the observation is visible to a global community of naturalists and taxonomists. Other users review your photo, vote on its classification, or refine the identification.

- **"Research Grade" Status:** When at least two-thirds of the community agrees on a specific species identification, the log is upgraded to **"Research Grade."**

Once an entry reaches "Research Grade," it doesn't just sit in the app. The verified data is automatically shared with the [Global Biodiversity Information Facility](https://www.gbif.org/) (GBIF).

Scientists and conservationists worldwide use this open-source database to track animal migration patterns, monitor the spread of invasive species, assess how climate change is shifting plant blooming cycles, and even rediscover species thought to be extinct.

- **Projects & BioBlitzes:** Organizations, schools, and parks can create targeted "Projects" on [iNaturalist](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESVAHrOzAVeBf-918OdKZhsafZaeaTMmxKlxW2Gldb46UncgjngdBz7ji4-XVkMr18XGYRtccFb9fQMQIMK1tUSByYhYV42gE_EmimYQmE_es-uFh64Q) to catalog all living things in a specific area (like a backyard, a national park, or an entire city) over a set timeframe, a competitive event often called a **BioBlitz**.

- Seek: To make nature exploration safer and more accessible for children and families, they launched **Seek**. Unlike the main app, Seek doesn't share geolocation data or upload photos to a public server, protecting user privacy. Instead, it uses the AI database offline to let users "scan" living organisms in real-time to earn badges and learn fun facts.
