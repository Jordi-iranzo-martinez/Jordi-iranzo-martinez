Loss of biodiversity referes to the decline or disappearance of the variety of living organisms on Earth, encompassing genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity. This phenomenon is primarily driven by human activities and is currently occurring at a rate 100 to 1000 times higher than the natural background extinction rate. We have lost 60% biodiversity since 1970. 

**Main causes of biodiversity loss include:**

- **Habitat destruction and change in land use:** Conversion of forests, wetlands, and grasslands into urban areas or agricultural land is the leading cause, fragmenting ecosystems and making them less resilient
- **Overexploitation:** Unsustainable hunting, fishing, and harvesting exceed the natural regeneration rates of species
- **Pollution:** Air, water, soil, noise, and light pollution negatively impact species and ecosystems
- **Invasive alien species:** Non-native species disrupt local ecosystems by outcompeting, preying on, or introducing diseases to native species
- **Climate change:** Alters temperature and precipitation patterns, affecting species’ survival and enabling the spread of invasive species

## The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework

The [Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework](https://www.cbd.int/gbf) (GBF) (2022) represents a landmark international agreement for biodiversity conservation. 

- **4 Global Goals for 2050**: Long-term aspirational outcomes
- **23 Global Targets for 2030**: Specific, measurable objectives to be achieved within this decade
- **Implementation mechanisms**: Monitoring, reporting, and review processes


The focus areas comprise [ecosystem protection](https://www.cbd.int/protected-areas), species conservation, [sustainable use](https://www.cbd.int/sustainableuse), [restoration](https://www.cbd.int/restoration), equitable benefit-sharing, financial mechanisms, and [capacity building](https://www.cbd.int/capacity-building).

- **Prior Informed Consent** (PIC): explicit permission given to a user to access genetic resources previor to that, based on trusthful information

- **Mutually Agreed Terms** (MAT): agreement between the provider and user of genetic resources and/or associated traditional knowledge, in which specifies the terms and conditions of access to genetic resources and/or associated traditional knowledge, and how the benefits that arise from the utilization are shared between parties.

PIC should have basic legal certainty and clarity, access facilitated at minimum cost, restrictions on access transparent, legal and not run counter to the objectives of the CBD, consent of relevant competent national authorities, and consent of IPLCs. 

The MAT is generally a legally binding contract, which includes types of benefits, timing of benefit-sharing, mechanisms for benefit-sharing, and distribution of benefits. Also type and quantity of genetic resources, the geological/ecological area of activity, any limitation on possible use of material, clause on whether terms of agreement can be renegotiated, and whether genetic resources can be transferred to third parties.
