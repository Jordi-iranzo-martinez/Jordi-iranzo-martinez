Marcus Aurelius Antoninus Augustus (121-180 AD) was a Roman Emperor since 161 to his death (between 161 to 169 co-emperor with Lucius Vero)

He was the last of the "five fine emperors", with Nerva, Trajanus, Adrianus, and Antoninus Pio.

A period of the most stability and prosperity (96-180 d.C.): the _Pax Romana_.

He faced invasions from Germanic tribes by the North

He participated in the Marcomanes Wars against germanic invaders from the north in the Danubius frontier, and a war with the Partian Empire on the East. Also, the Antoninus Pest (probably viruela o sarampión), by the troops from the east front.




A pesar de estas crisis casi permanentes, fue considerado un gobernante justo, disciplinado y dedicado al deber público, más que a los placeres del poder.


He was a practicioner of stoic phylosophy. During his militar campaigns he wrote personal reflections (_Ta eis heauton_: "To myself" in Greek), which are known as "Meditations".




El libro no es un tratado filosófico formal, sino más bien un diario íntimo de autodisciplina: notas que se escribía a sí mismo para recordarse cómo mantener la virtud, la ecuanimidad y el sentido del deber en medio de las dificultades del poder, la guerra y la mortalidad. Temas centrales:

- La aceptación de lo que no se puede controlar
- La brevedad de la vida y la importancia de vivir con virtud en el presente
- El deber hacia los demás y hacia el bien común, por encima del ego personal
- La muerte como algo natural que no debe temerse

Here are the main types of Roman gladiators, based on their weapons, armor, and fighting style:

**Heavily armed types**


- **Murmillo** — Named after the fish-shaped crest on his helmet ("murmillo" relates to a type of fish). Wore a large rectangular shield (_scutum_), a short sword (_gladius_), a helmet with a wide brim, and an arm guard (_manica_), but had bare legs except for a single short greave. Often matched against Thraex or Hoplomachus.

- **Secutor** ("chaser/pursuer") — Essentially a streamlined version of the murmillo, developed specifically to fight the Retiarius. Had a smooth, rounded helmet with small eyeholes (to avoid getting caught in the net) and a large shield.

- **Thraex** ("Thracian") — Carried a small square or rectangular shield (_parmula_), a curved short sword (_sica_), and wore high greaves on both legs, plus a helmet with a griffin crest. Often paired against the Murmillo or Hoplomachus.

- **Hoplomachus** — Modeled loosely on Greek hoplites. Carried a small round shield, a spear, and a dagger as backup, along with a helmet and heavy leg padding.



**Lightly armed / specialty types**

- **Retiarius** ("net fighter") — The most distinctive type: no helmet, minimal armor, fought with a weighted net (_rete_), a trident, and a dagger. Relied on speed and evasion rather than heavy protection. Classically paired against the Secutor.

- **Dimachaerus** ("bearing two knives") — Fought with a short sword or dagger in each hand, no shield.

- **Sagittarius** — A mounted archer type, using a bow.

- **Essedarius** — Fought from a war chariot (_essedum_), a style borrowed from Celtic Britain.

**Mounted/other types**

- **Equites** ("horsemen") — Fought on horseback with a spear and sword, usually against other Equites, often as an opening bout before the main foot-combat events.

- **Provocator** ("challenger") — Wore a breastplate (unusual among gladiators, most of whom fought bare-chested), a helmet with a visor, one greave, and carried a rectangular shield and short sword. Typically fought other Provocatores.

- **Bestiarius** — Technically not a "gladiator" in the strict combat sense, but a fighter (often condemned criminals) who fought wild animals (_venationes_) rather than other gladiators.

**A few less common/specialized types**

- **Andabata** — Fought blindfolded or with a helmet with no eye holes, relying purely on sound and instinct (largely a spectacle/comedic act).

- **Laquearius** — Similar to the Retiarius but used a lasso (_laqueus_) instead of a net.

- **Crupellarius** — Heavily armored, almost entirely encased in metal, from Gaul.

Pairings mattered a lot in the arena — organizers deliberately matched contrasting styles (e.g., heavily armored vs. lightly armored) to create dramatic, unpredictable fights rather than symmetrical ones.
