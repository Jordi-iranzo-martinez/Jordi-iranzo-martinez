Greta Thumberg (2003-today) is a world-renowned Swedish climate activist who gained global recognition for demanding immediate action from world leaders to combat the climate crisis.

Born on January 3, 2003, in Stockholm, she triggered an international youth movement when she was just 15 years old through her solitary school strikes.

In August 2018, instead of attending school, Thunberg sat outside the Swedish Parliament every day during school hours holding a now-iconic handmade wooden sign that read: _"Skolstrejk för klimatet"_ (School Strike for Climate).

Her demand was simple: the Swedish government needed to reduce carbon emissions in accordance with the Paris Agreement.

Her solitary protest quickly went viral on social media. By late 2018, it evolved into a global student strike movement known as [Fridays for Future](https://fridaysforfuture.org/) (FFF), inspiring millions of students around the world to skip Friday classes to protest outside their local parliaments and city halls.

Thunberg is famous for her blunt, science-first, and uncompromising speeches. She frequently challenges politicians, corporate leaders, and international assemblies (such as the UN and the World Economic Forum) for their lack of concrete action.

- **"Our house is on fire":** One of her most famous metaphors, used to describe the urgency of ecological collapse.

- **"How dare you!":** Her emotional and powerful phrase delivered during the 2019 UN Climate Action Summit in New York, where she accused world leaders of stealing her childhood and dreams with empty words.

- **The Asperger's "Superpower":** Thunberg was diagnosed with Asperger syndrome, OCD, and selective mutism. She has publicly referred to her Asperger's diagnosis not as an illness, but as her "superpower," explaining that it helps her see the climate crisis in black-and-white, logical terms without succumbing to social pressures or political politeness.


As she has matured into her twenties, Thunberg's activism has expanded significantly beyond climate policy into **climate justice and geopolitical human rights**:

- **Intersectional Activism:** She frequently emphasizes that the climate crisis cannot be solved without addressing capitalism, indigenous rights, and corporate exploitation.

- **Civil Disobedience:** In recent years, she has shifted from organizing student marches to participating in high-profile direct actions, blockade protests, and civil disobedience campaigns across Europe. These actions have occasionally resulted in brief detentions by police in countries such as the UK, Germany, the Netherlands, and Sweden.

- **Time's Person of the Year (2019):** She became the youngest individual ever to receive this title.

- **Nobel Peace Prize Nominations:** She has been nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize multiple times by international lawmakers.

- **Cultural Legacy:** Her work has been documented in books, theatrical releases (like the documentary _I Am Greta_), and she has co-authored _The Climate Book_, a comprehensive anthology features essays from over 100 scientists and experts.
