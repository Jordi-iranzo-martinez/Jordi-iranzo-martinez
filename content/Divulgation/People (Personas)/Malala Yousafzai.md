Malala Yousafzai (1997-today) is a Pakistani education activist and the youngest Nobel Prize laureate in history. 

Born in 1997 in the Swat Valley of northwest Pakistan, Malala was raised by her father, Ziauddin Yousafzai, a teacher and school owner who fiercely encouraged her to study and speak her mind.


When the Taliban took control of the region and banned girls from attending school, 11-year-old Malala began writing an anonymous blog for BBC Urdu. Under the pen name Gul Makai, she detailed her fears of losing her education and the harsh reality of living under extremist rule. Eventually, her real identity was revealed as she began appearing on television to advocate for girls' education.

She received the [Nobel Peace Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/2014/yousafzai/facts/) in 2014, at the age of 17, making she the youngest person in history to receive it.

Because of her growing platform, she became a target. On October 9, 2012, when she was 15 years old, a masked Taliban gunman boarded her school bus, asked "Who is Malala?", and shot her at point-blank range on the left side of her head

The assassination attempt sparked immediate international condemnation. Malala miraculously survived and was airlifted to Birmingham, UK, where she underwent emergency surgeries and intensive rehabilitation. Upon recovering, she vowed that fear would not silence her and that she would dedicate her life to fighting for every girl's right to learn.

- **The Malala Fund:** In 2013, she co-founded the [Malala Fund](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESSwHrOzAV4AA5Af3waqQP4XXsffW44OSOFIkWt4s9ZCl18EnmB1Ckwac2FI7DOUhn_oCXZGY9YnfsPZ69LNyp4llw-dBhemwFLEmDXA) alongside her father, an international organization championing every girl's right to 12 years of free, safe, and quality education.

- **UN Speech:** On her 16th birthday in July 2013, she delivered a powerful speech at the [United Nations](https://www.google.com/goto?url=CAESSwHrOzAVwN26S7essT6Zp3uWzkPbENwOmeAa_Xj3JGywmCrBb-8lyerwHgFk4934eEQDPpwdY3504NllvCkIi3SCjdeLZWM0p5vvZA), coining her most famous phrase: _**"One child, one teacher, one book, and one pen can change the world."**_

- **Nobel Peace Prize:** In December 2014, she was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize alongside Indian child rights activist Kailash Satyarthi for their struggle against the suppression of children and young people.


Over the years, Malala has matured into an influential global leader, expanding her focus beyond education to broader geopolitical human rights:

- **Oxford Graduate:** She pursued a degree in Philosophy, Politics, and Economics (PPE) at Lady Margaret Hall, University of Oxford, graduating with honors.

- **Fighting "Gender Apartheid":** In her recent addresses to international assemblies, Malala has been campaigning fiercely against the Taliban regime in Afghanistan. She has called on world leaders to officially recognize the systematic erasure of Afghan women and girls as a crime against humanity.

- **Author & Producer:** Following her bestselling autobiography _**I Am Malala**_, she has entered the media world to amplify marginalized voices. She served as an executive producer for the documentary film _Bread and Roses_, which chronicles the daily resistance of women living in Kabul under Taliban rule. She has also recently published an intimate new memoir titled _Finding My Way_.
