The [United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change](https://unfccc.int) (UNFCCC) is the UN process for negotiating an agreement to limit dangerous climate change. It is an international treaty among countries to combat "dangerous human interference with the climate system". The main way to do this is limiting the increase in greenhouse gases in the atmosphere.

Adopted at the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro and entering into force in 1994, it serves as the foundational parent treaty for both the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and the Paris Agreement (2015). It currently has 198 parties, achieving near-universal global membership.

The framework operates as the central political platform for global climate diplomacy, utilizing several foundational structures:

- **The ultimate objective:** Stabilizing greenhouse gas concentrations in the atmosphere at a level that prevents catastrophic anthropogenic climate change, allowing ecosystems to adapt naturally and ensuring food production is not threatened.

- **Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR):** A core ethical and legal principle acknowledging that while all nations must fight climate change, developed countries bear a greater historical responsibility for emissions and possess more financial resources to lead the transition.

- **The COP Summits:** The convention's supreme decision-making body meets annually at the **Conference of the Parties (COP)**. These summits serve as the global arena where prime ministers, presidents, and diplomats negotiate legally binding emission targets, carbon market rules, and climate finance packages.

Rather than remaining a static 1990s declaration, the UNFCCC functions as the active administrative umbrella managing the enforcement of international climate goals:

- **The "NDC 3.0" Submission Deadline:** Under the strict rules managed by the UNFCCC Secretariat, all 198 member nations face a critical legislative deadline to formally submit their updated Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC 3.0). These national roadmaps must detail aggressive, legally binding emission reduction targets aligned with keeping global warming within the 1.5°C threshold.

- **The New Collective Quantified Goal (NCQG):** A primary operational focus for the UNFCCC bodies is the implementation of the NCQG on climate finance. This framework officially replaces the outdated $100 billion annual pledge, establishing a vastly scaled-up financial architecture to channel hundreds of billions of dollars from developed economies to the Global South for clean energy infrastructure and climate adaptation.

- **The Global Stocktake Enforcement:** Following the first comprehensive assessment of global climate progress, the UNFCCC technical bodies are actively coordinating national task forces to turn the data into concrete policy. The current directives heavily prioritize accelerating the **transition away from fossil fuels** in energy systems and rapidly scaling up global renewable energy capacity.
