title: Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction
---
The BBNJ Agreement (formally known as the treaty on Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction), also globally recognized as the High Seas Treaty, is a historic, legally binding international accord designed to protect marine life in international waters.

The treaty officially entered into force on January 17, 2026, marking the dawn of a new era in global ocean governance. Operating under the framework of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), it regulates the areas outside national Economic Exclusive Zones (EEZs)—which constitute nearly two-thirds of the global ocean and had previously been a lawless wild west.

To guarantee the structural sustainability of Earth's largest ecosystem, the agreement establishes strict rules across four critical domains:

- **Marine Protected Areas (MPAs):** For the first time in history, the treaty provides a legal mechanism to establish fully protected marine sanctuaries in international waters. This is the single most vital tool needed to achieve the global "30x30" target, which aims to protect 30% of the world's land and oceans by 2030.

- **Marine Genetic Resources (MGRs):** It regulates the access to and use of biological material and DNA samples extracted from deep-sea marine life. Crucially, it sets up a framework for the fair and equitable sharing of benefits (both financial and scientific profits) derived from these resources, protecting the interests of the Global South.

- **Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs):** The treaty legally obligates member states to conduct rigorous, transparent scientific impact studies before greenlighting any commercial human activities in the High Seas (such as deep-sea mining, large-scale geoengineering, or new shipping lanes).

- **Capacity Building & Marine Technology Transfer:** Developed, wealthy nations are required to share oceanographic technologies, scientific data, and funding with developing countries to ensure everyone can equitably participate in and benefit from ocean conservation.
