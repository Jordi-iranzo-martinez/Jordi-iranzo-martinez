
### Improving the marine biodiversity protection in Europe under the Natura 2000 and Emerald Networks

Europe has led the way in global biodiversity protection. The Bern Convention (1979), established under the Council of Europe, pioneered international habitat conservation, while the European Union's Natura 2000 Network has become the world's largest coordinated network of protected areas. However, this leadership has been predominantly terrestrial-focused, leaving significant gaps in marine biodiversity protection.

The **Emerald Network** is a network of Areas of Special Conservation Interest (ASCIs) established under the **Bern Convention** (1979) on the Conservation of European Wildlife and Natural Habitats. It was officially launched in **1998** by the **Council of Europe**.

- It is designed for **non-EU countries**, although some **EU countries** also contribute to it as part of their international obligations.
    
- It aims to create a **coherent ecological network** to protect species and habitats listed in the **annexes of the Bern Convention**.

The Emerald Network has historically focused on **terrestrial** and **freshwater** ecosystems. Marine coverage is **very limited** and only began to be considered more seriously in recent years. While some marine species are included in the Bern Convention, the protection of **entire marine ecosystems** is not a central part of the network. A few countries have proposed **marine sites**, but overall, **marine conservation is not the Emerald Network's strength**.

In comparison, the **EU Natura 2000** network has made much more progress in designating **marine protected areas** (especially under the **Habitats Directive**).

The Habitats Directive (92/43/EEC) was adopted in 1992 when marine ecosystem knowledge was far less developed than today. The focus was primarily on terrestrial and coastal habitats that were better understood and more politically visible.

There are many more marine habitats than those listed in the Directive, both deep-sea and pelagic:

- **Hydrothermal vents**: Chemical-rich fluids emanate from the seafloor, often providing the energy to sustain lush communities of life in some very harsh environments
- **Cold seeps**: Locations where hydrocarbon-rich fluid seeps up from below the seafloor, often as methane or hydrogen sulfide
- **Abyssal plains** and deep-sea trenches
- **Seamounts** and underwater mountains
- **Deep-sea coral gardens** and sponge aggregations

- Open ocean water column habitats
- Upwelling zones
- Oceanic fronts and gyres
- Deep scattering layers
