
# Introduction

Birds are warm-blooded vertebrates characterized by feathers, beaks, wings, and the ability to lay eggs. Most can fly, though some are flightless. They are descendants of theropod dinosaurs and are found in virtually every habitat on Earth.




# Palaeognathae ("Old Jaw")

This most ancient clade includes flightless and weakly-flying birds with primitive jaw structures. Prior to 2024, the first four orders were classified as families within the single order Struthioniformes.

- 


**Orders include:**

- Order Struthioniformes (Ostriches)
- Order Rheiformes (Rheas)
- Order Casuariiformes (Cassowaries and Emus)
- Order Apterygiformes (Kiwis)
- Order Tinamiformes (Tinamous)

**The ostrich** (_Struthio camelus_) is the largest and heaviest bird on Earth, a flightless runner native to African savannas. They can reach speeds up to 70 km/h (43 mph) and have powerful legs that can deliver fatal kicks to predators.

**Rheas** are large, flightless South American birds similar to ostriches but smaller. They are excellent runners and swimmers, living in grasslands and open plains.

**The cassowary** (_Casuarius casuarius_) seems like a living dinosaur and is extremely aggressive. They can slash with their powerful feet using a dagger-like claw, making them one of the world's most dangerous birds.

**Emus** (_Dromaius novaehollandiae_) are Australia's largest birds, excellent long-distance runners that can travel great distances in search of food and water.

**The kiwi** (_Apteryx australis_) is a small, flightless bird native to New Zealand with hair-like feathers, a long beak with nostrils at the tip, and strong legs for digging. They are nocturnal and lay eggs proportionally larger than any other bird.

**Tinamous** are small, ground-dwelling birds from Central and South America that can fly but prefer to run. They have some of the most beautiful eggs in the bird world, with glossy, jewel-like colors.


# Infraclass Neognathae ("New Jaw")

The remaining ~11,000 extant bird species belong to this infraclass. These sister taxa diverged over 100 million years ago and include all modern birds with more advanced jaw structures.

- Order Anseriformes (Waterfowl)
- Order Galliformes (Chickens and Game Birds)


---
**Ducks** (family Anatidae) are waterfowl birds usually found in freshwater and marine environments. They have webbed feet for swimming and waterproof feathers.

**Swans** are large ducks known for their length, grace, and elegant curved necks. They inspired Tchaikovsky's famous ballet "Swan Lake" (_El lago de los cisnes_).

---
**The common pheasant** (_Phasianus colchicus_) is a colorful game bird native to Asia but widely introduced worldwide. Males are known for their bright plumage and long tail feathers.

**The chicken** (_Gallus gallus domesticus_) is a domesticated bird raised worldwide for meat and eggs, descended from wild jungle fowl.


### **Neoaves** (All Other Modern Birds)

- Order Sphenisciformes (Penguins)
- Order Procellariiformes (Tube-nosed Seabirds: albatross, petrels and shearwaters)
- Order Pelecaniformes (Pelicans, herons, cormorants, and frigatebirds)
- Order Charadriiformes (Shorebirds, Gulls, and Auks)
- Order Gruiformes (Cranes and Rails)
- Order Columbiformes (Doves and Pigeons)
- Order Psittaciformes (Parrots and cocktatoos)
- Order Strigiformes (Owls)
- Order Accipitriformes (Hawks, Eagles, and Vultures)
- Order Falconiformes (Falcons and Caracaras)
- Order Piciformes (Woodpeckers and Allies)
- Order Trogoniformes (Trogons)
- Order Apodiformes (Swifts and Hummingbirds)
- Order Passeriformes (Songbirds/Perching Birds)


_Passeriformes_ (from Latin "passer": sparrow) is the largest order of birds, containing over half of all bird species. They are characterized by their perching feet and, in many species, complex songs.


**Crows** (family Corvidae) are among the most intelligent birds, capable of using tools and solving complex problems. They are related to ravens, like in Edgar Allan Poe's famous poem "The Raven."

**Birds-of-paradise** (_Paradisaeidae_) are known for their spectacular plumage and elaborate courtship displays, mostly found in New Guinea and Australia.

**Swallows** (family Hirundinidae) are small, agile insectivores that catch insects on the wing with incredible aerial maneuverability.

**The European robin** (_Erithacus rubecula_) is a small insectivorous bird known for its bright orange-red breast and melodic song. It is common across Europe and often associated with gardens and woodlands.

**The Japanese robin** (_Larvivora akahige_) is a small migratory bird known for its beautiful song and orange-red throat.

**Hawaiian honeycreepers** (_Drepanididae_) are endemic Hawaiian passerines specialized in sipping nectar with curved bills. Many species are unfortunately extinct or critically endangered.

**Sparrows** are small, social seed-eating birds found worldwide, known for their adaptability to human environments.
