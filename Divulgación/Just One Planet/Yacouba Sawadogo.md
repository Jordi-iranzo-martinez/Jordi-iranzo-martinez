**The farmer who pushed back a desert**


Yacouba Sawadogo (1946-2023) was a farmer from Burkina Faso that achieved the feat to stop the desertation of the Sahel region, one of the driest places on the Earth. 

"The Man Who Stopped the Desert."

In the 1980s, during severe droughts, he started experimentating with zai (tassa in Hausa), an ancient farming technique that consists in digging small pits to collect rainwater. He also started adding organic matter to create fertile micro-environments for seeds and saplings.

He even improved it, increasing the pit sizes to capture more water, added strategic placement of stones to control water flow, combine it with strategic tree planting, and use termites to improve soil structure. 

Over decades he transformed 40 hectares of barren land into a thriving forest. Wildlife has returned to the area and underground water levels have risen.

He received in 2018 [_The Right Livelihood Award_](https://rightlivelihood.org/the-change-makers/find-a-laureate/yacouba-sawadogo/) "for turning barren land into forest and demonstrating how farmers can regenerate their soil with innovative use of indigenous and local knowledge". 

*The Right Livelihood Award is an international recognition for people that offer exemplary practical solutions to the most urgents problems in the world. It is considered an alternative to the Nobel Prize, because it covers fields that the Nobel don't, like human rights, social justice, environmental protection, peace, and sustainable development.*

In his late 70s, Sawadogo continued working his land and teaching others.
